Eight Straight Wins Meet A Depleted Lineup

Atlanta is 70-45 and has not lost a game since the afternoon of July 29. The streak started with a 1-0 win at the Mets, ran through a four-game sweep of Washington, and finished with three straight against Miami by scores of 4-2, 4-1 and 11-3. Over the last ten games the Braves are 8-2. Only Milwaukee, at 72-43, has a better record in the National League.

New York is 64-51, sitting 4.5 games behind Tampa Bay in the American League East, and the Yankees arrive off a home series they lost two games to one against St. Louis. There is a strange split running through their season. The Yankees are 37-26 away from the Bronx and only 27-25 inside it, which is a genuinely unusual shape for a contender and one worth holding onto for the next three nights.

These two clubs have not met in a regular-season game in 2026. This is the first of three at Yankee Stadium, running Friday through Sunday, and it lands at a moment when both rosters look nothing like the versions drawn up in March. Atlanta is running a rotation without Spencer Strider or Spencer Schwellenbach. New York is running a lineup without Aaron Judge, Cody Bellinger or Giancarlo Stanton.

Injury Report & Lineup Notes

Atlanta Braves
Rotation
Spencer Strider is on the 60-day injured list with an elbow injury and is not expected to pitch again this season. Spencer Schwellenbach is also on the 60-day list with an elbow injury and is still working through bullpen sessions before facing live hitters. Reynaldo Lopez is on the 15-day list with a knee injury after an MRI showed no structural damage.
Bullpen And Lineup
Joe Jimenez is on the 60-day list following another operation on his left knee, and Joey Wentz is on the 60-day list with a knee injury. Robert Suarez is on the 15-day list with a forearm issue and has not yet increased the intensity of his throwing program. Outfielder Jurickson Profar will miss the entire season after the appeal of his 162-game suspension was denied.
New York Yankees
Lineup
Aaron Judge is on the 60-day injured list with a rib injury and has only recently been cleared to begin light activity. Cody Bellinger is on the 10-day list with a hamstring injury and has been running outdoors while hitting and throwing. Giancarlo Stanton is on the 10-day list with a calf injury and has been taking swings in live batting practice.
Rotation
Carlos Rodon is on the 15-day list with an elbow injury and is scheduled to begin a rehab assignment. Clarke Schmidt remains on the 60-day list with an elbow and forearm problem and has been building back toward playing catch. That leaves Max Fried as the healthiest front-line arm New York has available for this series.

The Pitching Match-Up

Tyler Mahle, Atlanta

Mahle is 3-9 with a 5.13 ERA and a 1.39 WHIP, with 89 strikeouts and 37 walks across 94.2 innings in 18 starts. Opponents are hitting .265 against him and he has surrendered 14 home runs, a number that matters more than usual in a park where the right-field porch turns routine fly balls into souvenirs. The win-loss record oversells how badly it has gone, but the peripheral profile is honest: a 3.52 walk rate per nine and enough hard contact to keep innings alive.

His last month has been genuinely uneven. He gave Atlanta seven innings and one earned run against Colorado on July 11, then five innings and three earned against Kansas City on July 21, then six shutout innings against Milwaukee on July 27, the best team in the National League. On August 1 in San Diego he struck out nine but gave up five earned runs in five and two-thirds. Nine strikeouts and five earned runs in the same box score is the version of Mahle that has defined this season.

Max Fried, New York

Fried is 4-3 with a 3.12 ERA and a 0.99 WHIP in 75 innings over 13 starts, with 68 strikeouts and 23 walks. The number that jumps out has nothing to do with strikeouts. He has allowed exactly one home run all season, in 75 innings, against a Yankees staff that has given up 112 as a group. Opponents are hitting .195 off him. That is a pitcher keeping the ball on the ground and in the yard in a stadium that punishes anyone who cannot.

He also has not been available for most of the summer. Fried did not appear in a major league game between May 13 and July 22. Since coming back he has made three starts: five shutout innings with seven strikeouts against Pittsburgh on July 22, three innings and three earned runs against the White Sox on July 27, and five and a third innings with one earned run and seven strikeouts against the Cubs on August 1. Thirteen and a third innings, four earned runs, and a pitch count that New York has kept short on purpose.

The context is what makes this one different for him. Fried came up with Atlanta and stayed eight seasons, from a four-start cameo in 2017 through a 29-start 2024, winning 73 games in that uniform and posting a 2.48 ERA over 185.1 innings in 2022. He signed with New York before the 2025 season and went 19-5 with a 2.86 ERA in his first year in pinstripes. Tonight is the first time the schedule has put him opposite the club that developed him.

The Yankees own the best staff ERA in baseball at 3.33 and have hit more home runs than any team in the league with 164. They also carry a .231 team batting average, third-lowest in the majors behind only Cincinnati and Seattle. That combination describes exactly how New York has been winning: pitch well enough to keep the score in reach, then wait for someone to run into one. Take Judge, Bellinger and Stanton out of that lineup and the margin for error on those swings gets very thin.

Ben Rice has carried the load in their absence with 31 home runs, 73 RBI and a .260/.355/.550 line in 110 games. Jazz Chisholm Jr. has 17 home runs and 47 RBI, Paul Goldschmidt has 16 home runs and 44 RBI. That is a real middle of the order and it is also three names deep, which is a different thing than a healthy Yankees lineup.

Atlanta's offense is deeper right now. Matt Olson has 32 home runs, 70 RBI and a .896 OPS. Michael Harris II is hitting .293 with 20 home runs and 66 RBI. Ozzie Albies has 19 home runs and 63 RBI, and catcher Drake Baldwin has 20 home runs and 59 RBI while hitting .271. Ronald Acuna Jr. has played 63 games and posted a .358 on-base percentage with 11 home runs and 15 stolen bases. Four Braves regulars are at 19 home runs or better, and the run differential says the rest: plus 121 for Atlanta, plus 81 for New York.

Keys To The Game

Braves Keys
Force Fried's pitch count early. He has not thrown more than five and a third innings in any of his three starts since returning on July 22. Every extra pitch in the second and third innings is an inning the Yankees bullpen has to cover.
Keep the ball off the ground. Fried has allowed one home run in 75 innings. Atlanta has 155 as a team and has scored in bunches during this streak, including eleven runs on Thursday. If the Braves cannot elevate, they have to string singles together against a 0.99 WHIP.
Yankees Keys
Attack Mahle's home run rate. He has given up 14 home runs in 94.2 innings and opponents are hitting .265 against him. A lineup missing three bats still leads the majors in home runs, and this is the pitcher on the schedule best suited to give one up.
Fix the home split. New York is 27-25 at Yankee Stadium and 37-26 on the road. Nothing about that gap is normal for a club with the best staff ERA in baseball, and three games against the hottest team in the National League is a difficult place to start correcting it.

Market Context

New York is a short home favorite at -122 with Atlanta at +114 and the total posted at 8. Strip the vig out and the market is calling this close to a 54-46 game in the Yankees' favor, which is a notable read given that Atlanta has the better record, the deeper healthy lineup and a plus-121 run differential against New York's plus-81.

The number is doing two things at once. It is respecting Fried, whose 3.12 ERA and 0.99 WHIP are the best pitching credentials on the field tonight, and it is respecting the Yankees staff as a whole at 3.33. It is also discounting Atlanta's eight-game run more than a raw form read would, which is what books usually do when a streak was built against Washington and Miami rather than against a contender.

The total at 8 sits just below the average across tonight's fifteen-game board and reflects the same split: a strong home starter with a short leash against a Braves offense that has scored four or more runs in each of its last seven games. Final Thoughts

The cleanest way to read this game is as a test of whether Atlanta's streak is real. Eight straight wins is a genuine number, but the opponents were the Mets in a doubleheader nightcap, a Nationals staff carrying a 4.78 ERA, and a Miami club sitting exactly at .500. The Yankees are the first team with a winning record Atlanta has faced during the entire run.

On the other side, the Yankees are the more compromised roster. Their three most dangerous hitters are all on the injured list, their number two and three starters are unavailable, and the man taking the ball has thrown thirteen and a third innings since the middle of May. That is a lot of weight for one left-hander to carry, however good his numbers look.

Two things are worth watching more than the score. The first is how deep Fried goes, because the Yankees bullpen has been the quiet reason their ERA leads baseball and a fourth-inning hook changes the shape of the night. The second is whether Atlanta's power plays in this park. The Braves hit 155 home runs getting here. Yankee Stadium is the one place on the schedule where that number can decide a game in a single swing.
