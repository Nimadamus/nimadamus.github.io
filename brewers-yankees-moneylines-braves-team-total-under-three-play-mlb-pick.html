A .152 opponent batting average across 145 innings stops being a hot streak somewhere around June. Misiorowski has allowed three runs or fewer in 23 of his 24 starts, he averages 6.01 innings a night, and his last five outings have gone 1, 2, 3, 1 and 0 runs allowed. A 0.76 WHIP means fewer than three baserunners every four innings.

The opponent is a New York club that has been quiet all year. The Mets have scored three runs or fewer in 67 of their 133 games, which is 50.4 percent, and at Citi Field that number is 47.8 percent. They have 163 home runs, so the damage they do tends to arrive in one swing, and against a pitcher allowing 0.81 home runs per nine that swing is the whole realistic path to a Mets win.

Sean Manaea is the other half of the argument. He carries a 4.31 ERA, a 1.31 WHIP and 19 home runs allowed in 117 innings, and he has held opponents to three runs or fewer in only 8 of his 13 starts. Milwaukee scores 4.96 a game and has averaged 5.00 over its last fifteen. This is a good offense against a hittable starter while an elite starter works the other half of the innings.

The price is the problem and there is no way around it. At -191 this ticket needs 65.6 percent, and 2 units to win costs 3.82. That is 39 percent of the day's total risk sitting on one game.

He goes 6.51 innings a night, which means the Los Angeles bullpen is being asked for roughly two and a half innings rather than four, and that bullpen belongs to a 3.69 staff ERA.

Atlanta has been quiet lately in a way that fits the ticket. Over the last fifteen games the Braves have scored 3.20 runs a game and have been held to three or fewer seven times. Chris Sale opposing matters too, in an indirect way. Sale carries a 2.20 ERA and has held opponents to three or fewer in 22 of 23 starts. A game with two pitchers like this rarely turns into the six run inning that blows a team total, because neither manager is chasing a deficit early and neither bullpen gets exposed in the fifth.

What Can Beat This Card

Atlanta at home. This is the one to say loudest. The Braves have averaged 5.00 runs across their last fifteen home dates and have been held to three or fewer in only 18 of 68 home games all season, which is 26.5 percent. The price needs 59.2 percent. That gap is real and none of the numbers above hide it. This ticket is a bet on Yamamoto being the exception to Atlanta's home form, not a bet on Atlanta being quiet.

The price on Milwaukee. Laying -191 means the Brewers ticket costs 3.82 units and returns 2. Misiorowski is the best starter on any card this month and that still does not make 65.6 percent a bargain. One three run inning against a Mets lineup with 163 home runs and the whole day is dug out of a hole.

Houston is not its record. The Astros are 66-67 with a 4.68 staff ERA, but they score 4.57 a game and have hit 177 home runs, more than Milwaukee and more than Atlanta. Yankee Stadium is where a cold offense turns dangerous for three swings.

Wesneski has actually pitched fine. Five starts, a 4.23 ERA, a 1.23 WHIP and a .240 opponent average is a perfectly usable line. The argument against him is sample size and walk rate, not results.

Cole's innings. He averages 5.67 innings a start, the lowest of the three starters being backed today. That leaves nine to twelve outs for a bullpen to record against a lineup with 177 home runs.
