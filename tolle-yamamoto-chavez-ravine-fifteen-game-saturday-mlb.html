MLB - Saturday, August 1

Tolle Against Yamamoto Headlines All Fifteen Games

Every team in baseball plays on the first Saturday of August, and the night game at Chavez Ravine is the marquee: Payton Tolle and a Red Sox club riding three straight wins against Yoshinobu Yamamoto and the 69-41 Dodgers. Behind it, the Yankees and Cubs replay a 2-0 shutout at 62-48 apiece, Houston wakes up in first place after burying Texas 11-2, and Detroit sends Framber Valdez at a Sacramento team it just beat 13-1. Fifteen games, verified lines and starters. Analysis only, no picks.

Marquee Matchup
SNLA

Red Sox at Dodgers

Saturday, 9:10 PM ET | Dodger Stadium, Los Angeles, CA | LAD -168, O/U 7.5

Boston walked into Chavez Ravine on Friday and won 9-4, a third straight victory for a club that is 7-3 over its last ten and a remarkable 33-22 on the road. The Red Sox are 58-51 and playing their best baseball of the season away from Fenway, and rookie left-hander Payton Tolle has been a real part of it: 6-6 with a 3.32 ERA, a 1.11 WHIP and 103 strikeouts in 95 innings, including seven strikeouts over 5.1 innings in Sacramento his last time out. He holds hitters to a .218 average, which is frontline stuff from a first-year arm.

The problem is the assignment. Yoshinobu Yamamoto is 11-6 with a 2.72 ERA and a 0.90 WHIP, holds opponents to a .190 average, and threw a nine-inning complete game against the Yankees on July 19. Los Angeles is 69-41 with the best record in baseball and an offense to match, a league-leading .262 average, 5.15 runs per game and 142 home runs. Friday was only the Dodgers' 21st home loss against 33 wins, and the market's -168 reflects how rarely they drop two straight in their own park with Yamamoto involved.

The total of 7.5 splits the difference between Friday's 13-run track meet and the pitching matchup on paper. Boston's offense is bottom-third by average at .245 with 105 home runs, so its 9-run Friday leaned on volume of baserunners rather than raw power. Against a 0.90 WHIP, that fuel is much harder to find.

Featured Game
Regional

Yankees at Cubs

Saturday, 7:15 PM ET | Wrigley Field, Chicago, IL | CHC -120, O/U 6.5

Both teams are 62-48 after New York's 2-0 shutout in Friday's opener, and the middle game is a left-on-left duel with the lowest total on the board: Max Fried, carrying a 1.02 WHIP and a .202 opponent average in his third start back from a two-month absence, against David Peterson, whose 6.09 ERA hides two earned runs allowed over his last 12.1 innings. New York is doing all of this without Aaron Judge (ribs), Giancarlo Stanton and Cody Bellinger, and still owns a 36-25 road record. The full breakdown, injuries, advanced stats and keys are on the Featured Game page.

Ace Against The Skid
Regional

Phillies at Orioles

Saturday, 7:05 PM ET | Oriole Park at Camden Yards, Baltimore, MD | PHI -154, O/U 8.0

Philadelphia has lost four straight, is 2-8 over its last ten, and dropped Friday's opener here 6-4, yet the market still installs the Phillies as a clear road favorite because Cristopher Sanchez pitches Saturday. The lefty is 13-4 with a 2.73 ERA and one of the most lopsided strikeout-to-walk ratios in the sport, 157 to 28 across 138.2 innings. His last three starts came against Detroit, the Dodgers and the Yankees, and he completed at least 5.1 innings with three or fewer earned runs in all of them.

Baltimore has won two straight and gets Shane Baz, 4-9 with a 3.98 ERA that has been quietly respectable, a 6-inning, 1-run gem against Boston on July 20 among his last three outings. The Orioles are 54-56 and 30-27 at home, but the lineup profile is the worry against Sanchez: a .237 team average and 1,010 strikeouts on the season. Philadelphia's own slump is real, a .236 team average all season, so an 8.0 total in a hitter-friendly park is a bet that at least one of these offenses shows up.

After A 13-1 Night
Regional

Tigers at Athletics

Saturday, 9:40 PM ET | Sutter Health Park, West Sacramento, CA | DET -172, O/U 10.5

Detroit's 13-1 demolition on Friday night was the loudest result of the day, and the follow-up brings Framber Valdez to a mound where he has already dominated this opponent: seven innings, one run, nine strikeouts and no walks against the Athletics on July 9. His season line of 5-7 with a 4.53 ERA is dragged down by a two-out, six-run blowup at Wrigley on July 21, but the Tigers are 52-58 and still very much alive at 6.5 back in the AL Central, and this weekend is exactly the kind of series a fringe contender has to bank.

Sacramento is 45-65, has lost three straight and seven of ten, and owns one of the worst home records in baseball at 21-34. Rookie right-hander Jack Perkins brings real strikeout ability, 80 in 68.1 innings, and held Boston to one hit over 5.2 innings on July 27, but the 6.45 ERA tells the fuller story of a pitcher whose mistakes get punished. The 10.5 total is the second highest on the board, a nod to this park's offensive environment and Friday's fourteen combined runs.

Messick Against Drake
Regional

Diamondbacks at Guardians

Saturday, 7:15 PM ET | Progressive Field, Cleveland, OH | CLE -181, O/U 8.5

Arizona won Friday's opener 4-1, a third straight victory for a 58-52 club that has gone 7-3 over its last ten and pulled into second place in the NL West. The price says Saturday flips, and the reason is Parker Messick: 8-6 with a 2.59 ERA, a 1.04 WHIP and a .208 opponent average across 125.1 innings, coming off 7.2 innings of one-run ball against the AL East-leading Rays. He has not walked a batter in either of his last two starts.

The Diamondbacks answer with left-hander Kohl Drake, who has thrown exactly nine big-league innings, a solid five-inning debut stretch against the Athletics on July 21 and a four-run stumble against Washington on July 26. Cleveland is 56-55 and just 3-7 over its last ten, with a bottom-of-the-league offense at .229 and 3.90 runs per game, so the -181 is built almost entirely on the mound mismatch. Arizona's .241-hitting lineup against Messick's ratios is the whole ballgame.

AL West Lead Flipped
Regional

Rangers at Astros

Saturday, 7:10 PM ET | Daikin Park, Houston, TX | TEX -126, O/U 8.0

Friday night changed the standings. Houston's 11-2 blowout was its fourth straight win and ninth in ten games, and it pushed the 56-55 Astros past the 55-55 Rangers into first place in the AL West by half a game. Texas has lost three straight and now hands the season's biggest start yet to Jacob deGrom, who is 7-6 with a 3.67 ERA and a vintage 132-to-24 strikeout-to-walk ratio across 110.1 innings. The market makes the road team the favorite on his arm alone.

Houston counters with Ronel Blanco, making just his third appearance of the season with an 8.10 ERA over ten innings, twelve strikeouts against six walks. The Astros' surge has been an offensive one, .244 as a team with 154 home runs, and the eye-opening split is that Houston has done its damage lately despite a .500 home record of 27-27. A deGrom start against a short-stint arm in a race that flipped overnight is as much intrigue as any unranked-total game on the board can carry.

First Place Twice
Regional

White Sox at Rays

Saturday, 4:10 PM ET | Tropicana Field, St. Petersburg, FL | TB -172, O/U 7.5

Two division leaders, and the visitor just made a statement. Chicago's 6-1 win on Friday was a third straight victory for the 58-51 AL Central leaders, who have paired a 143-homer power game with enough pitching to hold off Cleveland and Minnesota by three games. Right-hander Jordan Hicks gets the ball with a 3.19 ERA in a swing role, 36 strikeouts in 31 innings, though his 22 walks make him one of the wilder arms on the day's board.

Tampa Bay remains the class of the AL East at 64-45, 7-3 in its last ten even after Friday's dud, and the home-field number is the loudest stat of the day: the Rays are 40-17 at Tropicana Field. Drew Rasmussen is a big reason the market lays -172 here, 9-5 with a 3.07 ERA, a 0.93 WHIP and just 19 walks in 111.1 innings. Against a White Sox lineup that hits .239 and strikes out 974 times, Rasmussen's strike-throwing is a stylistic hammer, and the 7.5 total leans his way.

Best Record On The Road
Regional

Brewers at Angels

Saturday, 9:38 PM ET | Angel Stadium, Anaheim, CA | MIL -123, O/U 8.5

Milwaukee is 68-41, a game behind the Dodgers for the best record in baseball, and Friday's 6-2 win in Anaheim showed the formula again: 5.06 runs per game built on a .255 average and constant pressure rather than the home run, just 100 of those all year, fewest among contenders. Left-hander Robert Gasser starts at 3-4 with a 4.71 ERA, but his control profile fits the club, 14 walks in 57.1 innings.

The Angels are 42-68, losers of four straight and 3-7 in their last ten, and veteran lefty Brent Suter gets the ball against his former club at 1-2 with a 4.26 ERA in a swing role. Los Angeles strikes out 1,037 times as a team while hitting .238, and at 24-32 at home the Angels offer little resistance to the National League's steadiest machine. The -123 looks thin for the record gap, a nod to Gasser's short starts and a Brewers bullpen night.

Gilbert Against The Slide
Regional

Twins at Mariners

Saturday, 4:10 PM ET | T-Mobile Park, Seattle, WA | SEA -173, O/U 7.0

Seattle has lost three straight and eight of ten, Friday's 5-3 defeat included, and the season is slipping: the Mariners are 53-58, third in the AL West and three games out of first place. The stop sign is Logan Gilbert, 8-6 with a 3.44 ERA, a 0.99 WHIP and 133 strikeouts against 27 walks in 125.2 innings. At home in a park that suppresses offense, with a 7.0 total that is the lowest on the board after Wrigley's, the market expects him to end the skid himself.

Minnesota is the quiet opposite, 56-55 and 7-3 over its last ten with two straight wins in Seattle. Left-hander Connor Prielipp is 3-5 with a 4.73 ERA and 76 strikeouts in 72.1 innings, and his margin for error is thin against even a .229-hitting Mariners lineup, because the Twins' own offense at 4.71 runs per game has carried them all month. Seattle's 30-24 home record against Minnesota's 26-28 road mark frames the -173.

Thornton's Audition
Regional

Marlins at Mets

Saturday, 4:10 PM ET | Citi Field, New York, NY | NYM -136, O/U 9.0

The Mets are 47-64 and buried 18.5 games back in the NL East, but rookie left-hander Zac Thornton has been one of the few reasons to watch: 2-1 with a 2.43 ERA and a 0.91 WHIP across his first 29.2 big-league innings. The market trusts the arm over the standings, laying -136 with a team that lost 5-2 at home on Friday.

Miami is the better story at 56-55, above .500 in August behind a 34-23 home record, though just 22-32 on the road. Right-hander Tyler Phillips is 2-5 with a 3.52 ERA that outruns his 63-to-38 strikeout-to-walk ratio, and the Marlins hit .250 as a team against the Mets' .233. A 9.0 total between two bottom-half offenses says the market sees two starters who put runners on and two bullpens that have been asked for too many innings.

Road Favorite Again
Regional

Pirates at Reds

Saturday, 6:40 PM ET | Great American Ball Park, Cincinnati, OH | PIT -126, O/U 8.5

Cincinnati outlasted Pittsburgh 8-7 in Friday's slugfest, a seventh win in ten games for a 52-57 club that keeps finding late runs at home. Lefty Andrew Abbott starts Saturday at 5-6 with a 3.85 ERA, though his 54 walks in 117 innings make him the least efficient strike-thrower on this part of the board, a real hazard against a Pittsburgh lineup that scores 5.15 runs per game, tied for second most in baseball.

The Pirates have lost four straight and seven of ten, which is why a 55-56 road team laying -126 raises eyebrows until the pitching card flips over: Braxton Ashcraft is 10-4 with a 4.05 ERA, a 1.19 WHIP and 135 strikeouts against 30 walks in 122.1 innings, facing his former organization's division rival with the better recent command by a wide margin. In this park, with these two offenses, 8.5 might be the most honest total on the slate after Friday's fifteen combined runs.

Powerhouse, Last In Class
Regional

Nationals at Braves

Saturday, 7:15 PM ET | Truist Park, Atlanta, GA | ATL -173, O/U 9.5

Washington owns the strangest stat line in the league: a league-leading 164 home runs and 5.42 runs per game, attached to a 55-56 record and fourth place. Friday's 6-2 loss in Atlanta was a third straight, and the road-home split explains a lot, 32-22 away against an ugly 23-34 at home. Veteran Miles Mikolas starts at 3-7 with a 5.65 ERA and only 52 strikeouts in 102 innings, a contact profile that is exactly what a 145-homer Atlanta lineup wants to see.

The Braves are 65-45, NL East leaders on a three-game winning streak with a 34-20 home record, and Reynaldo Lopez takes the ball at 4-3 with a 3.64 ERA in 71.2 innings. The 9.5 total is the second-highest non-Coors number of the day, a fair reading of Washington's power against Mikolas's hittability. Atlanta at -173 with this pitching edge at home is the market saying Friday was the template, not the exception.

The Early Window
Regional

Cardinals at Blue Jays

Saturday, 3:07 PM ET | Rogers Centre, Toronto, ON

The day's first pitch belongs to Toronto, where the 51-59 Blue Jays try to stack a third straight win after taking Friday's opener 3-1. Kevin Gausman gets the home start at 4-10 with a 4.44 ERA, a record that badly undersells 125 strikeouts against 35 walks in 123.2 innings; Toronto's 3.90 runs per game, among the lowest in the American League, is the real author of that win-loss line.

St. Louis, 54-56 and losers of seven of ten, counters with rookie lefty Quinn Mathews in just his second appearance of the season after two scoreless innings of relief work. The Cardinals hit .239 with 851 strikeouts, one of the lower-whiff but lower-impact profiles in the league, and at 26-24 on the road they have actually traveled better than they have played at Busch. With a fresh arm making a first start, the matchup is the day's biggest wild card and its earliest.

Coors At Twelve
Regional

Royals at Rockies

Saturday, 8:10 PM ET | Coors Field, Denver, CO | COL -120, O/U 12.0

A 12.0 total tells you where this game is played, and the pitching matchup does nothing to argue with it. Kansas City's Luinder Avila is 5-3 with a 4.95 ERA and 39 walks in 67.1 innings; Colorado's Ryan Feltner is 3-5 with a 5.73 ERA at altitude. Two of the league's four worst records meet with the Rockies, 43-67, installed as a home favorite over the 46-65 Royals, who have lost two straight including Friday's 3-1 opener in Denver.

Kansas City's road record is the quiet tiebreaker in the market's mind: the Royals are 20-38 away from Kauffman Stadium, among the worst travel marks in baseball, while Colorado holds a 25-29 home ledger that counts as respectable in this company. Both offenses hit near .245 to .252, neither pitches, and the smart framing is simple: the eleventh-place team hosting the twelfth-place team at a ballpark that turns mistakes into souvenirs.

After A Shutout
Regional

Giants at Padres

Saturday, 8:40 PM ET | Petco Park, San Diego, CA | SD -156, O/U 8.5

San Diego blanked San Francisco 7-0 on Friday, a statement from a 56-54 club that has won seven of ten and stabilized third place in the NL West. Walker Buehler gets the Saturday start at 6-5 with a 5.13 ERA across an even 100 innings, and the velocity of the market's trust, -156, comes less from his ratios than from the opponent: the Giants are 47-63, just 21-36 on the road, and were held to zero a night ago.

San Francisco's Tyler Mahle is 3-9 with a 4.96 ERA and a 1.40 WHIP, and his eight-week job has been damage control for a club 22 games out of the division lead. The offensive numbers frame the total honestly, San Diego at .235 and 4.19 runs per game against San Francisco's identical 4.19, two below-average groups in a pitcher's park. After a 7-0 night, 8.5 asks both lineups to double their Friday output combined.
