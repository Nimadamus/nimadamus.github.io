Dodgers vs Cubs: Matthew Boyd Meets A Rotation In Pieces At Wrigley

Eleven pitchers. That is how many arms the Los Angeles Dodgers currently have parked on the injured list, and the list reads like a rotation depth chart: Blake Snell, Tyler Glasnow, Ben Casparius, Gavin Stone, Bobby Miller. They arrive at Wrigley Field on Monday night at 69-43, owners of the best road record in baseball at 36-20, and they arrive without a starting pitcher named for the opener. Chicago counters with Matthew Boyd, a 35-year-old left-hander who has surrendered one earned run in his last fourteen innings. Analysis only, no picks.

A Ten-Game Lead And No One To Pitch

The standings and the injury list tell two different stories about the same team. Los Angeles is 69-43, ten games clear of Arizona in the National League West, hitting a major-league-best .262 as a club, and allowing a lower opponent batting average than any staff in the sport at .216. Los Angeles also has eleven pitchers on the injured list and could not name a starter for Monday night when the schedule went out.

Sunday made the depth problem visible. Emmet Sheehan got eleven outs against Boston, threw 58 pitches, gave up five earned runs, and handed the rest of the afternoon to four relievers who covered 6.1 innings between them. Jack Dreyer threw 27 pitches, Evan Phillips 11, Kyle Hurt 37 and Wyatt Mills 53. That is a bullpen day burned in a loss, on the back end of a home series in which Boston scored 20 runs in three games and swept the best team in the National League out of Chavez Ravine by 9-4, 3-2 and 8-4 scores. The Dodgers now fly to Chicago on a three-game losing streak with the same eleven names still unavailable.

Chicago has its own version of a good problem. The Cubs are 63-49, buried 6.5 games behind a Milwaukee club that has lost twice in its last ten, yet sitting four full games clear in the wild card race. They also just lost two of three to the Yankees at home and scored six runs total in the series, dropping the opener 2-0, winning 5-2, then losing the finale 2-1. A lineup that has posted a .338 on-base percentage, second only to the Dodgers, went quiet for three days against New York pitching. Both clubs walk into Monday needing the bleeding to stop, which is a strange sentence to write about teams a combined 132-92.

The Pitching Matchup

Matthew Boyd is the only announced starter in this game, and he has quietly become the steadiest thing in the Cubs rotation. The season line reads 6-1 with a 3.41 ERA and a 1.20 WHIP across 12 starts and 66 innings. Those 66 innings in 12 starts hint at a slow build, and the recent shape confirms it: Boyd has gone at least six innings in each of his last five outings, covering 32.1 innings and six earned runs, a 1.67 mark over that stretch. His last two were seven innings and one earned run against Pittsburgh on July 24 and seven scoreless against St. Louis on July 29, on 97 and 89 pitches.

How he does it matters against this lineup. Boyd throws a four-seam fastball 45.9 percent of the time at an average of 92.7 mph, then pairs it with a changeup he uses 27.7 percent of the time at 78.7 mph. That is a fourteen-mile-per-hour gap off the same arm slot, and it is the pitch that has to carry him, because the Dodgers stack left-handed bats and a lefty starter loses the platoon shield the moment Shohei Ohtani, Freddie Freeman, Max Muncy and Dalton Rushing come up in sequence. His slider sits at 15.6 percent and the curveball at 10.3 percent, both used mostly to change eye level.

Los Angeles has not announced who follows. The rotation names available to the club are the ones who have been covering: Yoshinobu Yamamoto went eight innings and threw 96 pitches on Saturday, so he is out of the equation, and Cole Irvin gave up six earned in six innings on Friday. Whoever gets the ball, the reality is that the Dodgers may be asking for length from a pitcher who has not consistently provided it, on a night when four of their relievers just worked. That is the single largest variable on this card, and no amount of a 3.64 team ERA changes it.

Injury Report & Lineup Notes

Los Angeles Dodgers
Eleven Pitchers Down, Plus The Catcher
The 60-day injured list holds Blake Snell, Tyler Glasnow, Ben Casparius, Bobby Miller, Gavin Stone, Brusdar Graterol and Jake Cousins. Blake Treinen, Brock Stewart and Will Klein are on the 15-day list. Catcher Will Smith is on the 60-day list, which has pushed Dalton Rushing into everyday duty; the rookie has answered with a .253/.335/.475 line and 12 home runs in 217 at-bats. Shohei Ohtani (.292/.397/.542, 24 home runs) and Freddie Freeman (.310 average) are healthy and carrying the offense.
Chicago Cubs
A Bullpen Held Together With Tape
Justin Steele, Cade Horton, Porter Hodge, Hunter Harvey, Shelby Miller and Riley Martin are on the 60-day injured list. Daniel Palencia, Phil Maton, Hoby Milner, Ethan Roberts, Ben Brown, Gavin Hollowell and Edward Cabrera are on the 15-day list. Infielder Matt Shaw is on the 10-day list. The one piece of good news is that Sunday's game barely touched the relief corps: Colin Rea went six innings, and only Ryan Rolison (30 pitches) and Aaron Civale (18 pitches) followed him. Pete Crow-Armstrong (.282/.383/.538, 24 home runs, 26 steals) and Seiya Suzuki (.840 OPS) headline the lineup.

Two runs is the entire offensive gap between these clubs: 572 for Los Angeles, 570 for Chicago, in 112 games apiece. The separation lives on the pitching side, and one number there is worth staring at. Chicago's staff has allowed 166 home runs, the second-highest figure in the majors behind only the Athletics. Wrigley Field in August is the least forgiving possible venue for that particular weakness, and the Dodgers have hit 143 of their own. The market set the total at 8 anyway, which is a bet on Boyd's changeup and on a Cubs bullpen that got Sunday off.

A counterpoint deserves equal weight. Los Angeles has hit .262 as a team, best in baseball, but ran into a Boston staff that held them to ten runs across three games at home. Their .216 opponent average is the lowest in the majors and it did not stop Boston from putting up twenty. Season-long profiles describe averages, and the last four days at Chavez Ravine were not average.

Keys To The Game

Dodgers Keys
Get to Boyd before the changeup settles in. He has thrown 89 and 97 pitches in his last two starts and worked into the seventh both times, which means the Cubs plan on him eating the middle innings so a thinned bullpen never has to. Ohtani, Freeman and Muncy seeing a 92.7 mph fastball in the first time through is the best look Los Angeles will get all night. Waiting until the sixth to solve him hands the game to a Chicago relief group that is fresh for once.
Cubs Keys
Make the Dodgers show their arm. Los Angeles has not announced a starter and just used four relievers on Sunday afternoon. Long at-bats in the first three innings drive whoever opens the game toward an early exit and force manager Dave Roberts into the shortest part of his staff. Chicago also has to score, which it did not do against New York; six runs in three games is not a formula that survives a night against a team allowing a .216 opponent average.

Betting Angle

Los Angeles is a -126 road favorite here, which prices in a 55.8 percent win probability and effectively says the market trusts the Dodgers name and the 36-20 road record more than it worries about an unnamed starter. Chicago at +104 is being treated as close to a coin flip at home against the best road team in baseball, and the run line reflects the same read: Dodgers -1.5 comes back at +135, a price that implies a blowout is genuinely unlikely.

That total tells the more interesting story. Eight runs at Wrigley Field in August, with a Cubs staff that has given up 166 home runs and a Dodgers lineup carrying 143 of them, is a number built almost entirely on Boyd's recent form and the Chicago bullpen's rest. Over comes back at -113 and under at -107, so the book has shaded it very slightly toward runs. Whether that holds depends on a variable nobody at either sportsbook can price yet, which is how many innings the Dodgers can get from whoever opens.

Final Thoughts

Series openers between division leaders and wild card leaders in August usually turn on rotation depth, and this one is a live demonstration of that. Chicago has the announced pitcher, the rested bullpen and the home dugout. Los Angeles has the better roster, the better record, the better road profile and a training room full of pitchers. The scoreboard resolves that argument by roughly the fifth inning.

The honest counterpoint to all the Dodgers-are-vulnerable framing is that this team is 36-20 away from home while missing most of a rotation, which suggests the depth problem has been priced into their results for a while and they keep winning anyway. The honest counterpoint to the Cubs case is that they scored six runs in three games at Wrigley over the weekend and the Dodgers staff still leads the majors in opponent average. Two teams playing badly, one very good pitcher, and a total that assumes the ballpark behaves. The rest of Monday's eight-game slate is broken down on the full MLB board.
