Steelers vs Browns: Four 2-1 Teams, One Thursday Night To Get Out Of The Pack

Pittsburgh Steelers at Cleveland Browns, Week 4 Thursday Night Football, Featured Game of the Day
Published October 1, 2026

Every team in the AFC North is 2-1. Pittsburgh and Cleveland get the first crack at breaking the tie, on a short week, on the lakefront, with two offenses that have mostly been grinding out field goals. The market has the Steelers a short road favorite and the total sitting under 40, and the numbers underneath explain why.

Matchup Context

Here's the setup. Pittsburgh, Baltimore, Cleveland and Cincinnati are all 2-1. Nobody in this division has separated, and whoever wins tonight wakes up Friday alone in first place, even if it's only for a couple of days. That's what makes a game between two offenses averaging under 18 points a night worth the prime slot.

Pittsburgh's three weeks have been a roller coaster. The Steelers opened with a 20-13 home win over Atlanta, then went to New England and scored three points in a 20-3 loss, then came home and outlasted Cincinnati 30-27. They've scored 53 and allowed 60, so the record is a little kinder than the scoreboard.

Cleveland's path went the other direction. The Browns got buried 34-10 in Jacksonville in Week 1, then won two one score games in a row: 23-19 at Tampa Bay and 21-18 at home against Carolina. They've scored 54 and allowed 71. Strip out the Jacksonville game and this is a team that has won close, low scoring football for two straight weeks.

So you've got two 2-1 teams that both have a negative point differential, both average right around 18 points, and both just played on Sunday. Short week, division rival, nobody pulling away. That's the profile of a game that gets decided by one drive.

Watson has already been sacked 9 times in three games. The Steelers' report is lighter, but Ramsey's status is worth checking right up to kickoff given who he'd be covering.

Rodgers, Watson And The Two Fronts

Pittsburgh with the ball

Aaron Rodgers is 66 of 113 for 700 yards, 4 touchdowns and 2 interceptions. That's a 58.4 percent completion rate and 6.2 yards per attempt, and as a team the Steelers are converting just 12 of 37 third downs (32.4 percent). Roman Wilson leads the club with 11 catches for 114 yards, which tells you the passing game hasn't found a true number one target yet.

The running game is the steadier piece. Jaylen Warren has 216 yards on 38 carries, 5.7 yards a pop, and with Rico Dowdle out he's the whole show. Pittsburgh averages 95.7 rushing yards a game on 4.3 yards per carry, and Cleveland is allowing 120.3 on the ground. Without Owusu-Koramoah at linebacker, that's the matchup the Steelers will lean on early.

Cleveland with the ball

Here's the surprise of the early season. Deshaun Watson is 56 of 82 for 587 yards with 5 touchdowns and 1 interception: 68.3 percent and 7.2 yards per attempt. Denzel Boston is his big play guy with 9 catches for 195 yards and 2 scores, which is 21.7 yards per reception. Cleveland's passing game has actually been efficient.

The ground game hasn't. Quinshon Judkins has 124 yards on 42 carries, about 3.0 a carry, and the team is at 3.5 yards per rush. Now the Browns are trying to fix that without their starting center and one of their guards. If Cleveland can't stay ahead of the sticks on early downs, Watson ends up in obvious passing situations against Watt (3.5 sacks) and a defense that has forced 6 turnovers.

On the other side, Mason Graham has 4 of Cleveland's 7 sacks. Rodgers has been dropped 8 times, so Graham against a Pittsburgh interior that has Gennings Dunker on injured reserve is the matchup to watch when the Steelers throw.

Look at the efficiency numbers and the market's opinion starts to make sense. Pittsburgh's offense is 29th in EPA per play at -0.141 and 26th in success rate. Its passing plays have averaged -0.177 EPA. That's a unit that has needed short fields and Warren runs to score, and it's the reason the Steelers can lose 20-3 one week and win 30-27 the next.

Cleveland's split is the strangest in the game. Browns dropbacks are averaging +0.106 EPA, which is genuinely good, while Browns runs are at -0.338, which is about as bad as it gets. Overall that nets out to -0.054 per play and 22nd in the league. The question tonight is whether Cleveland leans into what's working through the air or keeps feeding a run game behind a patched up interior line.

Defensively, Pittsburgh's -0.050 EPA allowed ranks 10th and Cleveland's -0.020 ranks 14th, so both defenses have been better than both offenses. The Steelers have also won the turnover battle by two, while the Browns have only two takeaways all season. On a night when both offenses gain barely over five yards a play, one extra possession matters a lot.

Keys To The Game

Steelers Keys
Win up front against a rebuilt middle. With both Jenkinses out, Watt and the Pittsburgh front have the best matchup on the field. Watson has taken 9 sacks already.
Keep feeding Warren. 5.7 yards a carry against a defense allowing 120.3 rushing yards a game and missing a starting linebacker.
Stay out of third and long. A 32.4 percent third down rate and -0.177 EPA on passes says Rodgers needs manageable downs.
Browns Keys
Let Watson throw early. Cleveland's passing EPA is +0.106 and its rushing EPA is -0.338. The efficient half of the offense is the arm.
Find Boston downfield. 195 yards on 9 catches, and Ramsey is questionable on the other side.
Get Graham home. He has 4 of the team's 7 sacks, and Rodgers has been sacked 8 times behind a line missing Dunker.

Market Context

DraftKings, as shown in ESPN's odds feed at 2:56 PM PT, has Pittsburgh -2.5 at -120 with Cleveland +2.5 at +100. The same feed lists the opening number as Pittsburgh -2.5 at -110 on both sides, so the point spread hasn't moved off 2.5, but the price has. The Steelers price went from -110 to -120 and the Browns from -110 to even money, which is a book telling you where the money has gone without wanting to move off a key number.

The moneyline moved the same way. Pittsburgh opened at -135 and Cleveland at +114, and it's now -148 and +124. Implied, that's 59.7 percent for the Steelers and 44.6 percent for the Browns before you take the vig out. Bovada at 2:57 PM PT is a touch further along at Pittsburgh -150 and Cleveland +130, with the spread at -2.5 (-120) and Cleveland +2.5 at even money.

The total is where the biggest move shows up. DraftKings opened at 40.5 and is now 38.5, with the over at -105 and the under at -115. Bovada is lower still at 37.5. Given two offenses at 17.7 and 18.0 points per game, both ranked outside the top 20 in EPA per play, the market drifting down two points isn't a shock. For context, ESPN's matchup predictor gives Pittsburgh a 55.1 percent chance to win, a bit below what the moneyline implies.

Final Thoughts

This game comes down to which offensive weakness gets exposed. Pittsburgh's passing game has been one of the least efficient in the league, but it has a healthy run game and a front that lines up against a Cleveland interior missing two starters. Cleveland's passing game has been one of the pleasant surprises of September, but its run game has been close to unusable and it just lost the guys who were supposed to fix it.

The Steelers' turnover edge and the injury report are the reasons the market has moved toward them. The Browns being at home, with the more efficient quarterback so far and a receiver averaging over 21 yards a catch, are the reasons it's only 2.5. With every team in the division at 2-1, whoever wins this one gets the AFC North to themselves for a few days.
