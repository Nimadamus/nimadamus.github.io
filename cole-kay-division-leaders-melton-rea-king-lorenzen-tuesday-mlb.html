MLB - Tuesday, July 28

Yankees-White Sox Division Race Headlines A Five-Game Tuesday

New York carries a six-of-eight surge into Rate Field trying to pull within range of Tampa Bay, and Chicago's White Sox are defending an AL Central lead that their raw record does not obviously support. Around that marquee, Chris Sale faces a Mets lineup that just erupted for 14 runs, a Tigers rookie with a sub-2.00 ERA hosts Baltimore, Paul Skenes' teammate Colin Rea runs into a hot Cardinals arm, and the Padres try to hold serve at home against a last-place Colorado club. Analysis only, no picks.

Marquee Matchup
Regional

Yankees at White Sox

Tuesday, 7:40 PM ET | Rate Field, Chicago, IL

New York (60-46) has won six of its last eight and sits just 2.5 games back of a idle Tampa Bay in the AL East, momentum it carried into Monday's series opener at Rate Field. Ali Sánchez hit a three-run homer, his first career long ball, and the Yankees rolled 9-5 to open the four-game set. Chicago (55-50) still leads the AL Central despite a record that would sit fourth in most other divisions, dropping its third loss in four games on Monday but holding the top spot on the strength of a soft division around it.

Gerrit Cole starts for New York at 3-5 with a 3.92 ERA, numbers that reflect a rotation that has had to absorb injuries to Carlos Rodón (elbow inflammation, 15-day IL) and Clarke Schmidt (60-day IL, internal brace rehab recovery), on top of losing Aaron Judge to a 60-day IL stint for a stress fracture in his right rib (July 15 imaging showed healing, but not enough to resume baseball activity) and Giancarlo Stanton and Cody Bellinger to separate 10-day leg injuries. Cole eating innings matters more than usual with the rotation this thin.

Chicago counters with Anthony Kay, who is 7-4 with a 4.21 ERA and leans on a bullpen fronted by closer Seranthony Domínguez (12 saves) and setup man Bryan Hudson (2.86 ERA). The White Sox have been able to hold the Central largely by not losing games they should win, and Kay giving Chicago length in front of that bullpen is the path to taking a game back after Monday's loss.

NL East Gap
SNY

Braves at Mets

Tuesday, 7:10 PM ET | Citi Field, Queens, NY

Atlanta (62-44) leads the NL East by a comfortable margin over a Mets club (45-62) that sits 17.5 games back, but New York just reminded the division it can still swing the bat, scoring 14 runs in Monday's series opener. Chris Sale (11-6, 2.19 ERA, 134 K) takes the ball for Atlanta looking to make sure Monday was the exception, against Mets right-hander Christian Scott (3-2, 3.13 ERA), who is making his 15th start with New York still without Juan Soto (calf strain, 10-day IL).

The full breakdown of the pitching matchup, the Soto injury and both team's keys to the game is on the Featured Game page.

Breakout Arm
MASN / DSN

Orioles at Tigers

Tuesday, 6:40 PM ET | Comerica Park, Detroit, MI

Neither of these clubs is contending this season, Baltimore at 52-55 and Detroit at 44-62, but the pitching matchup is the story. Rookie right-hander Troy Melton has been the best thing to happen to Detroit's season: after opening the year on the injured list with right elbow inflammation, he debuted May 24 and has been dominant since, 5-1 with a 1.95 ERA and 55 strikeouts, and the Tigers are 26-20 in games since his first start, the third-best mark in the American League over that stretch.

Baltimore sends Dean Kremer (1-3, 5.06 ERA, 34 K) to the mound, and the moneyline reflects the gap between the two arms: Detroit is priced at -144 at home, with Baltimore getting +119 on the road. For an Orioles club still searching for consistency in its rotation, matching Melton's stuff for six innings is a tall order regardless of either team's overall record.

Detroit's turnaround since Melton's debut is the more interesting subplot for a team well outside the Central race; Baltimore, meanwhile, needs its lineup to manufacture runs early before Melton settles in, since waiting on him to tire rarely worked against opponents the rest of July.

Wild Card Push
Regional

Cubs at Cardinals

Tuesday, 7:45 PM ET | Busch Stadium, St. Louis, MO

Chicago (60-46) sits second in the NL Central and holds the top Wild Card position, while St. Louis (53-53) is fighting to stay in the mix at fourth in the division. Colin Rea starts for the Cubs at 7-7 with a 4.85 ERA and 81 strikeouts, coming off a start against Detroit last Thursday in which he allowed four earned runs on six hits across five innings, a line that will need to be sharper against a Cardinals lineup playing meaningful games in late July.

St. Louis counters with Michael McGreevy, who has quietly been one of the more efficient arms in the division at 4-8 with a 3.07 ERA, a 1.13 WHIP and a 2.74 strikeout-to-walk ratio, making his 21st start of the season. His last time out, against Arizona on July 25, he allowed four earned runs on seven hits across 6.1 innings, a tougher outing than his season line suggests but still enough to keep the Cardinals competitive into the seventh. The market has this one nearly even, Chicago -116 and St. Louis -106, with the total set at 9 runs, a number that reflects two offenses capable of scoring in bunches against pitching that has been solid but not dominant.

NL West Series Opener
Regional

Rockies at Padres

Tuesday, 9:40 PM ET | Petco Park, San Diego, CA

San Diego (55-52) opens a series at home against last-place Colorado (42-65) with the market treating this as close to a formality, Padres -190 and Rockies +154, total set at 8 runs. Michael King takes the ball for San Diego at 6-7 with a 3.24 ERA, a 1.17 WHIP and 103 strikeouts against just 49 walks over 119.1 innings, and he was sharp in his last outing against Atlanta, allowing a run in the first inning before shutting the Braves down the rest of the way with seven strikeouts.

Colorado sends Michael Lorenzen (3-9, 6.53 ERA, 1.79 WHIP, 78 K) into a difficult spot on the road against a Padres lineup that has had its own cold stretches this month but still represents a significant step up in competition from what the last-place Rockies have faced recently. San Diego's own offense went through a rough patch earlier in July, including a game against Arizona's Merrill Kelly in which a single Manny Machado homer accounted for the only run, a reminder that even a heavy favorite on this staff can go quiet against a good arm. Lorenzen is not that, and King holding serve at home is the more likely path.
