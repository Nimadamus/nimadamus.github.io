Rays -1 And Athletics Under 3.5: Perkins Against Jax

Here's the matchup off MLB's own stat feed this afternoon.

Perkins has already seen this lineup. On August 12 the Rays tagged him for 12 hits and 6 earned runs in 5 innings, the last game of a series in which Tampa Bay scored 30 runs in three days. Opponents are averaging 6.57 runs a game when he starts. He strikes guys out, 119 of them, but the walks and the home runs keep handing innings away.

The -1 is the part worth slowing down on. A one run Rays win is a push, not a loss, so what this ticket needs is a win by two or more. And here's a strange little fact about Griffin Jax's season: the Rays have won 10 of his 20 starts, and all 10 of those wins came by two runs or more. When Tampa Bay wins behind him, it hasn't been close. At home this year the Rays are 50 and 25, the best home record in the American League, and they've won three straight.

Now the under. The A's are a different team away from their own park. Their OPS at home is .782. On the road it's .662, and they've scored 3.84 runs a game in 75 road games, getting held to three or fewer in 39 of them. Jax has been tougher on right handed hitters, a .669 OPS against, than on lefties at .736, so the A's left handed bats are the ones to watch. The Trop's a dome, so there's no wind to help anybody.

One honest caveat about Jax: 11 of his 31 appearances have come in relief and he rarely goes past five innings, which means the Rays bullpen owns the back half. Tampa Bay's staff has a 3.87 ERA at home.

The Cardinals still lost that game 7 to 6, which is the whole week in miniature for St. Louis: they dropped three one run games in San Francisco before winning Monday's.

What worries me is the Cardinals offense at home. It's a .667 OPS at Busch this year and 3.83 runs a game, worse than on the road. Pallante's home ERA is 4.02 too, against 2.76 on the road. This price isn't about St. Louis being good. It's about San Francisco being this bad away from home.

Yankees -165 And Twins Under 3.5: Max Fried At Target Field

Two tickets, one pitcher. Max Fried has allowed 4 home runs in 99 innings this season. That's 0.36 per nine. His ERA is 2.73 with a 0.99 WHIP, and it's even better on the road, 2.47 across 10 starts. Left handed hitters have a .391 OPS against him, which is almost unfair.

Minnesota is where you'd want to be sending him. The Twins are 3 and 7 in their last ten and they've scored three or fewer in 7 of those 10 games, including three runs in an 8 to 3 loss to these Yankees on Monday. They hit .678 in OPS against left handed pitching, compared with .740 against righties. And across the whole season, teams facing Fried have scored three or fewer in 11 of his 18 starts.

The Yankees side has a real wrinkle, and it's Bailey Ober at home. Ober's ERA at Target Field is 3.16 with a 1.07 WHIP. On the road it's 5.84. So he's not the soft spot his season line suggests. The case for the Yankees rests on Fried and on a team that's 45 and 31 on the road, the best road record in baseball, with a 2.91 staff ERA away from home.

The one thing to watch is Fried's pitch count. He's thrown 3.1 and 3.2 innings in two of his last three starts, and New York has only won 10 of his 18. The under survives a short Fried outing a lot better than the moneyline does.

Guardians -149: Foster Griffin Against Another White Sox Bullpen Night

Cleveland lost 7 to 3 on Monday, and the White Sox now lead the American League Central by a game and a half, 77 and 73 against 76 and 75. The Guardians still hold the third wild card spot, but a second straight loss to Chicago would make that division a long climb.

Chicago's opening with Chris Murphy, a left hander who's made 31 appearances and only 2 starts. He threw 33 pitches in St. Louis on Sunday. This is a bullpen game again. Cleveland's got Foster Griffin, 15 and 5 with a 3.65 ERA on the season, most of it in Washington before the trade.

I'll be straight about Griffin in Cleveland. In seven starts since the deal his ERA is 5.94, and most of that damage came in one night: 8 earned runs in 2.1 innings at Baltimore last Wednesday. Take that start out and he's allowed 14 earned runs in 31 innings. He's also far better against lefties, a .647 OPS, than righties at .723.

The lefty matchup cuts the wrong way for Cleveland at the plate, too. The Guardians hit .658 in OPS against left handed pitching, and the White Sox hit lefties better than righties. What the ticket is really buying is the middle innings, when Chicago's running through relievers after Murphy and Cleveland's got a starter who can give them five or six. The White Sox are 34 and 42 on the road.

Braves And Cubs Under 8.5: Pérez, Gausman And A Quiet Wind

This is the smallest ticket on the card, one unit, and it should be. Martín Pérez threw seven scoreless innings against Tampa Bay last Thursday and has a 3.08 ERA, but the split is ugly: 1.80 at home, 4.76 on the road with an .841 OPS against. Kevin Gausman's been worse since the Cubs got him, 23 earned runs in 39 innings over seven starts, though Chicago's won five of them.

So why the under? Start with the wind. Wrigley is the one park where the forecast changes the bet, and tonight it's calling for the mid 70s with the breeze under 3 miles an hour around first pitch. That's as neutral as Wrigley gets. The Braves bring a .689 road OPS and have gone 4 and 6 over their last ten, and Atlanta's pitching staff has a 3.62 ERA away from home. Their three meetings in May produced 7, 5 and 2 total runs.

The Cubs, though, score 5.36 a game at home, and only 33 of 76 games at Wrigley this season have finished with 8 runs or fewer. Monday's opener went 7 to 3. It's a price on two pitchers keeping it quiet on a still night, and it's sized that way.

The Argument Against This Card

Both big correlated positions can go at once. If Perkins has one of his good nights, and he's had them, the Rays ticket and the A's under can both lose, 8.12 units together. Same story if Ober pitches like he does at home and the Twins find three runs off the Yankees bullpen.

The Cardinals can't hit at home. A .667 OPS at Busch is a real problem, and they've just lost three one run games to this same team.

Griffin's last start was a disaster, and Cleveland's lineup is weakest against exactly the kind of pitcher Chicago is starting.
