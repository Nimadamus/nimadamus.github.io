How The Units Are Counted

The two unders at -125 need 55.56 percent each. The Phillies at -154 need 60.63. The Brewers and Reds at -110 need 52.38, and the two -105 tickets need 51.22. The whole card, 14.13 divided by 25.63, is 55.13 percent.

The distribution matters as much as the total. Cleveland is 26.5 percent of the risk, Philadelphia 21.8, Texas 17.7, and the last three are 11.7, 11.2 and 11.2. Going 4-2 in any combination leaves the day about 1.4 units green. Going 3-3 loses roughly 0.6. That is a narrow band, which is what a flat card gives you: fewer disasters and fewer bailouts.

Three Units On The Under In Cleveland

The other half is the Cleveland lineup, and it is the weakest on the slate. The Guardians are 72-70 and score 4.06 runs a game on a .686 OPS, the lowest of the twelve clubs playing on this card. A total needs both teams to participate. When one of them is the least productive offense on the board and the other has to face a 2.46 ERA, seven is a long way up.

The case against is Framber Valdez, who is the weaker starter here at 4.36 over 150.2 innings with a 1.40 WHIP. Detroit is 64-77 but scores 4.39 a game, better than Cleveland does. If this ticket loses, the Tigers put four or five up early and the Guardians add one late.

Under 9 In A Bandbox

What holds the number is the other three quarters of the game. Milwaukee's staff carries a 3.50 ERA and allows 3.81 runs a game, the second best run prevention on this card. Cincinnati is 67-74 and scores 4.17 on a 4.68 team ERA. For nine to clear, either the Reds find five against a Brewers staff that has not allowed five a game to anybody, or Milwaukee puts up seven or eight on its own. The second is live. The first is the part that usually fails, and a nine run total normally needs both halves to show up.

Dustin May starts for Milwaukee at a 6.41 ERA over 19.2 innings and Andrew Abbott for Cincinnati at 4.49 over 148.1. Neither is the reason for the play. The bullpens are.

Five Unders Is Not One Bet

The standard objection to a card like this is that five unders on one night is really one bet, because run scoring rises and falls across the whole league together. It is a confident claim and it is testable, so it got tested rather than repeated.

Across 2,122 games of the 2026 regular season through September 4, the intraclass correlation of combined runs within a calendar date is 0.0056. A permutation test that reshuffles every game at random across the same slate shapes returns p equal to 0.31, meaning roughly one random season in three looks at least as clustered as the real one. Drawing five games at random from a single slate, all five finish under 8.5 3.44 percent of the time against an independent 3.40. The daily share of games going under has actually bounced around slightly less than independence predicts.

So this is six bets, not one. What does move is the season, not the night: May averaged 8.61 combined runs and June averaged 9.35. If four of these lose tonight it will be four wrong opinions, and there is no shared condition to blame it on.
