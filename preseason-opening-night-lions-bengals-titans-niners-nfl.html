NFL Preseason - Thursday, August 13

Six Preseason Games And The Lines Tell You Who Plans To Play Nobody

The full 2026 preseason schedule opens tonight, a week after the Panthers edged the Cardinals 33-30 in the Hall of Fame Game. Six games, and the numbers are doing the talking: a Titans club that went 3-14 last season is favored by six in San Francisco against a 12-5 team, because August prices coaching intent, not rosters. Detroit at Cincinnati opens the night at Paycor Stadium. Analysis only, no picks.

Marquee Matchup - The Opener On The River

Lions at Bengals

Thursday, 7:00 PM ET | Paycor Stadium, Cincinnati, OH

The first full preseason night of 2026 opens on the Ohio River, and the market has Cincinnati laying -6.5 with the total at 38.5, via DraftKings. A touchdown-sized preseason spread is rare and it carries one specific message: the books expect the Bengals' depth to play more, and better, than whatever Detroit dresses tonight.

Both franchises enter August needing different things from it. Detroit went 9-8 last season, a step back into the pack after years as one of the NFC's measuring sticks, and a preseason opener on the road is traditionally where the Lions' staff evaluates the back half of the roster rather than chasing a scoreboard. Cincinnati is coming off a 6-11 season, and August reps matter more to a team climbing out of a losing year than to one protecting an established core.

At 38.5, the total is among the lowest of the night, tied with two other games, which is the market's shorthand for two teams expected to run simple installs and drain clock. For viewers the watch-list is straightforward: which quarterbacks take the second-half snaps, and which fringe receivers turn scripted preseason drives into roster decisions by the end of the month.

Two Playoff-Caliber Rosters, Backup Football

Packers at Steelers

Thursday, 7:00 PM ET | Acrisure Stadium, Pittsburgh, PA

Green Bay at Pittsburgh is the night's best franchise pedigree matchup, and the road team is favored: Packers -3 with the total at 38.5, via DraftKings. Green Bay finished 9-7-1 last season and Pittsburgh went 10-7, two teams that made January without scaring anyone once they got there, which is exactly the profile that treats August as a laboratory.

Pittsburgh's preseason openers under this regime have long been about the defensive back seven and whichever young quarterback is being auditioned for the room's third seat. Green Bay's roster math runs through its receiver depth chart, perennially the league's most crowded, where a preseason catch in traffic can be worth a September paycheck.

A field-goal line with the road team favored usually means the away staff has signaled longer run-outs for its twos while the home side rests deeper into the depth chart. Acrisure Stadium in mid-August also offers the small, real pleasure of preseason: a full NFL stadium, a meaningless scoreboard, and forty roster decisions being made in plain sight.

The 14-3 Team Is The Home Underdog

Colts at Patriots

Thursday, 7:30 PM ET | Gillette Stadium, Foxborough, MA

Here is tonight's cleanest illustration of what preseason lines actually measure. New England went 14-3 last season, the best record of any team playing tonight. Indianapolis went 8-9 and missed the playoffs. The market: Colts -3 with the total at 37.5, the lowest number of the night, via DraftKings.

A 14-3 team has nothing to learn in August that is worth a starter's hamstring, so New England's night belongs to the bottom of the roster from the first snap. Indianapolis, one game under .500 and close enough to contention to feel it, has real competitions to settle and more incentive to let its twos play extended, organized football. That asymmetry is worth three points to the oddsmakers regardless of what the season-win markets say about these franchises.

A 37.5 total completes the picture: two teams expected to play conservative, run-heavy, clock-friendly football with vanilla call sheets. Gillette Stadium preseason games have looked like that for two decades, and there is no roster-building reason for tonight to be different.

Two 3-14 Teams Starting Over

Cardinals at Raiders

Thursday, 8:00 PM ET | Allegiant Stadium, Las Vegas, NV

Nobody on tonight's board needs August more than these two. Arizona and Las Vegas both finished 3-14 last season, identical records at the bottom of their conferences, and both are running the kind of roster churn where a preseason game is genuinely the most important football of a hundred careers. The Raiders are -3 at home with the total at 41.5, the highest number of the night, via DraftKings.

Arizona is also the only team tonight with live game film already banked: the Cardinals played the Hall of Fame Game a week ago and lost it 33-30 to Carolina in Canton. Thirty-three points allowed in an exhibition is not a defensive indictment in August, but sixty-three combined points in their opener is a big reason this game carries the board's highest total. Rebuilding teams play their young players deeper into the night, and young players on 3-14 rosters tackle like it.

Vegas gets its first look of the summer in front of the home crowd at Allegiant. For two front offices this early in a rebuild, tonight's film is the real product: sixty-plus snaps of fringe roster evaluation, with the scoreboard as a side effect. The 41.5 says the market expects that side effect to be loud.

The Tightest Line Of The Night

Chargers at Texans

Thursday, 8:00 PM ET | NRG Stadium, Houston, TX

Tonight's closest number lands in Houston: Chargers -1.5 with the total at 38.5, via DraftKings. It is also the matchup with the most combined 2025 success, Los Angeles at 11-6 and Houston at 12-5, two playoff teams from a year ago meeting in a game where neither will show a single page of the real playbook.

A line this tight between two good teams reads as the market's shrug: comparable depth, comparable caution, no strong signal from either staff about extended runs for the twos. Houston's 12-5 season made it one of the AFC's established powers, and established powers treat the preseason opener as a controlled scrimmage with television cameras.

What makes it watchable is the collision of two deep defensive rosters. Both franchises have built their recent success on defensive talent, and preseason openers are where the next wave of that talent, the day-three picks and the undrafted corners, either shows up on film or disappears from the league. At NRG Stadium in the 8:00 window, the second halves will matter more to both buildings than the firsts.

Six Points Says Everything About August

Titans at 49ers

Thursday, 9:00 PM ET | Levi's Stadium, Santa Clara, CA

Frame this line and hang it in a classroom. Tennessee went 3-14 last season. San Francisco went 12-5. Tonight the Titans are -6 on the road with the total at 39.5, via DraftKings, the second-largest spread of the night, laid by the team that lost eleven more games. Preseason spreads price how long each coaching staff intends to play real football, and nothing else.

Both directions of that logic are sound. A 3-14 roster has jobs open at every level, so Tennessee's competitive snaps will stretch deep into the second half with players fighting for their professional lives. A 12-5 team with January expectations has nothing to gain at Levi's Stadium in August beyond getting its ones on and off the field untouched, and San Francisco's championship-window rosters have played their preseason openers accordingly for years.

Kickoff for the nightcap comes at 9:00 ET, closing a six-game evening that began in Cincinnati two hours earlier. By the end of it, the league will have its first real 2026 film on roughly four hundred fringe players, and the only numbers that survive to September will be the ones in the personnel reports, not the final scores.
