MLB Free Pick of the Day

Twelve Units On Eight Games Where The Arm Beats The Record

August 11, 2026|9 min read|BetLegend

Tanner Bibee is 4-11.

He also owns a 3.85 ERA, a 1.13 WHIP, 110 strikeouts in 138 innings and the highest workload of any of the thirty starters scheduled tonight. Opponents are hitting .231 against him. His most recent time out, on August 5 against the Mets, he struck out ten in five and two-thirds innings and walked nobody. The reason his record looks like a rebuilding club's fifth starter is that Cleveland scores 3.97 runs a game, the lowest figure in the major leagues, and there is no version of pitching well that survives that for a full season.

That is the thread running through all eight positions on tonight's twelve-unit card. Seven of them come down to a starting pitcher whose actual work looks nothing like his win-loss line, in either direction. Nick Martinez is 11-3 while striking out barely five men per nine innings. Cristopher Sanchez has fifteen wins and gets there throwing two pitches most of the night. Tomoyuki Sugano is 11-5 with 22 home runs allowed. Paul Skenes has 162 strikeouts and a losing record.

Fifteen games, eight plays, twelve units. Here is every one of them with the number that supports it and the number that could bury it.

Marlins Team Total Under 3.5: Skenes Against A Lineup That Went Quiet Six Times In Ten

Paul Skenes is 9-10. Read the rest of the line before deciding what that means: a 3.96 ERA, a 1.12 WHIP, 162 strikeouts in 129.2 innings and a .226 opponent batting average across 24 starts. He leads every pitcher working tonight in strikeouts by a wide margin. The Pirates score 5.02 runs a game, so this is not the Bibee problem in reverse; Pittsburgh's issue is a 4.34 staff ERA behind him and a bullpen that gave up eleven runs to the Mets on August 9.

His arsenal is the least predictable on the board. The four-seamer is 41 percent of what he throws, and after that it is a sweeper at 15.9 percent, a changeup at 13.9, a splitter at 10.7, a sinker at 10.5 and a slider at 7.4. Five secondary pitches, none of them a token look. That is a nightmare profile for a Miami lineup carrying a .251 average and a .731 OPS.

The Marlins have scored three runs or fewer in six of their last ten games: two against the Mets on July 30, two on August 2, two at Atlanta on August 4, one on August 5, three on August 6 and three against the Angels on August 7. Their season figure is 4.40 runs a game.

Here is the part that should make you uncomfortable, and it is the reason this is two units rather than three. Miami scored seven runs on August 8 and twelve on August 9 against the Angels. That is the loudest 48 hours the Marlins have had all month, and they are 36-24 at loanDepot park. Skenes has also been ordinary lately: thirteen earned runs over his last three starts across 14.1 innings, with four walks in each of the last two. At -147 the market needs this to land 59.5 percent of the time. A rested Miami bullpen, off since Sunday, does not matter to this bet at all, but a Skenes outing that ends in the fifth inning does.

Braves Team Total Under 4.5: McLean's Sinker Against A Home Club That Keeps Landing On Four

Atlanta is 71-48 and 39-21 at Truist Park, and the Braves have still scored four runs or fewer in seven of their last ten games. Look at the sequence: six, eight, four, four, four, eleven, two, four, two, five. Three straight fours against Washington and Miami in the first week of August, then two twos in the Bronx.

Nolan McLean is the reason to lean on that pattern tonight. The Mets right-hander is 8-7 with a 3.51 ERA and 156 strikeouts in 130.2 innings, holding hitters to a .216 average. He is a sinker-first starter, 32.5 percent, and he pairs it with a curveball at 14.7 percent and a sweeper at 14.5. On July 19 he threw six shutout innings against Philadelphia with ten strikeouts. On July 30 he gave Miami six and a third and two runs.

The counterargument is loud. Atlanta hung eleven on Miami on August 6, and Martin Perez, the Braves starter tonight, is the kind of pitcher who keeps his own club in low-scoring games rather than shootouts, which does nothing for a bet on Atlanta's bats. McLean's last time out he allowed five earned runs in five innings to Cleveland on August 6, his worst start in a month. New York's bullpen is thinner than it looks with Devin Williams and Cionel Perez both on the 15-day injured list. If McLean exits after five, the Braves get a lot of at-bats against relievers who are already being asked for too much.

Diamondbacks Team Total Over 4.5: Twenty-Two Home Runs Walk Into Chase Field

Tomoyuki Sugano's record says 11-5. His peripherals say something else entirely. He has surrendered 22 home runs in 107.2 innings, more than any other starter on tonight's slate, while striking out only 61 batters in that span. His most-thrown secondary pitch is a splitter at 21.4 percent, and his four-seamer is down at 20.5. A pitcher who misses that few bats has to live on soft contact, and the long ball total says the contact has not been soft.

Behind him sits the worst pitching staff in baseball. Colorado's 5.51 team ERA, 1.52 WHIP and 687 runs allowed are all the worst marks in the majors. Arizona is 36-25 at home and just put nine runs on this same Rockies staff on Monday night.

Say the risk plainly: this Arizona lineup has been streaky and short-handed. The Diamondbacks have scored four or fewer in six of their last ten, including one-run nights on August 6 and August 8. Lourdes Gurriel Jr., Jordan Lawlar and Tommy Troy are all on the 10-day injured list. Arizona's .242 team average and .710 OPS are both in the bottom third of the league. This bet is a wager on the pitcher and the park, not on a hot offense, and it is sized at a unit and a half for exactly that reason.

Phillies Moneyline: Fifteen Wins And A Changeup

Cristopher Sanchez is 15-4 with a 2.65 ERA and 174 strikeouts in 149.2 innings, and he does it with essentially three pitches: a sinker 43 percent of the time, a changeup 37.8 percent, and a slider 19.3. Almost eighty percent of what he throws is a sinker or a changeup. Hitters know it and still cannot square it up. On August 1 he struck out eleven Orioles in six innings.

Philadelphia is 64-56, has won seven of its last ten and is 32-26 away from Citizens Bank Park. St. Louis is 59-60 and, oddly for a club hanging around the wild card race, 30-34 at Busch Stadium. The Phillies are -163 on the road with the total at 7.5.

Philadelphia Phillies MONEYLINE (-163)

Andre Pallante is the complication, and he is a real one. The Cardinals right-hander is 12-6 with a 3.63 ERA, and over his last four starts he has allowed six earned runs in 23.2 innings, a 2.28 mark. He does not strike anybody out, 90 in 124 innings, which means Philadelphia's .238 team average has to actually put the ball in play against a sinker-slider pitcher who lets his defense work. One unit, because -163 already asks for a 62 percent hit rate and Pallante has earned more respect than his season line gives him.

Rays Moneyline: The Biggest Position On The Card

Tampa Bay has won seven in a row. The Rays are 72-46, the best record in the American League, 8-2 over their last ten. Oakland is 47-72, 2-8 in its last ten, and 21-37 at Sutter Health Park, the worst home record in baseball. The A's have allowed 684 runs behind a 5.45 team ERA.

Nick Martinez is the strangest line on the whole board. He is 11-3 with a 2.65 ERA and a 1.10 WHIP, and he has struck out 73 batters in 129 innings, roughly five per nine. Nobody starting tonight misses fewer bats. What he does instead is refuse to walk anyone: twenty free passes all season. His mix is a sinker at 30.8 percent, a changeup at 27.6 and a cutter at 20.9, three pitches that all move toward or away from the same tunnel. On August 5 he gave Colorado five and two-thirds scoreless innings without issuing a walk.

Mason Barnett takes the ball for Oakland. He has made two starts among sixteen appearances this season and carries a 5.56 ERA, and his last outing was four and a third innings with five earned runs allowed to Cincinnati on August 6.

Tampa Bay Rays MONEYLINE (-173)
2.5 units | Win outright, any margin

The market set this total at 10.5, the highest number of the night, which tells you what it thinks of both pitching staffs in that ballpark. A pitcher with a 5.09 strikeout rate in a small park against a lineup that hits enough home runs to have 140 on the season is not a comfortable place to have two and a half units. Tampa Bay also lost Griffin Jax to the 15-day injured list on August 9 with right elbow discomfort, which thins the late innings. This is the largest position because the gap between a 72-win club and a 47-win club playing at home in a converted Triple-A yard is the widest edge on the slate, not because the matchup is risk free.

Guardians +1.5: Buying A Run Against A Bullpen Game

Detroit is only -124 tonight with the total at 8, which is the market saying this is close to a coin flip. Taking Cleveland +1.5 at -190 turns that into a bet that the Guardians either win or lose by exactly one.

Bibee is the reason. Six innings and two earned runs against Minnesota on July 20, six and three against Tampa Bay on July 25, seven and a third and two against Arizona on July 31, five and two-thirds with ten strikeouts against the Mets on August 5. Four quality-adjacent starts in a row from a pitcher whose record reads 4-11. His pitch usage is remarkably flat: cutter 24.8 percent, sinker 23.2, four-seam 22.2, changeup 14.3, curveball 10.8. Nothing above a quarter of his pitches, which is why hitters keep putting weak contact in play against him.

Detroit counters with Drew Anderson, who has appeared in 41 games and started three of them. His 4.01 ERA over 67.1 innings is fine, but the shape of the night matters more than the number: this is a bullpen game dressed as a start, and the Tigers have Jack Flaherty on the 15-day injured list.

Now the number that should worry you, because it is the ugliest counterpoint on the card. Detroit has averaged 7.5 runs over its last ten games. Thirteen at Oakland on July 31, eleven on August 2, eight in Seattle on August 4, eleven on August 6, eight in San Francisco on August 8. The Tigers are 7-3 in that stretch. Cleveland's offense, meanwhile, is the worst run-producing unit in the sport. A run and a half of insurance is worth paying for, and it may not be enough.

Brewers And Padres Under 7.5: Petco At Its Quietest

These two played on Monday night. San Diego won 3-2. Five total runs.

Milwaukee has averaged 3.5 runs over its last ten games, with a zero against the Angels on August 2 and a two in the series opener here. San Diego has averaged 4.6 in the same stretch and scores 4.22 for the season, one of the lower marks in the National League. The Brewers own the second-best team ERA in baseball at 3.47.

Kyle Harrison starts for Milwaukee at 9-2 with a 2.84 ERA, a 1.04 WHIP and 111 strikeouts in 88.2 innings. He is close to a two-pitch pitcher, four-seamer 57.8 percent and a slurve 30.9, and on August 5 he threw five innings of one-hit, ten-strikeout ball against Pittsburgh.

Walker Buehler is the honest problem here. His season line is 6-5 with a 5.07 ERA and a 1.46 WHIP, the worst combination of any starter involved in tonight's card, and he has walked 45 hitters in 108.1 innings. Buehler has been sharper lately, eight earned runs across his last four starts, but a pitcher who puts that many runners on gives a good offense innings where the number stops mattering. Milwaukee is also missing JoJo Romero and Joel Kuhnel out of the bullpen. One unit at -115, no more.

Royals And Dodgers Under 8.5: A Rehab Start And A Cold Offense

Michael Wacha has been the quiet best pitcher on Kansas City's staff. Over his last four starts he has allowed five earned runs in 24.2 innings: one against San Francisco on July 20, two against Detroit on July 25, one against Colorado on July 31 and one against Minnesota on August 6. Season line, 5-8 with a 3.44 ERA and 112 strikeouts in 144 innings. His changeup is his second pitch at 24.4 percent behind a 27 percent four-seamer.

Los Angeles is 71-48 and has scored 3.5 runs a game over its last ten. That stretch includes single runs against the Cubs on August 4 and two-run nights on August 8 and August 9 in Phoenix. The Dodgers are still without both of their catchers, Will Smith on the 60-day injured list and Dalton Rushing on the 10-day. Kansas City averages 3.8 over the same ten games and is 20-41 on the road.

THE PLAY

Royals / Dodgers UNDER 8.5 (-115)
1 unit | 8 total runs or fewer

Blake Snell is listed as the Los Angeles starter, and that is where this gets complicated. He has thrown three major league innings this season, one start back on May 9, and the Dodgers sent him on a rehab assignment to Triple-A Oklahoma City on August 4. Whatever he gives them tonight will be short, and a short start means a Dodgers bullpen missing Blake Treinen, Brock Stewart and Will Klein covering four or five innings against a Royals lineup that put five on the board Monday. These two combined for eleven runs last night. That result is the single best argument against this bet, and it is the reason it sits at one unit instead of two.

What Can Beat This Card

Three things, ranked by how likely they are to actually happen tonight.

Detroit's offense is the clearest danger. A club averaging seven and a half runs over ten games does not need a good matchup to beat a run and a half, and Cleveland cannot trade blows with anybody. Second is the pair of team total unders landing on the wrong side of a bullpen. Both the Marlins and the Braves bets survive a short start from the opposing ace only if the relief corps behind him holds, and neither New York nor Pittsburgh has a bullpen worth trusting after August 9, when the Mets scored eleven in Pittsburgh and the Pirates burned six relievers. Third is Buehler. If the Padres right-hander walks four again, Milwaukee gets the kind of inning that turns a 7.5 into a formality.

What the card has going for it is that seven of the eight positions are supported by a starting pitcher who has been demonstrably better than his record, and one is supported by a starter who has been demonstrably worse. That is a coherent set of bets rather than eight separate hunches, and it is why twelve units is on the table instead of six.
