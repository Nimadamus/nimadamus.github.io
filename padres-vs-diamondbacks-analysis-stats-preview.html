A Series Arizona Has Already Bent Its Way

San Diego is 59-56 and Arizona is 61-54, two clubs separated by two and a half games in a National League wild card race that has room for both of them right now. This is the fourth game of a series that has already tilted hard toward the home side: Arizona won the opener 5-1 on August 3, San Diego answered 9-4 on August 4, and the Diamondbacks took control back with a 10-4 win on August 5. Two of three, both by wide margins, is the story of this week.

Zoom out to the full season and the same shape holds. Arizona has won six of ten decided meetings between these clubs in 2026, with an early-season game in March ending in a tie. Neither club has controlled the rivalry outright, but the Diamondbacks have owned the more recent stretches, including this week.

Both teams arrive playing solid baseball independent of each other. Arizona is 6-4 in its last ten games and San Diego is 7-3, both above-.500 stretches that have not lined up with what has actually happened when the two clubs share a field this week.

Injury Report & Lineup Notes

San Diego Padres
Season-Ending
Yu Darvish has been on the restricted list all year following ulnar collateral ligament surgery, removing the pitcher who was supposed to be San Diego's rotation anchor. Outfielder Ramon Laureano is also done for the year after right hip surgery, and reliever prospect Robby Hoeing will miss the full season after right elbow surgery.
Currently Out
Infielder Miguel Andujar is on the 10-day injured list with a right wrist fracture, expected back in late August. Utility man Samad Taylor is on the 10-day list with a right oblique strain, and reliever Jason Adam is on the 15-day list with a right shoulder strain, targeting early September.
Arizona Diamondbacks
Rotation
Ace Zac Gallen has been on the 15-day injured list since July 12 with right elbow inflammation and does not have a confirmed return date, which is a direct reason a three-start rookie is on the mound tonight. Michael Soroka has also been sidelined with a left glute strain, working back through simulated games and bullpen sessions.
Bullpen
Justin Martinez remains on the injured list recovering from Tommy John surgery and is not expected back until the second half of the year, thinning a relief corps that may be needed early if Drake's pitch count runs short.

The Pitching Match-Up

Walker Buehler, San Diego

Buehler is 6-5 with a 5.18 ERA, a 1.45 WHIP and 92 strikeouts against 44 walks in 104.1 innings across 22 starts. The walk total is the number that stands out, 3.79 per nine, and it has shown up in his results lately: two innings and four earned against Toronto on July 11, five and two-thirds and two earned against Atlanta on July 21, five and a third and two earned against Miami on July 26, and four and a third with three earned against San Francisco on August 1. He has not finished six innings in any of those four turns.

Kohl Drake, Arizona

Drake is a true rookie, three career starts and 13 total innings before tonight. The early returns are mixed: five innings and one earned run against the Athletics on July 21, four innings and four earned against Washington on July 26, and four innings with two earned against Cleveland on August 1. A 4.85 ERA and a 1.46 WHIP across that sample, with 12 strikeouts and five walks, is a small enough body of work that neither the strong start nor the shaky one should be read as the real version yet. What is certain is the workload: Arizona has not let him past five innings in any outing, so the bullpen enters early regardless of how he pitches.

The team numbers are close to a mirror image of each other, 4.21 to 4.20 in staff ERA and both offenses hovering around league average, which is a large part of why the market treats this as close to a coin flip. Arizona has scored 41 more runs on the year despite fewer plate appearances at home, and the Diamondbacks are 33-23 at Chase Field, a venue that has historically played as one of the more hitter-friendly parks in the league.

Neither bullpen is deep right now. Arizona is without Justin Martinez and still working Michael Soroka back, while San Diego's relief group has already absorbed the loss of Jason Adam. With both starters unlikely to see the seventh inning, tonight is as much a bullpen game as a starting pitcher's duel.

Keys To The Game

Padres Keys
Buehler finding the strike zone early. He has walked at least two batters in each of his last four starts and has not completed six innings in any of them. A short outing hands the game to a bullpen replacing Jason Adam.
Answering fast. San Diego's one win this week came in the game it scored nine runs. Against a rookie making his fourth start, an early lead changes how aggressively Arizona can use its thin bullpen.
Diamondbacks Keys
Getting length from Drake. He has not pitched into a sixth inning in his career. Every extra out he gets is an out Arizona's bullpen does not have to cover with Justin Martinez still out.
Keeping the week's pattern going. Arizona has outscored San Diego 15-9 across the two games it has won this series. The Diamondbacks have hit Padres pitching hard when they have gotten to it at all.

Market Context

Arizona sits as a modest home favorite at roughly -112 with the total posted at 9, one of the higher lines on the board tonight and a fair reflection of two shaky rotations and two thin bullpens. Betting splits on this game show a notable gap: roughly two-thirds of tickets have come in on the under, while a large majority of the actual dollar volume has landed on the over, the kind of ticket-versus-handle split that shows up when bigger individual bets skew toward one side of a number the public reads differently.

The number has reason behind it beyond the handle. Buehler has not finished six innings in a month, Drake has never pitched past five, and both bullpens are down key relievers. A high total is what a book posts when neither start feels like a lock to hold a line.

Final Thoughts

Both rotations are missing the pitcher each team actually built its season around, Darvish for San Diego and Gallen for Arizona, and what is left is a 5.18 ERA against a rookie with three career starts. That is an honest way to describe a coin-flip game, and the number on the board agrees.

Arizona's edge is recent and specific: two wins by a combined ten runs already this week, a better home record, and a bullpen that has been able to protect leads more often than San Diego's has. San Diego's counter is just as real: a 7-3 stretch over its last ten games and a lineup that has already shown it can hang nine runs on this same pitching staff in this same series.

Whichever bullpen gets summoned first tonight will probably matter more than either name currently starting the game.
