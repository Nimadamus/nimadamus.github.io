The Leagues Cup Sends Its Last Fifty-Fourth Game Through Portland

Phase One of the 2026 Leagues Cup closes tonight: Santos Laguna and their zero Apertura points visit last-place-shocked Philadelphia, Club America tops the young Liga MX table and still plays a home game inside Austin's own stadium, Cruz Azul meets the Chicago Fire in Bridgeview, and Tijuana at Portland is the phase's official final match. Two CONMEBOL round-of-16 first legs follow, with Corinthians at Rosario Central carrying the lowest total on the board. Analysis only, no picks.

Leagues Cup Phase One - Two Seasons Gone Wrong

Santos Laguna at Philadelphia Union

Thursday, 7:00 PM ET | Subaru Park, Chester, PA

Both of these clubs need the Leagues Cup to mean something because their league seasons have not. Philadelphia sits thirteenth in the Eastern Conference at 4-4-10 with 16 points from 18 matches, a collapse from a club that spent recent years as a model MLS operation. Santos Laguna have been worse relative to their table: zero points from three Apertura matches, three straight defeats, seventeenth in Liga MX.

The Leagues Cup format gives both a reset. Phase One is a straight MLS-against-Liga MX round robin, 54 games from August 4 through tonight, with three points for a regulation win and a penalty shootout deciding two points against one whenever ninety minutes ends level. Eight knockout places come out of the league phase, so every point tonight has real weight for teams still mathematically alive.

Subaru Park has been the one place Philadelphia's season has held any shape, and a struggling Liga MX side arriving on the East Coast for a 7:00 PM kickoff is the kind of matchup the format was designed to hand the home league. Santos' three Apertura losses mean the club has yet to take a single competitive point since the summer began, and a road trip to Chester is a hard place to start.

Leagues Cup Phase One - A Home Game Across The River

Necaxa at New York City FC

Thursday, 7:30 PM ET | Red Bull Arena, Harrison, NJ

New York City FC play a home Leagues Cup match at Red Bull Arena, their rival's building across the Hudson, which is its own commentary on the club's long-running stadium puzzle. The venue quirk aside, NYCFC arrive in decent form by their season's standard: fifth in the Eastern Conference at 7-5-6 with 26 points from 18 matches, comfortably clear of the conference's bottom half.

Necaxa have opened their Apertura at 2-0-1 with six points from three matches, and the trip north is a chance to bank Leagues Cup points against an MLS side that has spent the season grinding out narrow results rather than overwhelming anyone. The market leans to the home club at -145 on the three-way line with the total at 3.5, via DraftKings, the highest total of tonight's five Leagues Cup fixtures alongside Portland's.

That total tells you what the books think of both defenses. A 3.5 in a cup tie between a Liga MX side that has already won twice this term and an MLS club that has drawn five of eighteen is an invitation to goals, and the shootout rule hanging over any draw changes late-game incentives in a way league matches never see: there is always a reason to push for the regulation winner and the full three points.

Leagues Cup Phase One - The Away Team In Its Own Stadium

Austin FC at Club America

Thursday, 8:30 PM ET | Q2 Stadium, Austin, TX

The scheduling oddity of the night: Club America are the designated home team, and the match is played at Q2 Stadium, Austin's own building. The Leagues Cup keeps most of its games in the United States, so America "host" in front of what will likely be a heavily green crowd anyway, given how the club travels. Austin FC get the strangest road game in their history without leaving town.

America arrive as the form side of the entire board. They sit top of the young Liga MX Apertura table at 2-1-0 with seven points from three matches, and the market respects it: America are -105 on the three-way line even as the nominal traveling club, with the total at 2.5, via DraftKings. Austin's MLS season explains the rest of that price. The club is fourteenth in the Western Conference at 4-5-9, 17 points from 18 matches, and has spent the year unable to score its way out of trouble.

For Austin the Leagues Cup is the season's remaining storyline, and a home crowd against one of the biggest brands in North American soccer is the kind of night Q2 Stadium was built for. The tension is structural: a favored giant playing a road game labeled home, against a struggling MLS side with the actual home fans, on the last night of the phase when the point math is naked for everyone.

Leagues Cup Phase One - Bridgeview Hosts A Mexico City Giant

Chicago Fire at Cruz Azul

Thursday, 9:00 PM ET | SeatGeek Stadium, Bridgeview, IL

Another nominal Liga MX home game on MLS soil: Cruz Azul are the listed hosts at SeatGeek Stadium in Bridgeview, the Fire's former home ground in the Chicago suburbs. The market makes the geography plain, listing Cruz Azul at +145 on the three-way line with the total at 2.5, via DraftKings, an underdog price for a designated home team.

The Fire have earned that respect. Chicago sit fourth in the Eastern Conference at 9-2-6 with 29 points from 17 matches, one of the stronger MLS campaigns on tonight's board, built on a team that simply does not draw: only two stalemates in seventeen league games. Cruz Azul's Apertura start reads 2-0-1 with six points, a solid opening with one stumble.

Stylistically this is the most even of the five Leagues Cup fixtures, two clubs in real form meeting at a 2.5 total that suggests a tight, low-event match. The phase-ending stakes sharpen it: with eight knockout spots coming out of the 36-club league phase, a regulation win tonight is worth exactly what it says, and a draw drags both teams into the shootout for the extra point.

Marquee Matchup - The Official Phase One Closer

Tijuana at Portland Timbers

Thursday, 10:30 PM ET | Providence Park, Portland, OR

The Leagues Cup scheduled this one as the official curtain on Phase One, the last of the 54 league-phase games, and Providence Park is the right theater for it. Portland are 7-3-8 in the Western Conference, eighth place with 24 points, a middling league season attached to one of the sport's best home atmospheres. The market makes the Timbers a modest -115 favorite on the three-way line with the total at 3.5, via DraftKings.

Tijuana are the surprise of the young Liga MX season. The border club sits second in the Apertura at 2-1-0 with seven points, level on points with America at the top, and arrives at the one Leagues Cup venue where the road team's geography almost works in its favor, a two-hour flight up the coast rather than a cross-continent haul.

A 3.5 total on a phase-closing match between an inconsistent MLS side and a fast-starting Liga MX club is the board's most honest invitation to entertainment. Portland's season has alternated wins and losses in blocks all year, eight losses against seven wins, and Xolos have not yet trailed in league play deep enough to show what their response looks like. Whoever handles the occasion better closes the phase with three points and a live claim on one of the eight knockout places.

Copa Sudamericana Round of 16, First Leg - At Altitude

Botafogo at Cienciano

Thursday, 8:30 PM ET | Estadio Inca Garcilaso de la Vega, Cusco, Peru

The Copa Sudamericana round of 16 opens with one of South American soccer's oldest equalizers: altitude. Cienciano host Botafogo in Cusco, high in the Peruvian Andes, where visiting Brazilian sides have spent decades gasping through first legs. Even so, the market refuses to make the home team the favorite, listing Cienciano at +155 on the three-way line with the total at 2.5, via DraftKings.

That price is about the gap in squad quality. Botafogo sit ninth in the Brazilian Serie A at 8-6-7 with 30 points from 21 matches, a mid-table season by the standard of a club that has spent recent years competing at the top of the continent, but still a level of roster Cienciano cannot match line for line. The Peruvian side's edge is entirely circumstantial, and history says that edge is real for one night.

The two-leg format shapes the tactics. Botafogo can lose this match narrowly and remain in control of the tie back in Rio, which is exactly how Brazilian clubs have traditionally played Cusco: keep the damage to a goal, shorten the match, and settle it at sea level. Cienciano's task is the opposite, to turn the thin air into a scoreline big enough to survive the second leg.

Copa Libertadores Round of 16, First Leg - The Lowest Total On The Board

Corinthians at Rosario Central

Thursday, 8:30 PM ET | Gigante de Arroyito, Rosario, Argentina

The night's heaviest fixture is in Rosario, where Rosario Central open a Copa Libertadores round-of-16 tie against Corinthians at the Gigante de Arroyito. The market has hung a total of 1.5 on it, the lowest number on tonight's entire board in any sport, with the home side at +130 on the three-way line, via DraftKings. A 1.5 goal total in a Libertadores knockout first leg is the market saying, plainly, that nobody intends to concede first.

Corinthians arrive seventh in the Brazilian Serie A at 8-8-6 with 32 points from 22 matches, eight draws telling the story of a side that is hard to beat and hard to watch in equal measure. Rosario Central's domestic form in the Clausura reads 2-1-1 through four matches in their group, and the Argentine knockouts-first culture around this competition needs no introduction: the Gigante on a Libertadores night is one of the most hostile road trips in the hemisphere.

First legs like this are chess. Corinthians' incentive is simply to keep the tie level heading back to Sao Paulo, while Central know a single goal tonight might be the entire margin of the round. Expect the match the total predicts: narrow, physical, and decided by one moment if it is decided at all.
