NFL | Sunday, October 4

Lions vs Panthers on Sunday Night Football: Jahmyr Gibbs Gets A Run Defense Allowing 193 Yards A Game, And Bryce Young Gets A Lions Secondary Without Brian Branch

Detroit Lions at Carolina Panthers, Week 4 Sunday Night Football, Featured Game of the Day
Published October 4, 2026

Three weeks in, Detroit and Carolina look like mirror images with the strengths swapped. The Lions run the ball well and can't stop the pass, giving up 326.3 passing yards a game. The Panthers throw it all over the field and can't stop the run, giving up 193.3 rushing yards a game. That's the whole story of Sunday night in Charlotte, and the betting market has noticed: the total opened 45.5 at DraftKings and it's 51.5 now.

Matchup Context

Detroit is 2 and 1, and every one of its games has turned into a shootout. The Lions beat New Orleans 31 to 30 at Ford Field in Week 1, lost 41 to 31 in Buffalo in Week 2, and beat the Jets 31 to 24 at home last Sunday. That's 31 points in all three games, and 95 points allowed across the three. Their games have produced 61, 72 and 55 total points.

Carolina is 1 and 2 and has been all over the map. The Panthers opened by losing 59 to 37 at home to Chicago, then went to Atlanta and won 34 to 3, then lost 21 to 18 in Cleveland. So the same team has played a 96 point game and two games in the 30s. Sunday night is only Carolina's second home game, and the first one ended with Chicago scoring 59.

This is Detroit's second road game. The first was the loss in Buffalo, where the defense gave up 41. The Lions still have the cleaner résumé: Jared Goff hasn't thrown an interception, Detroit has scored on every red zone trip, and ESPN's matchup predictor makes the Lions a 61 percent favorite.

The injury that matters most sits in the Detroit secondary. With Brian Branch out and Thomas Harper on injured reserve, the Lions are down two safeties against a passing offense averaging 313 yards a game, and cornerback D.J. Reed, the team's leading tackler, is questionable with a rib injury. On the other side, Carolina's two questionable defensive tackles, Cam Jackson and Lee Hunter, are the exact bodies a defense allowing 193.3 rushing yards a game can least afford to lose. And Jalen Coker, Carolina's leading receiver with 222 yards and 2 touchdowns, is questionable with a quad injury. His status could be the biggest single swing in this game.

Both offensive lines are patched too. Detroit is missing guard Ben Bartch and center Cade Mays; Carolina is without guard Damien Lewis.

Strength Against Weakness, Both Ways

When Detroit has the ball

Goff is 77 of 109 for 802 yards, 8 touchdowns and no interceptions. That's a 70.6 percent completion rate and a 116.1 passer rating. Amon Ra St. Brown has 23 catches for 228 yards and 5 touchdowns. But the Carolina matchup points somewhere else. The Panthers allow only 186 passing yards a game, and they give up 193.3 on the ground. That's where Jahmyr Gibbs comes in. He has 307 yards and 4 touchdowns on 65 carries, about 4.7 a carry, and Detroit runs for 120.7 yards a game as a team at 4.4 a carry. If the Lions lean on Gibbs early, they also protect a defense that needs rest, and they already hold the ball longer, about 31 and a half minutes a game.

When Carolina has the ball

Bryce Young is 72 of 121 for 939 yards, 7 touchdowns and 2 interceptions, and Carolina throws for 313 yards a game at 7.7 yards an attempt. Young's 59.5 percent completion rate says it hasn't been efficient so much as explosive. Now he gets a Lions defense allowing 326.3 passing yards and 443.7 total yards a game, missing Branch and maybe Reed. The counter is Aidan Hutchinson, who has 3.5 of Detroit's 12 sacks. Carolina has allowed 7 sacks, and its third down rate is only 36.8 percent. If Hutchinson wins early, the Panthers' passing game turns into the stop and start version that lost in Cleveland. Chuba Hubbard, with 184 yards on 41 carries, gives Carolina a way to stay ahead of the chains.

Detroit has given the ball away once in three games. Carolina has given it away four times, but it has also taken it away seven times, and the Panthers held Atlanta to 3 points in Week 2. Both defenses are giving up chunks: Detroit through the air, Carolina on the ground.

Keys To The Game

Lions Keys
Feed Gibbs. A defense giving up 193.3 rushing yards a game is a soft matchup for a back averaging about 4.7 a carry.
Hutchinson in the backfield. His 3.5 sacks are the Lions' best answer to a passing game averaging 7.7 yards an attempt.
Keep the clean sheet. No interceptions from Goff in three games. Carolina has 3 picks and 7 takeaways.
Panthers Keys
Attack the safeties. With Branch out and Harper on injured reserve, the middle of the Detroit secondary is the softest spot on the field.
Coker's status. 222 yards and 2 touchdowns, and with Xavier Legette on injured reserve there isn't another receiver with that production.
Win third down. 36.8 percent won't keep Goff on the sideline, and Detroit converts 41.2 percent of its own.

Market Context

The biggest move is on the total. DraftKings opened it at 45.5 and it's 51.5 now, a six point climb, with the over at -102 and the under at -118. That's the market pricing in what the yardage numbers say: two defenses that have each allowed 27 or more points a game. Detroit's three games have averaged 62.7 total points, and Carolina's have averaged 57.3, though that number leans hard on the 96 point opener.

The side has moved toward Detroit as well. The Lions opened -3 and -162, and they're -3.5 and -192 now, with the -3.5 priced at -115. At -192 the moneyline implies about a 65.8 percent chance, a few points higher than ESPN's 61 percent predictor. ESPN's forecast for kickoff in Charlotte is 66 degrees with a 60 percent chance of rain and light wind.

Final Thoughts

This is a game where each team's best unit meets the other's worst. If Gibbs runs the way the numbers suggest, Detroit can control the clock and keep Young on the sideline. If Young finds the holes in a secondary missing Branch, Carolina can keep pace the way it did in a 37 point night against Chicago. The market has already decided it expects points and a Detroit edge. The questions left before kickoff are Coker's quad and Reed's ribs, and the inactive list about 90 minutes before the 5:20 PM Pacific kickoff will settle both.

Yankees vs Rays ALDS Game 1: Cole And Rasmussen Run It Back, And Aaron Judge Is On The Injured List

New York Yankees at Tampa Bay Rays, American League Division Series Game 1, Featured Game of the Day
Published October 3, 2026

Less than two weeks ago these two starters faced these two lineups in the Bronx and both of them won. Drew Rasmussen struck out 11 Yankees over 6.2 innings on September 22, and Gerrit Cole struck out 8 Rays the next night. Now they open a division series at Tropicana Field, with the 98 win Rays a modest home favorite and the Yankees arriving off a two game Wild Card sweep of Boston that they pulled off without Aaron Judge.

Matchup Context

Tampa Bay won the American League East at 98 and 64, four and a half games clear of New York, and it did a lot of that damage at home. The Rays went 55 and 26 at Tropicana Field. The Yankees finished 93 and 68 and were actually better away from home than in the Bronx, 47 and 34 on the road against 46 and 34 at Yankee Stadium.

Here's the part the standings hide. New York finished with a run differential of +138. Tampa Bay finished at +86. The Yankees scored 739 runs and allowed 601; the Rays scored 736 and allowed 650. So the two offenses produced almost the same number of runs over a full season, and the gap was on the run prevention side, in New York's favor.

The two teams also limped into the final week the same way, 5 and 5 over their last ten regular season games. What happened after that is different. The Rays have not played since their regular season finale on September 27, a loss in Philadelphia. The Yankees beat Boston 9 to 0 and 9 to 2 at Yankee Stadium on September 29 and 30, with Cam Schlittler and Max Fried starting those games, which is why Cole lines up for Game 1 on full rest.

The season series could not have been much tighter. Tampa Bay won it 7 games to 6, but New York outscored the Rays 54 to 45 across those 13 games. The Rays took 5 of 7 at Tropicana Field, including a three game sweep in April by scores of 5 to 3, 5 to 4 and 5 to 4. New York took 4 of 6 in the Bronx, including 9 to 2 and 6 to 4 in the last two meetings on September 23 and 24.

Judge is the headline, but look at what the Yankees did without him. Two Wild Card games, 18 runs, and a lineup built around Ben Rice, Cody Bellinger, Luis García Jr. and Paul Goldschmidt. Judge only logged 285 plate appearances this season, with an .871 OPS and 18 home runs, so New York has spent a lot of 2026 playing without him already. Rice has been the engine: 41 home runs, 97 RBI and an .897 OPS over 667 plate appearances.

For Tampa Bay, McClanahan's status matters more for the shape of the series than for tonight. He beat the Yankees on July 8, and losing him thins the rotation behind Rasmussen. Jax being day to day with a back issue is the one to watch for Game 1 itself, since it could narrow Tampa Bay's pitching options.

Cole vs Rasmussen, Round Three

Drew Rasmussen

Rasmussen had the better season by just about every measure. He went 16 and 5 over 30 starts with a 2.67 ERA, a 0.91 WHIP and 174 strikeouts against 32 walks in 172 innings. He allowed only 14 home runs, and opponents hit .199 against him. Using MLB's 2026 league totals, his FIP works out to 2.81, so the ERA isn't a mirage built on luck with balls in play. He was slightly better on the road (2.47) than at home (2.87), but both are excellent.

He's also on a run. Over his last six starts he allowed 6 earned runs in 37.2 innings and won all six. The last of those was the gem in the Bronx, 6.2 innings, 1 hit, 1 run and 11 strikeouts. Against the Yankees this season he made four starts and gave up 7 earned runs in 22 innings. Three of those starts were dominant, and one was a disaster: on July 9 New York knocked him out after 2.1 innings with 6 earned runs on 7 hits in a 12 to 4 win. That's the version of this game the Yankees are hoping for.

Gerrit Cole

Cole didn't make his first start until May 22, and he finished with 22 starts, a 10 and 9 record, a 3.61 ERA and a 1.10 WHIP over 127 innings. The strikeout and walk numbers are still elite, 131 strikeouts against 25 walks, but he gave up 22 home runs, and that pushes his FIP up to 3.92. Interestingly, he was better on the road (3.41 ERA) than at home (3.86).

Against the Rays, though, he has been very good. Three starts, 18 innings, 5 earned runs, 16 strikeouts and 1 home run allowed. He threw six scoreless innings against them at Yankee Stadium in his season debut on May 22, took a 3 to 0 loss at Tropicana Field on July 8 when the Yankees didn't score for him, and then beat the Rays on September 23 with 5.2 innings, 2 runs and 8 strikeouts. His recent form is less tidy than Rasmussen's: across his last six starts he went 34.1 innings and allowed 19 earned runs, including 5 in 4.1 innings against the Mets on September 12.

So the starting matchup is a strikeout pitcher with a home run problem against a strikeout pitcher who barely gives up the long ball. The Yankees hit 223 home runs this season, 54 more than the Rays. If Rasmussen keeps the ball in the yard the way he has all year, New York has to manufacture runs against a lineup that is built to do exactly that.

These are two offenses that got to the same place by very different roads. Tampa Bay hit .258 as a team and struck out only 1,145 times, 335 fewer than New York. The Yankees hit .235 but slugged their way to almost the same OPS, .720 to .728, on the back of 223 home runs. One team puts the ball in play and runs (the Rays stole 164 bases), the other waits for a mistake it can hit over the wall.

On the run prevention side, the Yankees' staff had the lower ERA, 3.23 to 3.70, and allowed 36 fewer home runs. The Rays' staff walked fewer hitters (409 to 488) and had the better WHIP. The bullpens tell a split story too. Tampa Bay converted 58 saves and blew only 19. New York saved 45 and blew 28, which is a number worth keeping in mind if Game 1 is close in the eighth inning.

The lineup leaders line up with those profiles. Junior Caminero led the Rays with 42 home runs, 101 RBI and an .884 OPS. Yandy Díaz hit .293, Liam Hicks .284 with a .367 on base percentage, and Chandler Simpson hit .306 with 46 steals. For New York, Rice, García Jr. (30 home runs, .830 OPS) and Bellinger (.767 OPS) carry the lineup with Judge out. Team wRC+ wasn't available from a source we could verify this morning, so it's left out rather than guessed.

Keys To The Game

Yankees Keys
Find the mistake pitch. Rasmussen allowed 14 home runs in 172 innings. The Yankees hit 223. The one start where they got to him, July 9, they scored 12.
Cole keeps it in the park. His 22 home runs allowed are the only real blemish on his line, and his three starts against the Rays included just one.
Win the late innings. A bullpen with 28 blown saves has to hold a lead in a road playoff game.
Rays Keys
Put the ball in play. Tampa Bay struck out 335 fewer times than New York. Against a pitcher who fanned 131 in 127 innings, contact and speed are the path.
Rasmussen goes deep. He averaged under 6 innings a start but went 6 or more in five of his last six. Every out he gets keeps a short bench of relievers fresh.
Lean on the dome. 55 and 26 at Tropicana Field, and 5 and 2 at home against New York this season.

Market Context

DraftKings, as shown in ESPN's odds feed at 1:58 AM Pacific, has Tampa Bay -137 and New York +113. The moneyline opened at Rays -133 and Yankees +110, so the overnight move has been small and toward the home side, less than one point of implied probability. At -137 the Rays are implied at 57.8 percent before the vig comes out.

The run line followed it by a hair. Rays -1.5 opened at +167 and sits at +163, with the Yankees +1.5 at -199. The total opened at 7 with -110 on both sides and is still 7, now with the under at -113 and the over at -107. A total of 7 with two starters carrying 9 plus strikeouts per nine innings is the market saying it expects the starters to control this one, and the slight shade to the under fits Rasmussen's season. These numbers are early morning prices, and playoff lines tend to move as lineups are posted, so check them again before first pitch.

Final Thoughts

On paper, Tampa Bay has the better starter tonight, the better home record and the more reliable bullpen, and the price reflects that. New York has the better run differential, the deeper power and a lineup coming off two lopsided Wild Card wins, even without Judge. The Rays, for their part, have had five days off since their last game.

The cleanest way to read it: if Rasmussen pitches like he did on September 22, the Rays' contact offense only needs a few runs. If he pitches like he did on July 9, the Yankees have already shown they can turn that into a crooked number in a hurry. Cole's job is simpler to describe and harder to do: limit the home run, and keep a pesky lineup from stringing singles together.

Matchup Context

Nobody has played a game yet, so everything we know about this matchup comes from last season and the offseason roster moves. Last season was not close. Dallas finished with 112 points, 50 wins, 20 regulation losses and 12 overtime losses, and a goal differential of +53. St. Louis finished with 86 points on 37 wins, 33 regulation losses and 12 overtime losses, and it was outscored by 27 over 82 games.

The Stars were also a very good home team. They went 26, 11 and 4 at American Airlines Center, while the Blues went 17, 19 and 5 on the road. Both clubs finished the season playing well, Dallas 7, 2 and 1 over its last ten and St. Louis 6, 3 and 1, so neither one limped into the summer.

The head to head leaned Dallas too. The Stars took 3 of 4 meetings, all four settled in regulation. St. Louis won the first one 3 to 1 at home on October 18, 2025. Dallas then won 3 to 2 at home in January, 4 to 3 in St. Louis four days later, and 5 to 4 at home on February 4. The last three were all one goal games, which is worth remembering before you assume the standings gap shows up on the scoreboard.

It's a Central Division game on the first night of the season, on national television. Both teams get a clean slate and an immediate measuring stick.

On the St. Louis side the bigger story is roster turnover rather than injuries: McMichael and McTavish combined for 87 points elsewhere last season and both arrive as new faces against a team they haven't faced in a Blues sweater.

Oettinger, Hofer And The Special Teams Gap

The goaltending

Here's the part of the matchup that runs against the standings. Joel Hofer was the better goalie on a per shot basis last season. He started 43 games, went 24 and 13, and stopped .910 of the shots he faced with a 2.61 goals against average and 6 shutouts. Jake Oettinger carried a bigger load, 54 starts and 35 wins against 12 losses, but his save percentage was .899 with a 2.59 goals against average and 4 shutouts.

Oettinger's goals against average was slightly lower because the team in front of him allowed fewer shots. Dallas gave up 26.2 shots a game; St. Louis gave up 27.7. Strip that out and Hofer had the better individual season, which is the main reason a team that finished 26 points behind can win a game like this.

The power play against the kill

This is where Dallas separated itself. The Stars' power play converted 28.6 percent of the time last season, and Robertson, Johnston and Rantanen each had at least 34 power play points. The Blues killed 76.7 percent of their penalties. Flip it around and St. Louis converted 17.6 percent with the extra man, against a Dallas kill that worked 80.3 percent of the time.

At five on five the two teams looked much more alike than the standings suggest. Both averaged 25.3 shots a game. Dallas won 51.6 percent of its faceoffs and St. Louis 49.4. The difference in goals, 3.33 a game for Dallas against 2.80 for St. Louis, leans heavily on what happened on special teams.

Keys To The Game

Blues Keys
Stay out of the box. A 76.7 percent kill against a 28.6 percent power play is the worst matchup on the board for St. Louis. Five on five, these teams were even on shots.
Let Hofer steal it. His .910 last season was better than Oettinger's .899. A one goal game is the Blues' path, and three of last season's four meetings were one goal games.
Get the new forwards going. McMichael and McTavish add 87 points of last season's production to a lineup that needed scoring behind Thomas.
Stars Keys
Draw penalties. Robertson, Johnston and Rantanen each had at least 34 power play points. More power plays means more of Dallas's biggest advantage.
Cover for Duchene. A 45 point center is out, so the depth lines have to hold their own against a Blues lineup with new faces.
Keep the home edge. 26, 11 and 4 at American Airlines Center last season, and both of last season's meetings in Dallas went to the Stars.

Market Context

DraftKings, as shown in ESPN's odds feed at 9:16 AM Pacific, has Dallas -185 and St. Louis +154. Those numbers opened at Dallas -205 and St. Louis +170, so the price has moved toward the Blues by about two points of implied probability. At -185 the Stars are implied at 64.9 percent before the vig comes out, and the Blues at 39.4 percent.

The puck line moved the same way. Dallas -1.5 opened at +120 and now sits at +145, with St. Louis +1.5 at -175. The total is 5.5, but the over has been bet from -120 to -135 with the under now +114. So the market is leaning toward a little more scoring and a slightly closer game than the opening numbers said, which lines up with Hofer's season and the turnover in the Blues forward group.

Final Thoughts

On last season's evidence Dallas is the better team in nearly every way that matters: points, goal differential, home record, scoring depth and above all special teams. The one place St. Louis had the edge was in goal, and a goaltender is the one player who can flatten a talent gap for a single night.

So the question is simple. If the Blues stay disciplined and keep the Stars' power play on the bench, this looks a lot like the last three meetings, one goal either way. If Dallas gets four or five power plays, last season's numbers say that's where the game gets decided.
