Buccaneers vs Cowboys: Baker Mayfield Is Out, The Line Has Tripled, And Tampa Bay Still Has Never Lost By More Than Seven

Tampa Bay Buccaneers at Dallas Cowboys, Week 5 Thursday Night Football, Featured Game of the Day
Published October 8, 2026

Tampa Bay comes to Arlington at 0 and 4 with its starting quarterback out with a dislocated thumb, and the market has reacted about as hard as a market can. Dallas opened 3.5 point favorites at DraftKings and sits at 9.5 now. The Cowboys have one of the best offenses in football and one of the worst defenses. The Bucs have one of the worst offenses and one of the best defenses. That's the whole game, and the numbers underneath it are a lot more interesting than the records.

Matchup Context

Start with the record, because it lies a little. Tampa Bay is 0 and 4, last in the NFC South behind Carolina and Atlanta at 2 and 2. But look at how they lost. Cincinnati beat them 33 to 27. Cleveland beat them 23 to 19. Minnesota beat them 23 to 16. Green Bay beat them 17 to 14 on Sunday. That's losses by 6, 4, 7 and 3. They've scored 76 points and allowed 96, a minus 20 differential spread over four one score games.

Dallas is 2 and 2 and has been a coin flip in a very different way. The Cowboys lost 28 to 20 at the Giants, blew out Washington 37 to 20, lost a 34 to 31 shootout to Baltimore and then won 34 to 30 in Houston on Sunday. They've scored 122 and allowed 112. Every Dallas game except the opener has had at least 57 total points. The Giants are 3 and 1 on top of the NFC East, so a home win keeps Dallas in the chase and a loss to a winless team would be a real problem.

So you've got a team whose games are low scoring and close against a team whose games are high scoring and wild. One of those identities is going to win out on a short week.

Mayfield is the headline and the reason the line moved six points, but don't skip the Tampa Bay secondary. With Winfield and Morrison both out, the Bucs are asking a thinned out back end to handle Lamb, who already has 37 catches for 498 yards and 4 touchdowns. On the Dallas side, losing Overshown hurts the one thing Tampa Bay might actually want to do, which is run the ball with Bucky Irving and keep Daniels in short yardage.

Jalon Daniels, Dak Prescott And Two Lopsided Units

Tampa Bay with the ball

Here's the uncomfortable part for Bucs backers: the offense wasn't working with Mayfield either. He was 61 of 96 for 615 yards with 2 touchdowns and 2 interceptions, and his dropbacks averaged minus 0.297 EPA. Tampa Bay has been sacked 16 times in four games and has 9 giveaways. The team averages 4.9 net yards per pass attempt.

Daniels has already seen real time. Against Green Bay on Sunday he went 19 of 30 for 148 yards with 1 touchdown and 2 interceptions, and he was sacked 3 times. His dropbacks across the season have averaged minus 0.145 EPA, which is bad, but it's actually less bad than the starter's number. The steadier piece is Irving, who has 241 yards on 56 carries, and a run game at 4.4 yards a carry.

Dallas with the ball

Dak Prescott is 105 of 150 for 1,065 yards with 8 touchdowns and 1 interception, and Dallas dropbacks are averaging plus 0.323 EPA. Lamb is the engine, and Javonte Williams has turned 62 carries into 231 yards and 5 touchdowns. The Cowboys convert 51.0 percent of third downs and have scored touchdowns on 81.25 percent of red zone trips.

But this is the matchup that keeps a 0 and 4 team inside the number in four straight weeks. Tampa Bay's defense allows just 292.8 yards a game, and its run defense has been elite: opponents average minus 0.253 EPA on runs and 78.5 rushing yards a game. If Dallas wants to win big, it'll have to do it through the air against a secondary missing two starters.

This is about as mirrored as an efficiency matchup gets. Dallas has the 2nd best offense by EPA per play and the 31st ranked defense. Tampa Bay has the 32nd ranked offense and the 5th best defense. Opponents are averaging plus 0.412 EPA when they throw against Dallas, which is the kind of number that usually belongs to a team giving up 30 a week. The Cowboys have also forced only 2 turnovers in four games.

On the other side, the Bucs' defensive numbers are why their losses have been so tight. They hold opponents to a 40.4 percent success rate, and nobody has run on them. The problem is everything the offense gives back: 16 sacks, 9 giveaways, a 34.5 percent third down rate and a red zone touchdown rate of 41.67 percent. Tampa Bay keeps getting into scoring range and kicking field goals.

Keys To The Game

Buccaneers Keys
Protect Daniels. 16 sacks allowed in four games, and Daniels took 3 on 33 dropbacks Sunday.
Lean on Irving. Dallas allows 150.3 rushing yards a game and is without Overshown. Shorter third downs mean fewer chances for turnovers.
Finish drives. A 41.67 percent red zone touchdown rate turned four winnable games into losses.
Cowboys Keys
Throw it to Lamb. Dallas passes are at plus 0.323 EPA, runs are at minus 0.058, and Tampa Bay is missing Winfield and Morrison.
Make Daniels beat them. A backup who threw 2 interceptions Sunday is the best chance for a defense with 2 takeaways all year to change that.
Avoid the shootout script. Three straight Dallas games have gone over 50 points. A loose game keeps the dog alive.

Market Context

This is a huge move for a regular season line. DraftKings, as shown in ESPN's odds feed, opened Dallas -3.5 at -110 on both sides. At 3:39 PM PT it's Dallas -9.5 at -108 and Tampa Bay +9.5 at -112. That's a six point move, straight through the key numbers of 4, 6 and 7, and almost all of it is the Mayfield news.

The moneyline tells the same story. Dallas opened at -205 and Tampa Bay at +170. Now it's -520 and +390, which implies 83.9 percent for the Cowboys before the vig comes out. ESPN's matchup predictor is even more bullish at 86.1 percent for Dallas. Bovada at the same minute is slightly off the DraftKings number at Dallas -9.5 (-105), Tampa Bay +9.5 (-115) and a moneyline of -480 and +350.

The total went the other way. DraftKings opened at 52.5 and is down to 48.5, with the over at -118 and the under at -102, while Bovada sits at 49. The market is betting that a backup quarterback drags the scoring down, even though three of Dallas's four games have cleared 50.

Final Thoughts

The case for Dallas is easy to make. Elite offense, home field, and a winless opponent starting a backup with a short week to prepare. If Prescott and Lamb do to this secondary what they've done to everybody else, the Cowboys can win this one going away.

The case for Tampa Bay is in the efficiency table. A top five defense that has kept all four games within one score, against a Dallas defense that ranks 31st and almost never takes the ball away. The Bucs' offense was already bad with Mayfield, so the question is how much worse it really gets with Daniels. The market says a lot. Thursday night will tell us whether that six point move was a correction or an overcorrection.

Dodgers vs Braves NLDS Game 4: Glasnow Tries To Close It Out Against A Mahle Who Has Been Nearly Untouchable Since Joining Atlanta

Matchup Context

Los Angeles won the National League West at 100 and 62, with the best run differential of any team in this series by a mile, +201. The Dodgers scored 801 runs and allowed 600, they were nearly as good on the road (49 and 32) as at home (51 and 30), and they closed the regular season 8 and 2 in their last ten. They had a bye through the Wild Card round.

Atlanta won the National League East at 94 and 68 with a +116 run differential, 742 scored and 626 allowed. The Braves are a different team at Truist Park, 52 and 29 at home against 42 and 39 on the road. They finished the regular season 5 and 5 over their last ten, then had to grind through a three game Wild Card Series against Philadelphia at home: a 5 to 3 win on September 29, a 4 to 3 loss on September 30, and a 6 to 2 win on October 1 to advance.

The division series has been tight and low scoring. The Dodgers won Game 1 at Dodger Stadium 5 to 3 behind six innings of one run ball from Tarik Skubal. Atlanta stole Game 2 by a 3 to 2 score in Los Angeles, with Dylan Lee getting the win and Raisel Iglesias the save after Blake Snell walked five in 3.2 innings. Then on Tuesday at Truist Park, Yamamoto went seven innings, allowed one run on four hits and struck out 10, and the Dodgers beat Chris Sale 3 to 1. Across three games Los Angeles has scored 10 runs and Atlanta 7.

Here's what makes this matchup awkward for the favorite. Atlanta owned Los Angeles during the regular season, winning the series 5 games to 1 and outscoring the Dodgers 26 to 15. The three games at Truist Park in late August were all one run Braves wins, 4 to 3, 6 to 5 and 1 to 0, and Glasnow started the first of them. If Atlanta wins today, Game 5 is Friday at 5:00 PM Pacific at Dodger Stadium.

Glasnow vs Mahle

Tyler Glasnow

Glasnow's season comes in two pieces. He made seven starts before low back spasms put him on the injured list on May 8, and he was excellent in that stretch, a 2.72 ERA over 39.2 innings. He didn't return until August 25, and his first start back came at Truist Park against this Braves team: 5 innings, 6 hits, 3 earned runs and 7 strikeouts in a 4 to 3 Dodgers loss. Since the return he has made six starts with a 4.46 ERA over 34.1 innings.

The odd thing is that the underlying numbers since the return are still very good. In those six starts he struck out 49 and walked 12, allowed only 25 hits and 2 home runs, and carried a 1.08 WHIP. His last two outings were 10 and 11 strikeout games. For the full season he's at 3.53 ERA, 0.95 WHIP and 98 strikeouts in 74 innings, and opponents hit just .175 against him. Using MLB's 2026 league totals, his FIP works out to 2.73, nearly a run below his ERA. He's been better on the road, a 2.84 ERA, than at home, 4.25. The catch is length: he hasn't reached 100 pitches in any of his six starts since coming back.

Tyler Mahle

Mahle's full season line, 7 and 10 with a 3.99 ERA and 1.23 WHIP over 149 innings, undersells what Atlanta has gotten. San Francisco traded him to the Braves on August 2, and in nine starts since he has a 1.99 ERA and a 0.96 WHIP over 54.1 innings, with 48 strikeouts against only 9 walks. He gave up two earned runs or fewer in eight of those nine starts. The lone blowup came in his final regular season start, 5 earned runs in 5 innings against Cincinnati on September 24.

He bounced right back in the Wild Card Series, going 7 innings with 1 earned run and 6 strikeouts against the Phillies on September 30, although the Braves lost that game 4 to 3. He's had six days off since. His home and road split is extreme, a 2.74 ERA at home and 5.28 on the road, and his only look at the Dodgers this season came while he was still a Giant, 7 innings on 3 hits with no earned runs and 5 strikeouts on April 22. The concern with Mahle is the long ball: 20 home runs allowed in 149 innings, and his FIP of 3.99 matches his ERA almost exactly.

So this is a matchup of a power arm whose results have lagged his stuff since he came back against a strike thrower with a 1.99 ERA since the trade. Glasnow misses more bats. Mahle has been the more reliable run preventer for two months, and he's pitching in the park where he's been at his best.

Final Thoughts

On paper, Los Angeles has the deeper, more patient lineup, the better run differential and the momentum of a series lead built on two dominant starts from Skubal and Yamamoto. Atlanta has the regular season edge in this matchup, a home park where it played at a 104 win pace, and a starter who has allowed 12 earned runs in 54.1 innings since he got there.

The cleanest way to read it: if Glasnow's strikeout numbers since his return finally turn into the results his FIP says he deserves, the Dodgers' lineup only needs a couple of swings to end this. If he gives up the kind of crooked inning that inflated his ERA in September, Mahle has shown since August that he can sit on a small lead, and the series goes back to Los Angeles for a Game 5 on Friday.

Nobody has played a game yet, so everything we know about this matchup comes from last season and the offseason roster moves. Last season was not close. Dallas finished with 112 points, 50 wins, 20 regulation losses and 12 overtime losses, and a goal differential of +53. St. Louis finished with 86 points on 37 wins, 33 regulation losses and 12 overtime losses, and it was outscored by 27 over 82 games.

The Stars were also a very good home team. They went 26, 11 and 4 at American Airlines Center, while the Blues went 17, 19 and 5 on the road. Both clubs finished the season playing well, Dallas 7, 2 and 1 over its last ten and St. Louis 6, 3 and 1, so neither one limped into the summer.

The head to head leaned Dallas too. The Stars took 3 of 4 meetings, all four settled in regulation. St. Louis won the first one 3 to 1 at home on October 18, 2025. Dallas then won 3 to 2 at home in January, 4 to 3 in St. Louis four days later, and 5 to 4 at home on February 4. The last three were all one goal games, which is worth remembering before you assume the standings gap shows up on the scoreboard.

It's a Central Division game on the first night of the season, on national television. Both teams get a clean slate and an immediate measuring stick.

Oettinger, Hofer And The Special Teams Gap

The goaltending

Here's the part of the matchup that runs against the standings. Joel Hofer was the better goalie on a per shot basis last season. He started 43 games, went 24 and 13, and stopped .910 of the shots he faced with a 2.61 goals against average and 6 shutouts. Jake Oettinger carried a bigger load, 54 starts and 35 wins against 12 losses, but his save percentage was .899 with a 2.59 goals against average and 4 shutouts.

Oettinger's goals against average was slightly lower because the team in front of him allowed fewer shots. Dallas gave up 26.2 shots a game; St. Louis gave up 27.7. Strip that out and Hofer had the better individual season, which is the main reason a team that finished 26 points behind can win a game like this.

The power play against the kill

This is where Dallas separated itself. The Stars' power play converted 28.6 percent of the time last season, and Robertson, Johnston and Rantanen each had at least 34 power play points. The Blues killed 76.7 percent of their penalties. Flip it around and St. Louis converted 17.6 percent with the extra man, against a Dallas kill that worked 80.3 percent of the time.

At five on five the two teams looked much more alike than the standings suggest. Both averaged 25.3 shots a game. Dallas won 51.6 percent of its faceoffs and St. Louis 49.4. The difference in goals, 3.33 a game for Dallas against 2.80 for St. Louis, leans heavily on what happened on special teams.

Keys To The Game

Blues Keys
Stay out of the box. A 76.7 percent kill against a 28.6 percent power play is the worst matchup on the board for St. Louis. Five on five, these teams were even on shots.
Let Hofer steal it. His .910 last season was better than Oettinger's .899. A one goal game is the Blues' path, and three of last season's four meetings were one goal games.
Get the new forwards going. McMichael and McTavish add 87 points of last season's production to a lineup that needed scoring behind Thomas.
Stars Keys
Draw penalties. Robertson, Johnston and Rantanen each had at least 34 power play points. More power plays means more of Dallas's biggest advantage.
Cover for Duchene. A 45 point center is out, so the depth lines have to hold their own against a Blues lineup with new faces.
Keep the home edge. 26, 11 and 4 at American Airlines Center last season, and both of last season's meetings in Dallas went to the Stars.

Final Thoughts

On last season's evidence Dallas is the better team in nearly every way that matters: points, goal differential, home record, scoring depth and above all special teams. The one place St. Louis had the edge was in goal, and a goaltender is the one player who can flatten a talent gap for a single night.

So the question is simple. If the Blues stay disciplined and keep the Stars' power play on the bench, this looks a lot like the last three meetings, one goal either way. If Dallas gets four or five power plays, last season's numbers say that's where the game gets decided.
