Soccer - Saturday, August 15

Spain Restarts In Vitoria And Seville While Messi Walks Into The Best Defence In MLS

The Spanish season begins at Mendizorrotza and moves to the Ramon Sanchez-Pizjuan four hours later, the first two fixtures of a 2026-27 LaLiga matchday one that runs until the end of the month. Major League Soccer answers with eleven matches, and the two that matter most sit at the top of each conference: Nashville and their fourteen goals conceded host Inter Miami and Lionel Messi, and LAFC close the night at BMO Stadium. Analysis only, no picks.

The First Whistle Of The Spanish Season

Alaves vs Getafe

Saturday, 1:30 PM ET | Estadio de Mendizorrotza, Vitoria-Gasteiz | ESPN Deportes, ESPN+

Spanish football restarts in Vitoria. Alaves against Getafe is the first fixture of the 2026-27 LaLiga season, a matchday one that stretches across nearly two weeks because of international recovery from the summer, and the two clubs opening it finished thirteen places apart in nothing but points.

Getafe were the better side by the table last season, seventh on 51 points from 15 wins, 6 draws and 17 defeats, and they got there in the most Getafe way imaginable: 32 goals scored across 38 matches, the fewest of any club in the top half of the division, against 38 conceded. A season of one-nil results either way is the model, and it worked well enough to finish above Athletic and Betis in the standings.

Alaves finished fourteenth on 43 points from 10 wins, 13 draws and 15 defeats, scoring 49 and conceding 57. The goal difference tells the story of a side that was competitive in most matches and short of a finisher in too many. Mendizorrotza gives them the one advantage worth having on an opening day, a full house in a small ground against opponents who spent the summer in a different climate.

Marquee Match - Forty-Three Points And A Minus-Fourteen Goal Difference
LaLiga Matchday 1

Sevilla vs Rayo Vallecano

Saturday, 3:30 PM ET | Estadio Ramon Sanchez-Pizjuan, Seville | ESPN Deportes, ESPN+

The Ramon Sanchez-Pizjuan gets the second fixture of the new Spanish season, and Sevilla arrive off the worst campaign of their modern era. Thirteenth place, 43 points from 12 wins, 7 draws and 19 defeats, 46 goals scored and 60 conceded. Only three clubs in the division shipped more, and none of them finished as high.

Rayo Vallecano were the more interesting story last season and the numbers are unusual enough to be worth stating plainly: 14 draws in 38 matches, more than any other side in LaLiga, on the way to eighth place with 50 points from 12 wins and 12 defeats. They scored 41 and conceded 44, a goal difference of minus three that somehow produced a top-half finish and a season of Sundays that ended level.

Two clubs separated by seven points and five places last season, one at home in a stadium that has decided plenty of Spanish football, the other carrying the most stubborn record in the league. Barcelona won the title on 94 points and Real Madrid finished on 86, which is the distance both of these sides are trying to close from the wrong side of the table.

Seventeen Points Against Thirty

Toronto FC vs New England Revolution

Saturday, 7:30 PM ET | BMO Field, Toronto, ON | Apple TV

Toronto sit twelfth in the Eastern Conference on 17 points from 18 matches, a record of 3 wins, 8 draws and 7 defeats with 24 goals scored and 32 conceded. Eight draws from eighteen is a lot of afternoons that finished without an answer either way.

New England are third on 30 points from 9 wins, 3 draws and 6 defeats, and the defensive record is the reason: 23 goals conceded, the second-fewest in the conference behind Nashville. They score 28, which is modest for a side that high, and the shape of the season has been controlling matches rather than winning them comfortably.

BMO Field in August has usually been Toronto's most useful asset, and it needs to be. A thirteen-point gap between two clubs in the same conference in mid-August is the sort of margin that decides whether a season has anything left in it, and Toronto have drawn more matches than they have won or lost.

Twenty-Five Points Against Twenty

Charlotte FC vs Columbus Crew

Saturday, 7:30 PM ET | Bank of America Stadium, Charlotte, NC | Apple TV

Charlotte are seventh in the East on 25 points from 7 wins, 4 draws and 7 defeats, with 29 goals scored and 27 conceded across 18 matches. It is the most balanced profile of any club in Saturday's MLS group, and it has produced a season sitting exactly on the playoff line.

Columbus are eleventh on 20 points from 5 wins, 5 draws and 8 defeats, scoring 26 and conceding 28. The margins are almost identical to Charlotte's and the results are not, which is the sort of season that turns on three or four late goals in either direction.

Bank of America Stadium hosts a fixture with real table consequences underneath the modest numbers. Five points separate seventh from eleventh in a conference where the playoff cut has historically fallen inside a two-match swing, and both clubs have eighteen matches of evidence that they will decide it by one goal.

Forty-Five Goals For And Forty-Four Against

Orlando City SC vs FC Cincinnati

Saturday, 7:30 PM ET | Inter&Co Stadium, Orlando, FL | Apple TV

FC Cincinnati have the strangest line in the Eastern Conference: 45 goals scored and 44 conceded in 18 matches, sixth place on 26 points from 7 wins, 5 draws and 6 defeats. Kevin Denkey has 11 of those goals, tied for fifth in the league, and the defence has undone most of what he provides.

Orlando are tenth on 20 points with 30 goals scored and 47 conceded, the worst defensive record of any club playing Saturday. Their record of 6 wins, 2 draws and 10 defeats includes only two matches all season that ended level, which for a side conceding at this rate means most of them were settled well before the end.

Martin Ojeda has carried the Orlando attack with 11 goals in 15 matches, the same total as Denkey in two fewer appearances. Two sides that concede this freely meeting in Florida in August is the fixture on the MLS board least likely to finish nil-nil, and the season numbers on both ends make that a statement of arithmetic rather than optimism.

The Best Defence In The East Hosts Messi

Nashville SC vs Inter Miami CF

Saturday, 8:30 PM ET | GEODIS Park, Nashville, TN | Apple TV

First against second in the Eastern Conference, two points apart. Nashville lead on 40 points from 12 wins, 4 draws and 2 defeats, and the number that separates them from everyone else is 14, the goals they have conceded in 18 matches. No club in the conference has been harder to score against.

Inter Miami sit on 38 points with 45 goals scored, the joint-highest total in the East alongside Cincinnati, and 32 conceded. Lionel Messi has 12 goals in 15 appearances and shares the league lead in assists with 8, level with his teammate Rodrigo De Paul and with Nashville's Cristian Espinoza. Two defeats all season for the home side, eleven wins for the visitors, and the top of the conference decided on a Saturday night in August.

The stylistic collision is clean. Miami score more than anyone and concede at a rate that keeps their matches loud. Nashville have built the season on 14 goals against and a strike rate of 35 for, and they have lost twice since March. GEODIS Park sells out for this fixture, and the two points between these clubs make it the most consequential match on the entire soccer board.

Thirty-Two Points Against Twenty-One

Houston Dynamo FC vs LA Galaxy

Saturday, 8:30 PM ET | Shell Energy Stadium, Houston, TX | Apple TV

Houston are fourth in the West on 32 points from 10 wins, 2 draws and 6 defeats, and the record has been built the hard way: 27 goals scored, fewer than any club in Saturday's MLS group outside San Diego, with 24 conceded. Only two of their eighteen matches have ended level.

Guilherme Augusto has 11 goals in 18 matches, which is 41 percent of everything Houston have scored this season. A club that relies that heavily on one finisher and still sits fourth says a great deal about the rest of the structure, and the 24 goals against is the part doing the work.

The Galaxy are twelfth on 21 points from 5 wins, 6 draws and 7 defeats, with 32 goals scored and 29 conceded. They have outscored Houston by five and sit eleven points behind them, which is the clearest statement anyone could make about where each club's season has been won and lost.

Thirty-Four Points Against Seventeen In The Late Window

LAFC vs San Diego FC

Saturday, 10:30 PM ET | BMO Stadium, Los Angeles, CA | Apple TV

LAFC close the board second in the Western Conference on 34 points from 19 matches, a record of 10 wins, 4 draws and 5 defeats with 35 goals scored and 19 conceded. That defensive figure is the second-best of any club playing Saturday, behind only Nashville.

San Diego are thirteenth on 17 points from 4 wins, 5 draws and 9 defeats, and the split in their numbers is stark: 22 goals scored, the fewest in Saturday's group, against 36 conceded. Marcus Ingvartsen has 11 of those 22 goals, half of everything the club has produced.

Denis Bouanga has 10 for LAFC, and the contrast between the two sides is really a contrast in distribution. San Diego have one player scoring and little else; LAFC have Bouanga plus a defence conceding a goal a match. A seventeen-point gap in the same conference usually settles a match before it starts, and the late kickoff at BMO Stadium is where the Western Conference table gets its final word of the night.
