MLB - Thursday, August 13

Baseball Builds A Ballpark In The Corn And Hands Nola The Ball

Nine games. The Phillies and Twins play the third MLB at Field of Dreams game in Dyersville, Iowa, streaming exclusively on Netflix. Jacob deGrom takes a .229 opponent average into Anaheim against a lineup that keeps scoring three, Max Scherzer starts for Toronto against Boston, Max Fried and his two home runs allowed face Logan Gilbert in the Bronx, and the Brewers bring the National League's best record to Dodger Stadium to meet Roki Sasaki. Analysis only, no picks.

Messick's 2.59 ERA Against The Hotter Club

Guardians at Tigers

Thursday, 1:10 PM ET | Comerica Park, Detroit, MI

Cleveland is 59-63 and has lost seven of ten. Detroit is 60-61 and has won seven of ten. Same neighborhood in the standings, opposite directions on the road there, and the afternoon matinee gives the Guardians their best remaining argument: Parker Messick, the left-hander with a 2.59 ERA and a 1.03 WHIP across 142.1 innings, holding hitters to a .207 average with 142 strikeouts. Cleveland's problem all season has been everything that happens when he leaves. The Guardians score 3.96 runs a game with a .232 team average, the quietest offensive profile in this game by a wide margin.

Keider Montero answers for Detroit with numbers that deserve more attention than they get: 9-7 with a 3.22 ERA and a 0.99 WHIP, a .208 opponent average, and just 29 walks in 128.2 innings across 20 starts and 26 appearances. Two starters who both hold hitters near .200 puts this afternoon on a short leash for offense.

Detroit's bats have carried the recent run. The Tigers average 4.56 runs a game behind 146 home runs, and their 3.47 staff ERA sits among the stingiest of any team on today's board. A club one game under .500 with numbers that healthy is a September problem for the rest of the AL Central, and Cleveland arrives 31-32 on the road with the quietest bats it will bring anywhere this month.

The Quiet First-Place Team

Reds at White Sox

Thursday, 1:10 PM ET | Rate Field, Chicago, IL

The White Sox lead the AL Central at 62-58, and almost nobody outside Chicago talks about it. The formula is home cooking: a 37-24 record at Rate Field, the best home mark of any team playing today except Milwaukee and Miami. The lineup hits 157 home runs' worth of damage and averages 4.78 runs a game despite a .238 team average, boom-or-bust baseball that has been mostly boom on the South Side.

Andrew Abbott gets the ball for Cincinnati at 6-7 with a 4.13 ERA, and his 64 walks in 133 innings are the most of any starter on today's board. The Reds are 58-62 with a .227 team average, the lowest of any club playing today, and they compensate the same way Chicago does: 158 home runs, third-most among today's eighteen teams. Two all-or-nothing lineups and two walk-prone staffs, with Cincinnati's 4.51 team ERA and 1.41 WHIP carrying most of the risk.

Davis Martin starts for Chicago at 9-6 with a 4.32 ERA and a .266 opponent average, the highest bats-against figure of any starter working today other than Aaron Nola. He survives on run support and Rate Field's forgiveness, and the assignment is friendlier than most: Cincinnati has been a .500 club for weeks, 6-4 in its last ten, without ever stringing together the kind of series that moves a standings needle.

Ashcraft Chases Win Thirteen

Pirates at Marlins

Thursday, 1:10 PM ET | loanDepot park, Miami, FL

Braxton Ashcraft is 12-5 with a 3.88 ERA, a 1.11 WHIP and 155 strikeouts in 141.1 innings, and his twelve wins have come on a Pittsburgh club that sits 59-64. The Pirates remain baseball's strangest offense: 5.02 runs a game and a .256 team average, genuinely productive numbers, attached to a pitching staff and bullpen that keep handing leads back. Ashcraft is the exception, with only 33 walks all season.

Miami is 62-60 and 38-25 at loanDepot park, still one of the best home records in the sport, and sends Tyler Phillips out at 3-6 with a 3.65 ERA. The shape of his season tells the real story: 14 starts among 30 appearances, a swing man asked to stretch out, with 40 walks in 98.2 innings. The Marlins average 4.39 runs a game and hold a 3.98 staff ERA, the profile of a team that wins close games at home and not much on the road, where they are 24-35.

These clubs have been sharing loanDepot park all week, with Tuesday's opener the Paul Skenes and Eury Perez duel that headlined that day's board. The Pirates are 3-7 in their last ten, and an afternoon against a converted reliever is the most favorable pitching matchup they have drawn in this building.

Two Home Runs In Fifteen Starts

Mariners at Yankees

Thursday, 1:35 PM ET | Yankee Stadium, Bronx, NY

Max Fried has allowed two home runs in 86.1 innings. Read that again in the context of his home ballpark, the short porch and all: fifteen starts, a 2.81 ERA, a 1.00 WHIP, a .197 opponent average, and two baseballs over the fence all season. The Yankees are 68-53, the second-best record in the American League, on the strength of a 3.28 staff ERA that leads every team playing today.

Seattle counters with the right kind of spoiler. Logan Gilbert is 9-7 with a 3.28 ERA and a 0.99 WHIP, 152 strikeouts in 145.1 innings, and a .211 opponent average. This is the best pure pitching matchup of the afternoon window, two strike-throwers with WHIPs at or under 1.00, and it lands on a Mariners club that has quietly collapsed: 57-65, 3-7 in its last ten, scoring a board-worst 3.84 runs a game with a .228 team average.

New York's offense is not the 2019 kind, a .231 team average says so, but 169 home runs and 4.50 runs a game is more than enough support for a rotation like this. Seattle's 24-36 road record against the Yankees' 31-27 home mark frames the mismatch: everything Seattle does well, New York does slightly better, and the lineup gap does the rest.

Scherzer At Forty-Two

Red Sox at Blue Jays

Thursday, 3:07 PM ET | Rogers Centre, Toronto, ON

Max Scherzer's line this season is the hardest one on the board to look at: 1-5 with a 6.59 ERA in ten starts, 41 innings, 11 home runs allowed, and 31 strikeouts from a man who built a Hall of Fame case on missing bats. Toronto is 59-64 and sinking, just 31-32 at Rogers Centre, and the franchise's veteran rotation bet has not paid a dime of it back.

Boston sends the opposite story. Payton Tolle, the young left-hander, is 8-6 with a 2.97 ERA, a 1.06 WHIP and 128 strikeouts in 115 innings, holding hitters to a .214 average across 20 starts. The Red Sox are 65-56 with a 3.48 staff ERA, and their 36-25 road record is one of the best in baseball, a strange team that plays better everywhere except Fenway, where they are 29-31.

Toronto's offense gives Scherzer almost nothing to work with: 3.92 runs a game, a .242 average, and 115 home runs, fewer than any team playing today except Milwaukee. Boston is no juggernaut at 4.45 runs a game, but the gap between Tolle's season and Scherzer's is the widest starter-versus-starter split on the entire Thursday board.

Gausman Against The Loudest Lineup

Cubs at Nationals

Thursday, 4:05 PM ET | Nationals Park, Washington, DC

The Cubs are 71-51, winners of eight of their last ten, and they run into the highest-scoring team on today's board. Washington is 60-63 and averages 5.38 runs a game with 177 home runs and a .769 team OPS, all tops among the eighteen clubs playing today. The Nationals stay under .500 anyway because the staff gives it all back: a 4.72 team ERA, the worst mark on this slate, alongside a 1.39 WHIP.

Kevin Gausman's first Cubs season has been heavy lifting: 6-11 with a 4.53 ERA despite 138 strikeouts in 139 innings and just 38 walks. The strikeout-to-walk work says the stuff is intact; the 4.53 says the damage has come in bunches. Handing him the loudest lineup in the majors in their own park is not the spot where that trend reverses on its own, and Chicago's 5.20 runs a game will need to keep pace.

Cade Cavalli has been Washington's best answer to its own staff problem: 10-5 with a 3.36 ERA and 151 strikeouts in 136.2 innings. The matchup inverts the team profiles, Washington with the better starter tonight and Chicago with the better everything else, including a 36-27 road record that is among the best in the National League. The Cubs have been the more complete club for four months; the Nationals have been the more dangerous nine innings all season.

Marquee Matchup - The Field Of Dreams Game
Netflix (exclusive)

Phillies at Twins

Thursday, 7:30 PM ET | Field of Dreams, Dyersville, IA

The third MLB at Field of Dreams game, and the first since 2022, sends Philadelphia and Minnesota into the corn with Netflix carrying it exclusively. Minnesota is a slight favorite at -107 with the total at 8.5, via DraftKings. This is today's BetLegend featured game with a full breakdown here, and the game total is also on tonight's card.

Aaron Nola arrives at 3-9 with a 5.47 ERA and 29 home runs allowed in 125 innings, the most homer-prone season of any starter working today, in the ballpark whose founding memory is eight home runs vanishing into the stalks and Tim Anderson's 9-8 walk-off in 2021. Taj Bradley counters at 9-4 with a 3.76 ERA, 148 strikeouts and a .220 opponent average, with 55 walks as the crack in the armor against a Philadelphia lineup carrying Kyle Schwarber's 35 home runs and Bryce Harper's 25.

The standings stakes are real underneath the cinematography. Philadelphia is 64-58 and has scored one run combined over its last two games, both losses. Minnesota is 60-62, three games behind the first-place White Sox, with Joe Ryan, Pablo Lopez and David Festa all on the injured list behind Bradley. The 2022 edition of this event was a 4-2 pitching duel; the 2021 original was a seventeen-run shootout. Tonight's starters lean loud.

DeGrom And The Three-Run Lineup

Rangers at Angels

Thursday, 10:07 PM ET | Angel Stadium, Anaheim, CA

Texas is -147 with the total at 7.5, via DraftKings, and Jacob deGrom is the reason the price is that steep for a 60-61 club. He is 8-7 with a 3.87 ERA, a 1.13 WHIP and 144 strikeouts in 118.2 innings, coming off five innings of one-run, nine-strikeout work in Baltimore. The Angels' team total in this game anchors tonight's BetLegend card, with the full reasoning there.

Los Angeles is 47-74 and has scored three runs or fewer in seven of its last ten games, averaging 4.07 a night for the season with a .235 team average and 1,144 strikeouts. Mike Trout's 20 home runs and .833 OPS remain the center of gravity, with Zach Neto matching the 20 homers at a .224 average. The wrinkle is that the Angels have actually won two straight, including 3-2 over the Dodgers, their best forty-eight hours of August.

Walbert Urena is the quiet reason this game could stay low from both sides. The 22-year-old right-hander carries a 2.83 ERA and a .205 opponent average, numbers that would headline most matchups, with the asterisk of 55 walks in 101.2 innings and a two-and-a-third-inning, four-run stumble in Miami his last time out. Texas has its own issues holding leads late with Nathan Eovaldi and Jakob Junis both on the injured list, which keeps a 7.5 total honest in a game featuring two quality starters.

The NL's Best Record Visits Sasaki

Brewers at Dodgers

Thursday, 10:10 PM ET | Dodger Stadium, Los Angeles, CA

The nightcap is the heavyweight bout: Milwaukee at 74-47, the best record in the National League, against a 73-48 Dodgers club sitting one game back of them. Los Angeles is -140 with the total at 8, via DraftKings, a price that says home field and lineup depth outweigh the standings. The Dodgers have won three straight; the Brewers have lost three straight, including 11-2 on Tuesday, their worst loss of the month.

Roki Sasaki starts for Los Angeles at 5-5 with a 4.54 ERA and 106 strikeouts in 105 innings, and the number that has defined his season is 21 home runs allowed. His last month has been his best: two earned or fewer in four of his last five starts, including seven innings of one-run ball against the Mets on July 24. Behind him the Dodgers score 5.01 runs a game, the highest figure of any team playing tonight, with 155 home runs and a .768 team OPS.

Milwaukee's answer is Shane Drohan, 6-4 with a 3.87 ERA in a swing role, coming off his roughest start of the year, six earned runs against Minnesota on August 7. The Brewers' spine is still run prevention: a 3.53 team ERA, second-stingiest on today's board, backing an offense that scores 4.89 a game without slugging, just 111 home runs all season. A team that manufactures runs against a rookie-season ace who allows home runs in bunches is exactly the stylistic collision a race this close deserves in mid-August.
