MLB - Thursday, July 30

Sasaki Against Woo Headlines A Ten-Game Thursday

Roki Sasaki and Bryan Woo close out Mariners-Dodgers at Chavez Ravine, the Cubs and Cardinals settle a four-game series in an afternoon window, the White Sox host the Yankees as a home favorite with first place on the line in two divisions, and the Giants and Padres open a set in San Diego. Ten games, verified lines and starters. Analysis only, no picks.

Marquee Matchup
SNLA

Mariners at Dodgers

Thursday, 10:10 PM ET | Dodger Stadium, Los Angeles, CA | LAD -162, O/U 9.0

The series has been a coin flip so far. Seattle (53-56) took Tuesday's opener 7-6 behind five home runs, Los Angeles (68-40) answered 4-2 on Wednesday, and the finale gives both clubs their most interesting starter of the set. Roki Sasaki is 4-5 with a 4.71 ERA and 94 strikeouts across 93.2 innings, numbers that undersell how electric the stuff remains, though the 20 home runs allowed explain the ERA better than anything else.

Seattle counters with Bryan Woo, 7-7 with a 4.37 ERA and one of the stingiest walk rates on the board, 24 free passes in 115.1 innings against 114 strikeouts. Woo's 1.14 WHIP is the profile of a strike-thrower who lives on the edges, and against a Dodgers lineup averaging 5.15 runs per game with 139 home runs, the margin between painting a corner and catching plate is the whole game.

The stakes tilt toward Seattle. The Dodgers lead the NL West by double digits and are playing for tune-up value; the Mariners sit third in the AL West at 2.5 games back and cannot afford to hand back the gains of Tuesday's statement win. A road series win at Dodger Stadium would be Seattle's loudest result of the summer.

Series Finale
Regional

Cubs at Cardinals

Thursday, 2:15 PM ET | Busch Stadium, St. Louis, MO | STL -114, O/U 8.5

Chicago (61-47) leads this four-game set 2-1 after wins of 7-3 and 10-2 gave way to a 3-2 St. Louis (54-54) comeback on Wednesday night, and the afternoon finale is a genuine pitching matchup: Andre Pallante, coming off seven shutout innings with eight strikeouts against Cincinnati, against Javier Assad, who is 6-1 with a 3.86 ERA but has not been stretched past five innings all month. The market makes the 54-54 home side a short favorite over the 61-47 road side purely on the starters. The full breakdown, injuries and keys are on the Featured Game page.

Two First-Place Fights
Regional

Yankees at White Sox

Thursday, 2:10 PM ET | Rate Field, Chicago, IL | CHW -110, O/U 7.5

Chicago's 6-5 win on Wednesday broke up the sweep bid and set up a stranger finale than the records suggest: the White Sox (56-51) are actually the favorite at home against the 61-47 Yankees, and the reason is on the mound. Sean Burke has been Chicago's best starter all season, 7-5 with a 3.19 ERA, a 1.12 WHIP and 130 strikeouts in 118.1 innings, and he leads a club that has quietly built a 33-21 home record while sitting first in the AL Central, a game and a half up on Cleveland.

New York starts Ryan Weathers, 4-7 with a 4.25 ERA but 122 strikeouts in 108 innings, a swing-and-miss lefty whose record has suffered from 17 home runs allowed. The Yankees sit 2.5 back of Tampa Bay in the AL East, and with both clubs leading or chasing a division, this low total of 7.5 reads as the market's bet on Burke and Weathers both missing bats in a getaway-day matinee.

Ace At The Trop
Regional

Rangers at Rays

Thursday, 12:10 PM ET | Tropicana Field, St. Petersburg, FL

The earliest first pitch of the day is also one of its best pitching stories. Shane McClanahan takes the ball for Tampa Bay (63-44) at 9-6 with a 3.00 ERA, a 1.11 WHIP and a .207 opponent average, and he does it for a club that is 39-16 at home. The Rays lead the AL East and have won seven of ten, including a 3-0 shutout of these same Rangers on Wednesday.

Texas (55-53) still leads the AL West, one game up on a surging Houston club, but the margin is thin and the offense is the problem: the Rangers average 4.17 runs per game with a .246 team average, and they were just blanked by Tampa Bay a day ago. Cole Winn gets the start with a 5.94 ERA across 36.1 innings, most of that work coming in relief, so length is an open question against the best home team in the American League.

Getaway Day
Regional

Royals at Twins

Thursday, 1:40 PM ET | Target Field, Minneapolis, MN | MIN -136, O/U 9.0

Kansas City (46-63) is playing better than its record, 6-4 over the last ten with a 4-0 shutout win in Minneapolis on Wednesday behind the kind of pitching performance this club has rarely gotten all year. Noah Cameron starts the finale at 5-8 with a 4.93 ERA and exactly 100 strikeouts in 107.2 innings, and his 1.41 WHIP against a .270 opponent average explains why so many of his outings turn into traffic management.

Minnesota (54-55) counters with Bailey Ober, 7-3 with a 4.56 ERA and a much cleaner 1.25 WHIP, on a club that has hovered around .500 all month, 5-5 in its last ten and three games back in the AL Central. The Twins average 4.72 runs per game to Kansas City's 4.18, and the 9.0 total leans on two starters who have each been homer-prone, 13 for Ober and 14 for Cameron this season.

Under Alert At Citi
SNY

Marlins at Mets

Thursday, 7:10 PM ET | Citi Field, Queens, NY | NYM -131, O/U 7.0

The lowest total on the board belongs to the night's best pure pitching matchup. Eury Perez starts for Miami (55-54) at 5-8 with a 3.56 ERA, a 1.11 WHIP and a .205 opponent average, with 106 strikeouts in 98.2 innings, a record that says far more about Miami's support than about him. The Marlins have won three straight and sit a surprising 34-23 at home, though they are just 21-31 on the road.

New York (46-63) answers with Nolan McLean, who has been the best thing about a last-place season in Queens: 7-7 with a 3.32 ERA, 142 strikeouts in 119.1 innings and a 1.11 WHIP of his own. The Mets just took two of three from Atlanta in Wednesday's doubleheader split, including a 3-2 win in the opener, and with two starters holding opponents near the .205 mark, the 7.0 total still has to survive two thin bullpens behind them.

Road Favorite
Regional

Pirates at Reds

Thursday, 7:10 PM ET | Great American Ball Park, Cincinnati, OH | PIT -115, O/U 9.5

Pittsburgh (55-54) has quietly become a real offense, averaging 5.17 runs per game, second only to Washington among the National League clubs on today's board, and the market respects it enough to price the Pirates as road favorites despite two straight losses. Yohan Ramirez handles the start at 6-3 with a 3.21 ERA across 56 innings of what has been almost exclusively relief work, so this is a bullpen-led assignment in a park that punishes mistakes.

Cincinnati (50-57) starts Rhett Lowder, 3-7 with a 5.61 ERA and a 1.57 WHIP that ranks among the roughest of any regular starter on this slate. The Reds have actually played decent baseball lately, 6-4 in their last ten, but the 9.5 total at Great American Ball Park reflects both Lowder's traffic problem and a Pittsburgh lineup that has scored in bunches all month.

A Return From The Void
Regional

Nationals at Braves

Thursday, 7:15 PM ET | Truist Park, Atlanta, GA | ATL -156, O/U 9.5

Washington (55-54) is the strangest team in baseball by home-road split, 23-34 at home and 32-20 on the road, the best road record of any club on tonight's board. Jake Irvin makes his first major league start since May 23, carrying a 5.23 ERA from the 11 starts he made before the layoff, and everything about Washington's night depends on what version of him shows up after more than two months away.

Atlanta (63-45) leads the NL East and hands the ball to Grant Holmes, 6-4 with a 3.79 ERA and 83 strikeouts across 97.1 innings, coming off three straight five-inning starts. The Braves' offense is the wildcard: Atlanta has not scored more than three runs in any of its last five games, including a three-run total across Wednesday's entire doubleheader against the Mets, and a lineup hitting .248 has leaned hard on the rotation to keep winning at a 63-45 pace. Washington averages 5.47 runs per game, most in the NL East, so the 9.5 total is carried almost entirely by the visitors' half of the scoreboard.

Ace Against A Bullpen Game
Regional

Red Sox at Athletics

Thursday, 9:40 PM ET | Sutter Health Park, Sacramento, CA | BOS -185, O/U 10.0

Boston (56-51) leads this four-game series 2-1 after Wednesday's 4-2 win and closes it with its best card to play: Sonny Gray, 12-2 with a 2.78 ERA and a 1.16 WHIP across 106.2 innings. His last start was a rare stumble, five earned runs in Toronto, but the two before it were six-inning, one-run outings against the Mets and Rays, and the Red Sox have won seven of ten behind exactly this kind of pitching.

The Athletics (45-63) will piece the night together. Mason Barnett opens with zero starts this season, 24.2 innings all in relief and a 4.74 ERA, and his longest outing of the year lasted 3.1 innings. Sacramento has lost seven of ten, and the 10.0 total, the highest on the board, says the market expects the A's staff to leak runs even if Gray gives some back on the other side.

Series Opener At Petco
Regional

Giants at Padres

Thursday, 9:40 PM ET | Petco Park, San Diego, CA | SD -161, O/U 8.5

San Diego (55-53) has won five straight, a run built on a sweep in Miami and two home wins over Colorado, and opens this set with JP Sears, 3-3 with a 3.86 ERA in a five-start season that has yet to include a six-inning outing in his last three turns. The Padres' issue all year has been the bats, a .234 team average and 4.19 runs per game that rank near the bottom of the National League, which makes every short start a stress test for the bullpen.

San Francisco (46-62) is 22 games back and playing out the string in the standings, but the club that shows up night to night is anyone's guess: 16 runs against the Brewers on Wednesday, two the night before that. Robbie Ray is the stabilizer, 9-6 with a 3.16 ERA, one earned run or none in each of his last three starts and consecutive eight-inning scoreless outings in late June. On form, Ray against Sears is a mismatch in the visitors' favor, and the -161 price on the home side is a bet on the streak and the lineup gap holding up anyway.
