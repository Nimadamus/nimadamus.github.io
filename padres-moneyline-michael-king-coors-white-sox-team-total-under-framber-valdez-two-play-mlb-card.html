Padres -195: Michael King Against Tanner Gordon At Coors

These two clubs are headed in opposite directions and they've been doing it all week. San Diego is 9 and 1 in its last ten and holds the third wild card spot at 83 and 69. Colorado is 56 and 96, 1 and 9 in its last ten, with a run differential of 170 runs in the red. The Padres have won 10 of the 12 meetings this season, including Monday's 8 to 7 and Wednesday's 9 to 3 in this series.

King's form is the reason I'm comfortable laying this much. His last five lines: 6.2 innings and 2 earned against the Mets, 7 scoreless against the Pirates, 6 scoreless against the Reds, 6 scoreless against the Yankees, then 7 innings and 3 earned in San Francisco. He's gone six innings or more in every one of those, which matters at Coors, where the bullpen is the part of a road favorite that usually leaks. He's also seen this lineup already, 6 innings and 4 earned at Petco Park in July, and the Padres still won that game.

Gordon is the bigger story. Opponents are hitting .313 against him, and left handed hitters have a .907 OPS with 13 home runs. At home this season hitters have a .965 OPS against him. His last two outings were 4.2 innings with 5 earned against St. Louis and 4 innings with 10 hits allowed in Detroit. The Rockies' home staff has a 5.85 ERA behind him, so there isn't much relief coming if he's gone early.

Coors games have averaged 11.38 total runs this season, and the forecast is 77 degrees with the wind blowing out to left at 5 miles an hour. That cuts both ways, but it cuts deeper against the team that allows 885 runs.

White Sox Team Total Under 4.5: Framber Valdez At Rate Field

The White Sox are in a real race, 77 and 75 and a half game out of the division lead, and they're scuffling at the worst time. They're 3 and 7 in their last ten and have lost two straight in Cleveland. Over those ten games they scored 4 runs or fewer six times, including 0, 1, 2 and three separate 3 run nights.

Valdez's season ERA of 4.01 undersells where he is right now. His last two starts were 7 scoreless innings against Cleveland and 6 innings against Colorado with no earned runs, and across his last five he's at 2.93. He keeps the ball in the park, 15 home runs allowed in 163.2 innings, and that's the key against a White Sox lineup that leans on the long ball with 195 homers and a .235 team average.

The Tigers' staff helps too. Detroit is 72 and 80 but has allowed only 607 runs, 70 fewer than Chicago, with a 3.97 road ERA. The White Sox have been held to 4 or fewer in 79 of 152 games this season, and in 8 of their last 15 at home.

The Argument Against This Card

-195 is a lot to lay at Coors. The altitude turns routine fly balls into doubles, and the Rockies did beat the Padres 9 to 3 on Tuesday. One bad inning from the bullpen is all it takes.

The White Sox hit lefties better than righties, a .743 OPS against left handed pitching, and they've scored 4.89 runs a game at home. When Valdez faced them in May he gave up 4 earned in 6.2 innings and Chicago scored 7. This under is a bet on his current form, not his history against this lineup.
