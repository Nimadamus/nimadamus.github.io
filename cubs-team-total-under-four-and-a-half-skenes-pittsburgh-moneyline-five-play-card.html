The Cubs Team Total Under: Betting Against The Best Offense In Baseball

Start with the thing that looks wrong. Chicago has scored 792 runs this season, more than any other club in Major League Baseball, at 5.35 a game with a .771 OPS and 204 home runs. No team on the board tonight hits like this one, and the ticket is their team total under 4.5.

A 3.83 ERA is not what people expect next to the name, and it should be said plainly rather than hidden. The number that matters more for a team total is the distribution underneath it. Skenes has allowed three runs or fewer in 20 of his 29 starts, and two or fewer in 16 of them. A 3.83 built that way is a pitcher who is mostly excellent and occasionally blown up, and a team total under is a bet on the mostly, priced at -118.

He has also already seen this lineup twice. On May 28 he threw 5.1 innings with 10 strikeouts and Chicago scored 3. On July 25 he threw 5.1 again with 11 strikeouts and Chicago scored 5. Twenty one strikeouts in 10.2 innings against them, one game under this number and one over. That is not a trend, it is two data points, and anyone selling it as a trend is selling something.

The season shape is the better argument. For all the scoring, the Cubs have been held to four runs or fewer in 68 of their 148 games, 45.9 percent. A -118 price asks for 54.13. So the ticket is paying about eight points of edge for the difference between a league average starter and one who misses ten and a half bats per nine innings, in a game Chicago will see for the third time this year.

The Argument Against This Card

The money is in the wrong place. Pittsburgh risks 8.4 units, more than the other four legs combined, on the leg supported by the least evidence. Two week one blowouts of overmatched opponents is not a basis for laying that kind of price, and if this card loses money today that is almost certainly the reason.

Both college games are being bet on ignorance, which cuts both ways. The argument for Arizona +7 is that nobody knows anything yet. That same sentence is the argument against Pittsburgh -280, and the card is holding both at once.

The under 9.5 is fighting the park. Sixty three percent of Athletics home games have gone over this number. No amount of lineup analysis erases a sample that large, and Gage Jump walking 4.19 men per nine is exactly how a slow game turns into a nine run inning.

The Cubs have hit 204 home runs. A team total under against a club that lives on the long ball only needs one swing with traffic on base, and Chicago has already gone over four runs in five of its last ten games, including a 12 run night.

Bottom Line

The best ticket on this page is the one that reads worst on a graphic. The Cubs lead baseball in runs and the play is their team total under, because for a single afternoon the starter matters more than the season average, and Paul Skenes has held teams to three or fewer in 20 of 29 tries.

The most expensive ticket is Pittsburgh, and it is expensive in the way that actually costs money: 8.4 units at risk to win 3, on one Saturday of evidence against an opponent nobody can evaluate yet.
