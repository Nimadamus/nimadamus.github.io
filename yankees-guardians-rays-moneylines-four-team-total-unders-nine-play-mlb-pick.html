Twelve Units On Nine Plays And A Starter Opponents Are Hitting .199 Against

August 28, 2026|11 min read|BetLegend

Nine plays today for 12 units to win, and making them costs 15.72 units of risk. It is the widest card of the month and the shape of it is not an accident. Five of the nine tickets are unders, four of those are team totals, and every one of them is pointed at the same thing: a starting pitcher who has been suffocating hitters all season standing opposite a lineup that has not scored.

Yesterday's card went 2-1 and still lost 0.28 units, which is the cleanest possible illustration of what heavy chalk does. Milwaukee won 8-2 at Citi Field. Atlanta was held to a single run and the team total under cashed. Then the Yankees lost 5-1 to Houston, and because that ticket was 2.5 units to win at -151 it cost 3.78 units by itself. Two right opinions out of three, one losing day.

A .199 Opponent Average Against A .323 One

Two tickets in the Bronx. Yankees moneyline at -149 for 1.5 units, and the Red Sox team total under 3.5 at -135 for 1 unit. Both of them are the same bet wearing different clothes, and both of them are about Cam Schlittler.

  • Opponents hit .199
  • Three or fewer runs in 23 of 27 starts

PATRICK SANDOVAL, 2026

  • 8 starts, 39.2 innings, 1-2
  • 4.54 ERA, 1.76 WHIP
  • 18 walks, 4.08 per nine
  • Opponents hit .323
  • 11.80 hits allowed per nine

THE TWO CLUBS

  • Yankees: 75-58, 7-3 last ten
  • Yankees staff: 3.24 ERA, 1.18 WHIP
  • Red Sox: 73-60, 7-3 last ten
  • Red Sox held to 3 or fewer in 43 percent of games
  • Boston scoring 5.07 a game over its last fifteen

Schlittler has been the best story on this pitching staff all season and the numbers stopped being a story a long time ago. A 2.16 ERA and a 0.96 WHIP across 158 innings is not a stretch of good luck, it is two thirds of a season. He has held opponents to three runs or fewer in 23 of his 27 starts and to two or fewer in 20 of them. His last five outings read 1, 4, 1, 1 and 1 runs allowed. Opponents are hitting .199 against him, and he walks 2.11 per nine, which means the traffic almost never arrives for free.

Patrick Sandoval is the other side and he is the reason both tickets exist rather than one. Eight starts into his season he is carrying a 4.54 ERA, a 1.76 WHIP and a .323 opponent batting average. He allows 11.80 hits per nine innings and walks 4.08 more. He averages under five innings a start, which hands the middle of the game to a Boston bullpen on a night when the Yankees will already have seen the top of their order twice.

Now the honest part, because it cuts against the smaller ticket. Boston is not slumping. The Red Sox have scored 5.07 runs a game over their last fifteen and have been held to three or fewer only four times in that stretch. Season long they are at 4.51 a game with a .729 OPS, which is the best mark of the ten clubs on this card. Asking that lineup to stay under 3.5 is asking a good offense to have a quiet night, and the only reason to ask it is the man on the mound.

The moneyline at -149 needs 59.8 percent. The team total at -135 needs 57.4. The Yankees have the better starter by a distance that is hard to overstate, the better bullpen behind him at a 3.24 staff ERA, and home field. What they do not have is a lineup that scores much. New York hits .231 as a team and strikes out 1226 times, the most of any club playing tonight. This is a pitching bet on both tickets, and if it goes wrong it goes wrong 6-4 rather than 2-1.

Start with the offenses, because this is the rare game where both of them are the story. Seattle ranks 29th in baseball at 3.93 runs a game. Toronto ranks 30th at 3.92. Nobody else on tonight's fifteen game board is below either of them. Put the two lowest scoring lineups in the sport in the same building and the total opens low for a reason, and 7 is already a low number.

Then add Cease, who has quietly built the best strikeout season of anyone starting tonight. A 13.03 K/9 rate and a .175 opponent average across 144 innings, with a 2.37 ERA and three runs or fewer allowed in 19 of 24 starts. His last five: one run, zero runs, three runs, two runs, one run. He averages six innings a night, so Toronto's bullpen is being asked for nine outs rather than fifteen.

Seattle's half of the scoreboard is the 2 unit ticket and it is the one with the most evidence behind it. The Mariners have been held to three runs or fewer in 69 of their 134 games, which is 51 percent of the season, and they are doing it against the whole league rather than against aces. Tonight they face the best strikeout arm on the board while carrying the 29th ranked offense. The price at -155 needs 60.8 percent, and Seattle's own season rate is 51. The gap is closed by Cease and by nothing else, which is exactly why the writeup names him rather than the record.

The game under 7 is the softer version of the same opinion at a better price. It needs 54.5 percent. Seattle games have finished under 7 in 49 of 134 this season, Toronto games in 49 of 135, so on raw season rate this is a 36 percent proposition. That number is not the argument. The argument is that neither of these lineups has scored all year and one of the two starters is running a sub 2.40 ERA with the highest strikeout rate in the game. The Seattle starter has not been announced, which is the honest hole in the ticket and it is addressed below.

The 5-13 next to Bibee's name is the most misleading number on this page. His ERA is 3.97, his WHIP is 1.14 and he has held opponents to three runs or fewer in 20 of 27 starts. Cleveland has scored 4.05 runs a game this season, 28th in baseball, and a starter cannot win games a lineup does not give him. Judge him on the 1.14 and the 20 of 27.

Cleveland is 8-2 in its last ten and its staff has struck out 1250 batters, the most of any club playing tonight, behind a 3.73 team ERA. Kansas City counters with a 4.68 staff ERA and 165 home runs allowed. At -131 the moneyline needs 56.7 percent, and a home team with the better rotation, the better bullpen and the better run prevention against a club eleven games under .500 is not an unreasonable ask at that price.

The team total under is the harder sell and it deserves the plain version. Kansas City is 9-1 in its last ten and has scored 5.40 runs a game over its last fifteen. That is the hottest offense on this card by a wide margin. The counterweights are that the Royals have still been held to three or fewer in 66 of 135 games on the season, Bibee has done exactly that to 20 of 27 opponents, and Cleveland's bullpen is the second best unit on tonight's board by team ERA. At -120 the ticket needs 54.5 percent. Buying an under on a lineup this hot is the single most contrarian thing on the card and it is priced like it.

Sixty five percent. That is how often games in this ballpark have gone over 9.5 runs this season, across a sample of 66 home dates, and the price is asking for 51.2. Sutter Health Park is a minor league park with major league hitters in it, the ball carries, and the combined average of 12.02 runs is not close to any other home environment in baseball. The last ten games there have averaged 11.3.

The pitching matchup does nothing to argue with the park. The Athletics staff carries a 5.46 ERA and a 1.50 WHIP and has allowed 204 home runs, the worst three numbers of any club on this card, and their starter has not been announced, which historically has meant a bullpen game. Baltimore brings the 11th ranked offense at 4.54 runs a game.

Brandon Young is the one thing pulling the other way and it is worth naming rather than hiding. He is 9-3 with a 3.50 ERA and has held opponents to three or fewer in 18 of 22 starts. But his WHIP is 1.36 and he walks 45 batters in 123 innings, and traffic is what this park converts into runs. At -105 for 1 unit, taking 65 percent of a 66 game sample at a price that needs 51 is the most straightforward ticket on the board.

  • Padres: 72-62, 4.25 runs a game, 22nd

Tampa Bay is 79-54 and has been the quietest very good team in the league all year. The Rays hit .260 as a club, the best mark on this card, and they strike out 930 times, fewest in the sport. That is a lineup that puts the ball in play, which travels better against a starter working with a 1.09 WHIP than a lineup that lives on the home run does.

McClanahan carries a 3.10 ERA with a .209 opponent average and eight home runs allowed in 107 innings. The one caveat on him is length. He averages 4.88 innings a start, the shortest of any starter being backed tonight, so the Tampa bullpen is covering four or more innings. That bullpen belongs to a 3.78 staff ERA, which is fine rather than dominant, and it is the reason this ticket is 1 unit instead of 2.

The Padres team total under 3.5 at -118 is the cheaper way into the same read. San Diego scores 4.25 runs a game, 22nd in baseball, on a .238 average and a .703 OPS. Against a starter holding opponents to .209 with three runs or fewer in 16 of 22 starts, and inside a park where Tampa's staff has held its ERA under 3.80, asking the 22nd ranked offense to stay under four runs at a price needing 54.1 percent is a fair ask.

Casey Mize on the other side is why the moneyline is only 1 unit. His 1.09 WHIP and his 26 walks in 104 innings are genuinely good numbers and his last five starts read 1, 8, 2, 0 and 4 runs, which is a pitcher who is either excellent or blown up with very little in between. Betting Tampa at -130 is betting the club and the bullpen, not a mismatch on the mound.

What Can Beat This Card

Kansas City is the hottest team in baseball right now. Say it loudest, because it is the ticket most at odds with what is actually happening on the field. The Royals are 9-1 in their last ten and averaging 5.40 runs a game over their last fifteen. The under 3.5 asks that lineup to stop for a night against a starter who has allowed four or more in seven of 27 starts. The evidence for it is season long and the evidence against it is from this week.

Two starters have not been announced. Seattle has not named one at Rogers Centre and neither have the Athletics in Sacramento. That is 3.5 units of exposure across the Mariners team total, the Blue Jays and Mariners under and the Athletics over, sitting behind a name nobody has. The Seattle side is partially insulated because the Mariners ticket is about Cease and about Seattle's own bats, and the Sacramento side actually improves if it turns into a bullpen game, but the honest read is that neither ticket has a full matchup behind it.

Boston is scoring 5.07 a game. The Red Sox team total under is priced at -135 against a lineup that has been held to three or fewer only four times in its last fifteen. Schlittler is the entire argument and there is no secondary argument.

McClanahan does not go deep. Under five innings a start means the Tampa bullpen owns the second half of that game, and that is two tickets exposed to the same relief corps.

The Mariners ticket is 3.10 units of risk. It is the single largest position on the card at the single worst price on the card. Seattle has scored four or more in 65 of 134 games. This is not a lineup that never scores, it is a lineup that usually does not.

Five unders on one slate is a correlated card. Cold nights and quiet umpires help every one of them and a leaguewide slugfest hurts five tickets at once. The Athletics over is the only hedge in that direction and it is 1 unit.
