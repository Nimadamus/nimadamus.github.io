Two And Six Against The Team Nobody Beats

Milwaukee has outscored the league by 136 runs, the widest margin in the sport, and owns the best record in baseball at 70-43. Pittsburgh is 57-58 with a run differential of plus 29. Those two profiles have met eight times this season and the worse team has won six of them.

It began in April, when the Pirates went into American Family Field and won 6-0 and 6-3 before dropping the finale 5-0. It continued in July at PNC Park, where Pittsburgh took a doubleheader 7-6 and 3-2 on the eleventh and then hung fourteen runs on the Brewers the following afternoon. This week the pattern held for one more night, a 4-3 Pirates win Monday, before Milwaukee finally answered 4-2 on Tuesday. Tonight decides the series.

None of that changes the season shape of either club. The Brewers are fourteen games clear in the National League Central and have been in front since the spring. The Pirates are three and a half games out of the third wild card, which is close enough to matter and far enough that August has to go right.

Recent form points the same way the standings do. Milwaukee is 6-4 in its last ten. Pittsburgh is 4-6 and has lost seven of twelve, a stretch that included getting outscored 22-15 across four games at Cincinnati. Home field is the part of the Brewers profile that travels least well into a one-game read: they are 37-21 in this building and 33-22 away from it, a real split but not a decisive one, while Pittsburgh is 28-30 on the road.

Injury Report & Lineup Notes

Pittsburgh Pirates
Out
Center fielder Oneil Cruz is on the 60-day injured list with non-displaced fractures of the fourth and fifth metacarpals in his left hand. Outfielder Ryan O'Hearn, who carries a .788 OPS with 17 home runs and 69 RBI, is on the 10-day list with a left quad strain.
Rotation
Mitch Keller is on the 60-day list with a right teres major injury, which is a large part of why the innings load behind Skenes has fallen on a patched group. Shortstop prospect Konnor Griffin is also on the 60-day list with a left ring finger injury.
Bullpen
Evan Sisk is on the 15-day list with left elbow inflammation and Chris Devenski is on the 60-day list. The relief corps has thrown 449 innings this season with a 4.29 ERA and 219 walks, the highest walk total of any unit involved in this game.
Milwaukee Brewers
Out
Right fielder Sal Frelick is on the 10-day injured list with a right shoulder strain. That removes a regular from a lineup already leaning on its top four bats for most of the slug.
Rotation Attrition
Brandon Woodruff is on the 60-day list with right shoulder inflammation and Quinn Priester is on the 60-day list with right thoracic outlet syndrome. Kyle Harrison himself has been on the 15-day list with left forearm tightness since early July and is scheduled to come off it for this start.
Bullpen
JoJo Romero and Joel Kuhnel are both on the 15-day list, Kuhnel with a right shoulder impingement, and Angel Zerpa and Rob Zastryzny are on the 60-day list. What remains has still produced a 3.56 relief ERA across 437.1 innings.

The Pitching Match-Up

Paul Skenes, Pittsburgh

Twenty-three starts, 124.2 innings, 156 strikeouts against 32 walks. That is 11.26 punchouts per nine with a 1.11 WHIP and a .230 opponent average, and it belongs to a 3.90 ERA that looks ordinary next to the raw stuff. The gap between those numbers is the story of his summer.

His last three outings show both halves of it. On July 19 he gave Cleveland seven innings and one earned run with eight strikeouts. On July 25 he struck out eleven Cubs in five and a third and gave up five earned. On July 31 at Cincinnati he lasted four innings, walked four and allowed five earned while still fanning seven. The bat-missing never leaves. The damage control comes and goes.

One split belongs on the table before anyone treats him as untouchable tonight, and it is his own first inning. Skenes has thrown 22.2 opening frames this season and allowed fourteen runs in them, a 5.16 ERA, with 18 hits and nine walks. He has still worked a scoreless first in sixteen of twenty-three starts, so the damage is concentrated rather than constant, and he strikes out better than a third of the hitters he faces in that inning. From the second inning forward he is a different pitcher.

Kyle Harrison, Milwaukee

Harrison has been the quiet success of the Milwaukee rotation. He is 8-2 with a 3.01 ERA, a 1.08 WHIP, 101 strikeouts and 20 walks in 83.2 innings, and opponents are hitting .221 against him. He struck out twelve Cubs across seven shutout innings on May 20 and twelve Giants in five and two-thirds on June 2.

Then he stopped. His last appearance was July 8, a four-inning, three-run outing against St. Louis, and he has been on the 15-day injured list with left forearm tightness ever since. Twenty-eight days is a long absence for a pitcher whose longest outing all year is seven innings, and the practical read is a limited pitch count with a bullpen missing four arms behind it.

His own first inning has been his best inning, which points the opposite direction from Skenes: 17 opening frames, five earned runs, a 2.65 ERA, three walks, nineteen strikeouts and a .190 opponent average. Thirteen of his seventeen starts have begun with a scoreless top of the first.

That grid hides the fact that decides most of the evening. Pittsburgh has scored more runs than Milwaukee, 584 to 562, and ranks third in baseball in that category. Take the Pirates on the road and the offense becomes a different animal: .243 with a .704 OPS away from PNC Park against .272 and .794 at home. That is a ninety-point swing depending on the building, and tonight is the away column.

Handedness compounds it. Harrison throws left-handed, and Pittsburgh is 10-23 against left-handed starters this season while hitting .246 with a .693 OPS against them. On the road against a lefty the Pirates are 6-13. Milwaukee is barely better in that split, batting .237 with a .685 OPS against southpaws, but the Brewers will not see one tonight. Skenes throws right-handed, and Milwaukee is 49-32 against right-handed starters.

Free passes are the other lever. Milwaukee has issued 353 walks and drawn 467, both the better side of this matchup, while Pittsburgh has issued 418 and drawn 416. With the market at 7, the club giving away fewer bases and stealing more of them, 111 steals against Pittsburgh's 101, has the cleaner route to a low-scoring win.

Keys To The Game

Pirates Keys
Skenes past the fourth. His last two starts ended at five and a third and four innings. A Pirates bullpen carrying a 4.29 ERA and 219 walks cannot absorb five innings against the best record in baseball.
Reynolds and Lowe against a lefty. Bryan Reynolds has an .824 OPS with 69 walks, and Brandon Lowe has 23 home runs and 71 RBI. With Ryan O'Hearn on the injured list, those two carry a lineup hitting .246 against left-handed pitching.
Scoring first. Pittsburgh is 27-31 against clubs with winning records, and its bullpen profile does not suit chasing. In the six wins over Milwaukee this year the Pirates generally led early.
Brewers Keys
Harrison's pitch count. Four innings is a realistic ceiling after twenty-eight days off. Milwaukee has to script the fifth through ninth from a bullpen missing JoJo Romero, Joel Kuhnel, Angel Zerpa and Rob Zastryzny.
Bauers and Turang doing the damage. Jake Bauers leads the club at .270 with 20 home runs and an .878 OPS. Brice Turang is at .268 with 16 homers, 69 RBI and 15 stolen bases. Against a pitcher striking out better than eleven per nine, contact from those two is the whole plan.
The first inning. Skenes has allowed fourteen runs in his opening frames, more than in any other inning, and Milwaukee has drawn 467 walks as a team. The Brewers did not touch him early in either July meeting.

Market Context

Milwaukee is -122 at home with Pittsburgh at +112, and the total sits at 7 with the over priced at -120 and the under at -102. A -122 favorite implies a 55.0 percent win probability, a remarkably soft price for a club thirteen games better in the standings. The market is openly respecting both the head-to-head history and the fact that Milwaukee is starting a pitcher who has been away for a month.

The total is the number that stands out across the full slate. Seven is the lowest figure posted anywhere on Wednesday's fifteen games. Coors Field carries an 11.5. Cincinnati carries a 9.5. Philadelphia carries a 9.5. Shaving two and a half runs off the median prices two things at once: Skenes striking out 11.26 per nine, and a Milwaukee home staff running a 3.11 ERA inside this building.

History has not always cooperated with that number. Brewers games this season have finished under seven runs 40 times in 113 with seventeen landing exactly on seven, and Pirates games have gone under seven 35 times in 115. Both clubs live above this figure more often than below it, which makes a 7 an aggressive posting rather than an obvious one.

Final Thoughts

The honest read is that Milwaukee is the better team and Pittsburgh has spent all season proving that fact does not survive contact with this particular matchup. The Brewers own the best record, the better staff ERA, the better home record and the better bullpen, and they are 2-6 against a club playing .496 baseball. Six games is a small sample. It is also a sample that keeps producing the same result.

What tilts tonight back toward the home side is handedness and workload. Pittsburgh is 10-23 against left-handed starters and hits .243 with a .704 OPS on the road, and it arrives without Oneil Cruz and Ryan O'Hearn. What tilts it away is Harrison's month on the shelf, a bullpen missing four arms, and a Pirates offense that ranks third in the sport in runs scored for a reason.

Expect short nights from both starters and long ones from two thinned relief corps, the exact set of conditions that makes a 7 uncomfortable in both directions. The first team to hand out a walk with two men on will probably decide it.
