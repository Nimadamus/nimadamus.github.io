Four And A Half Units On One Quiet Game In Milwaukee

August 5, 2026|8 min read|BetLegend

Fifteen games are on the board tonight and fourteen of them carry a total of eight or higher. Coors Field is priced at 11.5. Cincinnati and Philadelphia sit at 9.5. One game stands apart from all of it: Pittsburgh at Milwaukee, where the number is 7.

The market almost never posts a 7 in August. It has posted one here because Paul Skenes strikes out 11.26 hitters per nine innings, because Kyle Harrison holds opponents to a .221 average, and because Milwaukee's pitching staff runs a 3.11 ERA inside American Family Field. Three plays come off that same game, and they are all versions of the same idea.

NRFI -150: Harrison's Best Inning Is The First

Most first-inning unders are sold on a starter's overall quality. This one is sold on a specific split, and it belongs to the pitcher nobody is talking about.

Kyle Harrison has thrown 17 first innings this season. He has allowed five earned runs in them, a 2.65 ERA, on twelve hits and three walks with nineteen strikeouts, and opponents are hitting .190 against him in the opening frame. Thirteen of his seventeen starts have begun with a scoreless top of the first. That is the cleanest first-inning line of any starter working tonight.

He faces a Pittsburgh lineup that has no business handling him. The Pirates are 10-23 against left-handed starters and hit .246 with a .693 OPS against left-handed pitching. Away from PNC Park the whole offense drops to .243 with a .704 OPS. Their two best bats against a lefty, Bryan Reynolds and Brandon Lowe, will see him at most once in the first, and Pittsburgh is playing without Oneil Cruz and Ryan O'Hearn.

The other half is Skenes. His first inning is genuinely his worst inning, and that gets its own paragraph in the counterpoint section below because it deserves one. What is also true is that he has still worked a scoreless first in sixteen of twenty-three starts, that he strikes out better than a third of the hitters he faces in that frame, and that Milwaukee has not scored on him in the first in either July meeting. Against a Brewers lineup hitting .237 with a .685 OPS versus left-handers, Skenes has the advantage of being right-handed and facing a group whose leadoff sequence has to earn its way on.

Two units. Not three, because -150 is a real price and the base rates behind it are thinner than the pitcher-level splits suggest.

Under 7: The Lowest Number On The Board

Seven is not a normal August total. Across the fifteen games tonight the median is 8.5, and the spread from this game to Coors Field is four and a half runs. When a book cuts that far below the median it is pricing pitching, park and lineup all at once, and here all three point the same direction.

Milwaukee's staff is the second best in baseball at a 3.47 ERA with a 1.16 WHIP and a .219 opponent average. Inside this building it is better still: a 3.11 ERA across 527 innings. Brewers starters as a group hold hitters to .212. Skenes, for his part, has a 1.11 WHIP and a .230 opponent average, and neither of these lineups is well suited to the handedness it is about to see.

The number itself carries a bonus that a 7.5 would not. Seven is a whole number, so a 7-6 or 4-3 final pushes the bet rather than losing it. Brewers games have landed exactly on seven runs seventeen times in 113 games this season, which is a meaningful slice of protection at a price of -105.

One and a half units rather than two, because the raw scoring history on both clubs argues the other way and that argument is addressed below rather than buried.

Pirates Under 3.5: Ten And Twenty-Three Against Lefties

Pittsburgh has scored 584 runs, third most in baseball. That is the first thing anyone will throw at this play and it is a fair thing to throw. It is also a number built almost entirely at home.

On the road the Pirates hit .243 with a .704 OPS against .272 and .794 at PNC Park, a ninety-point swing. Against left-handed pitching they hit .246 with a .693 OPS and carry a 10-23 record. Put those two filters together, which is exactly what tonight does, and Pittsburgh is 6-13 on the road against left-handed starters.

The lineup is also short. Oneil Cruz is on the 60-day injured list with non-displaced fractures of the fourth and fifth metacarpals in his left hand. Ryan O'Hearn, who has 17 home runs, 69 RBI and a .788 OPS, is on the 10-day list with a left quad strain. What remains is Bryan Reynolds at .268 with an .824 OPS, Brandon Lowe with 23 homers, and Nick Gonzales at .306 with a .398 slugging mark that produces singles rather than crooked numbers.

They face the second-best staff in the sport in its best environment. Milwaukee's home ERA is 3.11, its rotation holds hitters to .212, and its relievers have a 3.56 ERA across 437.1 innings. One unit, because the road scoring history does not clear the price on its own and this is the least certain of the three.

The Part Nobody Prices: Two Broken Bullpens

Both relief corps arrive damaged, and that cuts both ways, which is worth saying plainly.

Milwaukee is without JoJo Romero and Joel Kuhnel on the 15-day list, Kuhnel with a right shoulder impingement, plus Angel Zerpa and Rob Zastryzny on the 60-day list. The rotation is also missing Brandon Woodruff and Quinn Priester. Harrison is coming off twenty-eight days on the injured list with left forearm tightness and has not pitched since July 8, so four or five innings is the realistic assignment. Milwaukee has to cover the rest with a thinned group.

Pittsburgh's pen is the shakier one on paper. Pirates relievers own a 4.29 ERA and 219 walks in 449 innings, the highest walk total of any unit in this game, and Evan Sisk is on the 15-day list with left elbow inflammation while Chris Devenski is on the 60-day list. Skenes has not gone past five and a third in either of his last two starts.

Neither of those facts helps a low total on its own. What supports it is that Milwaukee's remaining relievers have still produced a 3.56 ERA, and that Pittsburgh's offense on the road against a lefty is the least likely group on the schedule to punish a middle-relief inning.

What Can Beat This Card

The season-long base rates. This is the honest problem with all three plays and it should be stated first rather than last. Pirates games have finished with no run in the first inning 61 times in 115, a rate of 53.0 percent. Brewers games have done it 60 times in 113, or 53.1 percent. A -150 price needs 60 percent to break even. The pitcher-level splits are what push this bet over that line, and if you do not believe Harrison's 2.65 first-inning ERA and Skenes' sixteen scoreless firsts outweigh the team histories, the number is against you.

Skenes' own first inning is the sharpest version of that risk. He has thrown 22.2 opening frames this year and allowed fourteen runs in them, a 5.16 ERA, with eighteen hits and nine walks. That is his worst inning by a wide margin, and Milwaukee has drawn 467 walks as a team, more than any club in this matchup. If he starts the game the way he started July 31 at Cincinnati, when he walked four and gave up five earned in four innings, the first leg is gone before anyone sits down.

The full-game under has the same problem in a different shape. Brewers games have gone under seven runs 40 times in 113 and Pirates games 35 times in 115. Both clubs play over this number more often than under it, and Pittsburgh's 5.08 runs per game plus Milwaukee's 4.97 add up to a shade under ten, not seven. The bet needs the pitching matchup and the park to beat the season averages by three full runs.

The team total is the thinnest of the three. Pittsburgh has been held to three runs or fewer in 23 of 58 road games, a rate of 39.7 percent, against a -130 price that needs 56.5 percent. The Pirates have also beaten Milwaukee six times in eight meetings this year, including a 14-5 afternoon on July 12, and they scored four in the series opener on Monday. This leg rests entirely on the handedness split holding up.

Last risk, and it is structural: all four and a half units are on one game across three correlated markets. A three-run first inning kills every one of them at the same time.

The Bottom Line

NRFI at -150 takes two units on Harrison's 2.65 first-inning ERA, his .190 opponent average in that frame, and a Pittsburgh lineup that is 10-23 against left-handed starters. Under 7 at -105 takes a unit and a half on a Milwaukee home staff ERA of 3.11, a Brewers rotation holding hitters to .212, and a whole number that pushes on seven. Pittsburgh under 3.5 at -130 takes one unit on a road offense hitting .243 with a .704 OPS, missing Oneil Cruz and Ryan O'Hearn, against the second-best pitching staff in baseball.

Four and a half units, one game, three correlated markets, and no attempt to pretend the base rates agree.

Card FAQ

What are the three plays?

No runs in the first inning at -150 for 2 units, the game under 7 runs at -105 for 1.5 units, and the Pittsburgh Pirates team total under 3.5 at -130 for 1 unit. Four and a half units, all on Pirates at Brewers, 7:40 PM ET at American Family Field.

Who is on the mound?

Paul Skenes for Pittsburgh against Kyle Harrison for Milwaukee. Skenes is 9-9 with a 3.90 ERA, a 1.11 WHIP and 156 strikeouts in 124.2 innings. Harrison is 8-2 with a 3.01 ERA and 101 strikeouts in 83.2 innings, returning after 28 days on the injured list with left forearm tightness.

Why the first-inning under specifically?

Harrison has a 2.65 ERA and a .190 opponent average across 17 first innings with thirteen scoreless starts, and Pittsburgh is 10-23 against left-handed starters while hitting .246 with a .693 OPS off them.

How can a top-three scoring offense be held under 3.5?

Pittsburgh scores at home. On the road the Pirates hit .243 with a .704 OPS, they are 6-13 away from home against left-handed starters, and they are without Oneil Cruz and Ryan O'Hearn against a Milwaukee staff with a 3.11 home ERA.

What is the biggest risk?

Base rates and correlation. Pirates games produce no first-inning run 53.0 percent of the time and Brewers games 53.1 percent, both short of the 60 percent a -150 price needs, and all four and a half units sit on one game.
