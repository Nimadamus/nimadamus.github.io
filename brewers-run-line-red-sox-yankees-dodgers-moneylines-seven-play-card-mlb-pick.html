MLB Free Pick of the Day

Thirteen Units On Six Games Where The Missing Bats Decide It

August 9, 2026|10 min read|BetLegend

Byron Buxton leads the Minnesota Twins in home runs with 25, and he is on the ten-day injured list. Joe Ryan is on the fifteen-day list. Pablo Lopez is on the sixty-day list. What Minnesota is bringing to American Family Field tonight is a 58-60 club that has lost the three players it could least afford to lose, and the man waiting on the mound has held opposing hitters to a .145 batting average across 127 innings.

That is the shape of this whole card.

Six games, seven plays, thirteen units. In five of them one side is missing bats that the price has not fully swallowed. Oakland is without Brent Rooker, Nick Kurtz, Shea Langeliers and Zack Gelof. San Francisco is without Matt Chapman, Harrison Bader, Casey Schmitt and Marcelo Mayer. Arizona is without Corbin Burnes, Zac Gallen and Lourdes Gurriel Jr. Detroit is without Kerry Carpenter, Matt Vierling and Parker Meadows. Those are not depth pieces. Those are middles of orders and fronts of rotations.

The sixth game is the exception, and it is the opposite problem. Nobody is missing for Colorado at St. Louis. The man on the mound simply cannot get anybody out.

Brewers Run Line -1: Three Units On A 1.63 ERA

Jacob Misiorowski has made 21 starts this season. He is 11-5 with a 1.63 ERA, a 0.73 WHIP and 195 strikeouts against 29 walks in 127 innings. Opposing hitters are batting .145 against him. That is not a small-sample number anymore, and the strikeout rate of 13.82 per nine is the highest of any starter working today.

Milwaukee is 73-44, the best record in baseball, and 40-22 at American Family Field. The pitching staff sits second in the majors at 3.48 with a .220 opponent average. The offense scores 4.97 runs a game, fifth best. There is no soft spot on this roster tonight.

Minnesota counters with Connor Prielipp, who is 3-5 with a 4.75 ERA and a 1.31 WHIP in 15 starts. He misses bats, 81 strikeouts in 77.2 innings, but he has given up nine home runs and the Twins staff behind him carries a 4.60 ERA that ranks 26th. Minnesota has allowed 584 runs, which works out to 4.95 a night. They are 28-33 away from Target Field and 4-6 in their last ten.

Then subtract Buxton and his 25 home runs.

THE PLAY

Milwaukee Brewers RUN LINE -1 (-198)
3 units | Ticket TMR-0004115 | Win by two or more. A one-run Brewers win is a PUSH, not a loss.

The alternate -1 line is the point of this bet. At -1.5 you need two runs and a one-run win kills you. At -1 you still need two runs to cash, but the one-run win gives the stake back instead of taking it. Paying -198 instead of a shorter price buys that insurance, and against a team that has scored three or fewer in a meaningful share of its road games, the one-run outcome is exactly the outcome to protect against.

The honest problem: Milwaukee has hit only 107 home runs, fewest of any club currently leading a division. This is a team that strings singles together, and the market has set the total at 7. A low total means fewer runs to spread around, which is the environment where a 2-1 or 3-2 Brewers night becomes very live. That is the push, not the win. Three units says the strikeout gap is wide enough to survive it.

Red Sox Moneyline: Three Units On A Bullpen Game

Boston is 64-52 and has won nine of its last ten. The staff ERA of 3.51 is third best in baseball. The Athletics are 46-71, have gone 1-9 across their last ten, and own a 5.45 staff ERA that is second worst in the majors with 671 runs allowed in 117 games.

Oakland's lineup is a wreck. Brent Rooker is on the sixty-day list. Nick Kurtz, Shea Langeliers and Zack Gelof are all on the ten-day list. Luis Severino and Mark Leiter Jr. are gone from the pitching staff for the season. J.T. Ginn is the one thing working: 8-6, 3.51, 105 strikeouts in 115.1 innings, a .212 opponent average.

And here is where it gets uncomfortable. Boston's listed starter is Erik Miller, who has made zero starts in 2026. Thirty-five innings, all in relief, a 2.57 ERA and 49 strikeouts. This is an opener. Boston is running a bullpen game and asking you to lay -230.

THE PLAY

Boston Red Sox Moneyline (-230)
3 units | Ticket TMR-0004111

That price implies roughly 69.7 percent before the book's margin comes out. It is a lot to lay on a team that is 29-30 at Fenway Park while going 35-22 on the road, and Boston is missing Roman Anthony, Trevor Story, Triston Casas and Garrett Crochet. Nothing about the price is a gift.

What justifies three units is the other dugout. A team that has lost nine of ten with the middle of its order in street clothes, throwing to a pen that has surrendered 671 runs, does not need a conventional starting pitcher opposite it. Boston's relief corps is the third-best run prevention unit in the sport. The bullpen game is the plan, not the accident.

Detroit At San Francisco: Two Plays, One Quiet Ballpark

Troy Melton has 12 starts, a 1.58 ERA, a 0.91 WHIP and a .170 opponent batting average across 74 innings. Logan Webb has thrown 125 innings and allowed nine home runs all season, with a 1.11 WHIP and a .230 opponent average. Two arms, one ballpark that has never been kind to fly balls, and a total set at 7.5.

San Francisco scores 4.14 runs a game, 25th in baseball, and the roster is shredded. Matt Chapman, Harrison Bader, Casey Schmitt, Marcelo Mayer, Victor Bericoto and Jesus Rodriguez are all on the injured list. That is six position players.

Detroit is missing three of its own: Kerry Carpenter, Matt Vierling and Parker Meadows. The Tigers score 4.58 a game across the full season, and they are 27-33 away from Comerica Park. Facing a pitcher whose entire profile is soft contact and no home runs, in a park that swallows the ones that do get hit, four runs is a real ask.

Getting plus money on the Tigers under 3.5 is the better of the two. The market is treating four Detroit runs as close to a coin flip against a starter who has allowed nine home runs in 20 outings.

Be clear about what this is, though. Three units on the same game is correlated exposure. If Detroit puts up five in the third inning, both tickets die together. That is a deliberate choice rather than an oversight, but it is the concentration risk on this card, and San Francisco's staff ERA of 4.40 ranks 22nd, which is the mechanism that could donate late runs and break the game under after the starters have both done their jobs.

Dodgers Moneyline: The Best Road Team In Baseball

Los Angeles is 37-24 away from Dodger Stadium. No club in the majors has a better road record. The offense scores 5.03 runs a game, fourth best, and the run differential of plus 140 is tied for the largest in baseball.

Justin Wrobleski has quietly put together the season nobody talks about: 11-3, a 3.31 ERA, a 1.07 WHIP and 91 strikeouts against just 24 walks in 117 innings. Twenty-four walks in eighteen starts is the number that matters against a Chase Field crowd that punishes free baserunners.

Arizona is 62-56 and 34-25 at home, which is the counterweight. Eduardo Rodriguez has been better than Wrobleski on the raw line, 10-4 with a 2.71 ERA over 136.1 innings, though his 45 walks are nearly double Wrobleski's total. What tilts it is who Arizona does not have. Corbin Burnes, Zac Gallen, Ryne Nelson, A.J. Puk and Justin Martinez are all on the sixty-day list, and Lourdes Gurriel Jr. and Jordan Lawlar are on the ten-day. That rotation and that bullpen have been rebuilt on the fly all summer.

The counterpoint is real and it is recent form. Los Angeles is 3-7 in its last ten. Blake Snell, Tyler Glasnow and Will Smith are all on the sixty-day list, and Dalton Rushing is on the ten-day, which leaves the catching position thin. A team that has lost seven of ten is not a team you back with conviction. This is 1.5 units, not three, for exactly that reason.

Yankees Moneyline: Schlittler Against A Cooling Atlanta

Cam Schlittler is 10-6 with a 2.26 ERA, a 0.95 WHIP and 171 strikeouts in 139.2 innings across 24 starts. Opponents hit .199 against him. He pitches in front of the best staff in baseball, a New York group whose 3.33 ERA leads all thirty clubs and whose 447 runs allowed is the lowest total in the majors.

Atlanta is 70-47 and has gone 8-2 in its last ten, but the Braves have dropped their last two. Grant Holmes takes the ball at 3.67 with a 1.34 WHIP, and the number that jumps is 18 home runs allowed in 108 innings. Yankee Stadium is not the park where you want that trend to continue.

THE PLAY

New York Yankees Moneyline (-137)
1.5 units | Ticket TMR-0004112

Now the part that keeps this at a unit and a half. New York is playing without Aaron Judge, Cody Bellinger and Giancarlo Stanton, and Carlos Rodon is on the fifteen-day list. The Yankees are hitting .231 as a team, 28th in baseball. They are also 29-25 at home against 37-26 on the road, which is a strange split for a club with this pitching. Atlanta scores 4.91 a game, sixth best in the sport.

This is a bet on Schlittler and the bullpen behind him holding a good offense to three, and on New York scratching out four. It is not a bet on the Yankee lineup, because there is not much of a Yankee lineup right now.

Rockies And Cardinals Over 9: The Play Our Own Model Hates

Michael Lorenzen has thrown 107.2 innings this season. Opposing hitters are batting .336 against him. His ERA is 6.94, his WHIP is 1.85, and he has allowed 17 home runs while walking 43. He starts tonight at Busch Stadium.

Behind him is a Colorado pitching staff with a 5.48 ERA, the worst in baseball, and 671 runs allowed. Colorado is 46-71 and 19-39 on the road. But the Rockies can hit: 564 runs, 4.82 a game, seventh in the majors. St. Louis counters with Michael McGreevy, who has been solid at 3.59 with a 1.20 WHIP, though he has also served up 16 home runs in 120.1 innings.

THE PLAY

Rockies/Cardinals game total OVER 9 (-120)

Here is the disagreement, stated plainly because hiding it would be worse than losing the bet. BetLegend's own Atlas team-total model projects St. Louis for 3.92 runs and preferred the Cardinals under 4.5. If that projection is right, this over needs Colorado to score six at Busch Stadium, and the Rockies are 19-39 away from Coors Field. That is the hole, and it is a real one.

The case for the over is Lorenzen and only Lorenzen. A .336 opponent average is not a rough patch, it is 23 starts of evidence, and the relief arms behind him belong to the worst run prevention staff in the sport. Colorado's offense is genuinely better than its record. Ten combined runs is one bad Lorenzen inning plus a normal Cardinals night.

One unit. Smallest play on the card. That sizing is the honest answer to a model that says no.

What Can Beat This Card

Three things, in order of danger.

First, the Boston price. Laying -230 with an opener against a legitimate starter in J.T. Ginn is the largest single risk here. Oakland has won exactly one of its last ten, and the one time a team like that wakes up is the night the market has priced it for dead.

Second, the Detroit correlation. Two tickets, three units, one game. A four-run Tigers inning takes both. Oracle Park and two low-ERA starters make that unlikely, but unlikely is not the same as hedged.

Third, the Yankee lineup. Judge, Bellinger and Stanton are all out and the team is hitting .231. Schlittler can throw seven shutout innings and New York can still lose 1-0. That is the version of this bet nobody enjoys.

The Bottom Line

Thirteen units across six games, and the two biggest positions are the two clearest ones. Milwaukee has the best record in baseball and the best strikeout arm on the slate, against a Minnesota club without its leading home run hitter. Boston has won nine of ten against a team that has won one of ten with half its order unavailable.

Everything else is sized down on purpose. The Dodgers are 3-7 in their last ten. The Yankees cannot hit. The Rockies over runs against our own projection. Those all get a unit or a unit and a half, because conviction should show up in the number on the ticket, not in the adjectives around it.
