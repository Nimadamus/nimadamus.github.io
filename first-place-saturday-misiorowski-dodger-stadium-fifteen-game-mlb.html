MLB - Saturday, August 15

Fifteen Games, And The Best Record In The League Is Decided In Chavez Ravine

Fifteen games. Milwaukee and Los Angeles finish a weekend series with the National League's best record between them and Jacob Misiorowski's 0.74 WHIP on the mound, FOX takes three games at 7:15, Tampa Bay brings a 9-1 stretch back to the Trop, Troy Melton carries twenty consecutive scoreless innings into Comerica Park, and Colorado has to solve Logan Webb in a ballpark where it has scored fourteen runs in five tries. Analysis only, no picks.

Twenty Innings Without An Earned Run

White Sox at Tigers

Saturday, 1:10 PM ET | Comerica Park, Detroit, MI

Troy Melton has made thirteen big league starts and the last three produced nothing at all. Seven innings against Baltimore on July 28, seven against Seattle on August 4, six against San Francisco on August 9, and zero earned runs in any of them. His season now sits at 7-1 with a 1.46 ERA, a 0.90 WHIP and a .171 opponent average across 80 innings, the lowest ERA of any starter working Saturday.

Chicago answers with Anthony Kay, 9-5 with a 3.96 ERA, a 1.34 WHIP and 94 strikeouts in 116 innings, coming off six innings of two-run ball against Cleveland on August 8. The White Sox lead the AL Central at 63-58 on the strength of a 37-24 home record and a lineup that lives on the long ball: 161 home runs from Munetaka Murakami's 27, Miguel Vargas's 27 and Colson Montgomery's 26, attached to a .239 team average and 1,102 strikeouts.

Friday said the quiet part loudly. Chicago walked into Comerica and won 9-5, taking a 4-3 lead in the season series, which is the reminder that a boom-or-bust lineup only needs one inning to make a 1.46 ERA irrelevant. Detroit sits 60-62 and 3.5 games back with a 3.52 staff ERA, and the Tigers keep running into the same problem: Dillon Dingler's 26 home runs and 81 RBI carry an offense averaging 4.57 runs a game that goes missing on the days the pitching is at its best.

The Loudest Lineup In The National League

Cardinals at Cubs

Saturday, 2:20 PM ET | Wrigley Field, Chicago, IL

Chicago is 72-51 and 8-2 in its last ten, three games behind Milwaukee in the Central and holding a seven-game cushion in the wild card race. The Cubs score 5.18 runs a game, the highest figure of any National League club playing Saturday, with a .756 team OPS, 156 home runs and 524 walks drawn. Wrigley has been the engine at 36-24.

Matthew Boyd has been the most efficient version of himself in years: 8-1 with a 3.50 ERA, a 1.22 WHIP and 66 strikeouts against 23 walks in 79.2 innings across fourteen starts. The counterweight sits behind him, where a Cubs staff carrying a 4.07 ERA has surrendered 182 home runs, more than any team on the board.

St. Louis arrives 61-61 and 6-4 in its last ten, hanging three and a half games off the last wild card spot with a 29-27 road record that outperforms its 32-34 mark at Busch. Michael McGreevy's 4-9 record undersells a 3.64 ERA, a 1.22 WHIP and only 32 walks in 126 innings. The Cardinals score 4.34 a game with a .692 team OPS, which makes an afternoon at Wrigley against the league's hottest offense a game they need their starter to keep at four runs or fewer.

The Widest Rotation Gap On The Board

Yankees at Blue Jays

Saturday, 3:07 PM ET | Rogers Centre, Toronto, ON

Cam Schlittler has 182 strikeouts and 31 walks in 146.2 innings. His 2.21 ERA and 0.93 WHIP lead a New York staff whose 3.27 team ERA is the best of any club playing Saturday, and his August 9 start against Atlanta was seven innings, one run, eleven strikeouts and no walks. On July 24 he struck out twelve Phillies over seven and a third scoreless.

Toronto counters with an opener. Braydon Fisher has seven starts on his ledger and the last four of them lasted 1, 1.1, 1 and 1.1 innings, which makes this a bullpen game dressed as a rotation matchup. The Blue Jays have the arms for it, with 42 saves and a 3.99 staff ERA, but they are asking five or six innings of relief work against a lineup carrying 170 home runs.

The standings say New York should win comfortably and the last ten days say otherwise. Toronto is 7-3 in its last ten and beat the Yankees 3-1 on Friday to level the season series at 4-4. The Blue Jays also score 3.83 runs a game at Rogers Centre with a .688 home OPS, the quietest home offense in this game, and New York hits .228 with a .690 OPS on the road. Two clubs going in opposite directions arrive at a game neither side's numbers make obvious.

Webb And The Ballpark That Keeps Swallowing Colorado

Rockies at Giants

Saturday, 4:05 PM ET | Oracle Park, San Francisco, CA

Colorado has scored fourteen runs in five games at Oracle Park this season: two, four, two, one and five. The Rockies are 22-41 on the road and the split behind that number is the widest in the National League, .272 and 5.19 runs a game at Coors Field against .238 and 4.38 everywhere else, with a road OPS of .706. Hunter Goodman's 34 home runs and .861 OPS are the one thing that travels.

Logan Webb is the reason the ballpark keeps behaving. He is 7-7 with a 3.59 ERA, a 1.08 WHIP and 103 strikeouts against 32 walks in 133 innings, with nine home runs allowed all season and a 3.58 ERA at home. Over his last four starts he has given up seven earned runs in 26 innings, including eight innings of one-run ball against Detroit on August 9 and six shutout innings against Texas on August 3.

Michael Lorenzen has the ugliest line of any starter on the slate. He is 3-10 with a 6.83 ERA, a 1.88 WHIP and a .340 opponent average across 24 starts, and his last four outings ran 3.2, 4.0, 3.0 and 4.1 innings with eighteen earned runs between them. San Francisco is 50-72 and has lost seven of ten, scoring 3.93 a game at home behind Rafael Devers's 24 homers, so the Giants need Lorenzen to be exactly what the numbers say he is.

The Highest-Scoring Team In Baseball Visits Queens

Nationals at Mets

Saturday, 4:10 PM ET | Citi Field, Queens, NY

Washington averages 5.35 runs a game with 177 home runs and a .766 team OPS, the loudest offense of any club in action Saturday, and the Nationals are still 60-64 because the staff hands it all back. Their 4.72 team ERA and 1.38 WHIP are the worst marks on the board, and 174 home runs allowed sit near the top of the same list.

Brad Lord has been the exception in that bullpen-heavy staff, working to a 3.79 ERA and a 1.18 WHIP with 56 strikeouts in 61.2 innings across mostly relief work and two starts. Washington's road record of 33-28 is meaningfully better than its 27-36 mark at Nationals Park, which is a strange way to run a season and a real reason this trip matters.

New York is 54-69 and buried in the East, yet 7-3 in its last ten and playing its best baseball since June. Sean Manaea has 106 strikeouts in 104.2 innings with a 4.13 ERA, and the Mets have 150 home runs attached to a .235 team average. FS1 has the national window, and the pairing of the sport's most productive lineup against a Mets club finally winning games gives the 8.0 total something to work with.

Baseball's Best Record Comes Home

Orioles at Rays

Saturday, 6:10 PM ET | Tropicana Field, St. Petersburg, FL

Tampa Bay is 74-47 and 9-1 in its last ten, the best record in baseball by winning percentage and a club that has turned Tropicana Field into a fortress at 41-19. The Rays hit .260 as a team, the highest average of anyone playing Saturday, and their 48 saves and 3.72 staff ERA are both at the top of the board.

Ian Seymour has been the rotation's late-season find. He is 9-3 with a 4.08 ERA, a 1.13 WHIP and 106 strikeouts in 90.1 innings, a rate of 10.56 per nine, holding hitters to a .216 average across nine starts. The thirteen home runs allowed in that sample are the flaw, and Baltimore hits enough of them to matter.

The Orioles are 59-63 and 27-33 on the road, one game out of the last wild card spot in a jammed American League, with 145 home runs and 1,118 strikeouts describing the shape of the lineup exactly. Kyle Bradish gives them a real chance at 7-11 with a 3.69 ERA and 121 strikeouts in 127 innings, though his 54 walks are the most of any starter in this game and the Rays draw enough free passes to punish it.

Two Lineups That Only Work In One Direction

Marlins at Reds

Saturday, 6:40 PM ET | Great American Ball Park, Cincinnati, OH

Cincinnati hits .227 as a team, the lowest average of any club playing Saturday, and has 158 home runs anyway. That is the entire Reds season in one sentence: 1,145 strikeouts, a .701 OPS, and a lineup that scores 4.11 a game in bursts. They are 59-62 and 6-4 in their last ten with two straight wins.

Brady Singer is the reason the 9.0 total is the highest number in this window outside Sacramento. He is 5-11 with a 4.66 ERA and a 1.43 WHIP, and the 22 home runs he has allowed in 119.2 innings are the most of any starter on the board. Opponents are hitting .274 against him, and Great American Ball Park has never been the place to fix that.

Miami is 62-61 and remains one of the most location-dependent teams in the sport, 38-25 at loanDepot park against 24-36 on the road. Ryan Gusto takes the ball at 0-3 with a 4.78 ERA in ten starts covering 49 innings, a swing man being asked to hold a lineup that lives on the home run. The Marlins score 4.35 a game behind a 3.96 staff ERA that is the healthier of the two here.

Six Straight Wins Against A Two-And-Eight Slide

Padres at Guardians

Saturday, 7:10 PM ET | Progressive Field, Cleveland, OH

San Diego has won six in a row and eight of ten, climbing to 66-57 and a full game clear in the National League wild card race. The Padres do it with pitching and margins: a 4.09 staff ERA, 37 saves, and an offense scoring 4.30 a game with a .239 team average. Their road record of 29-31 is the one number that has not caught up with the run.

San Diego had not announced a starter for this one as of Saturday morning, which leaves Cleveland's side of the matchup carrying the preview. Joey Cantillo is 8-7 with a 3.91 ERA and 132 strikeouts in 119.2 innings, a strikeout-per-inning arm whose 56 walks and 1.48 WHIP explain why the ERA sits where it does rather than lower.

The Guardians are 59-64 and have lost eight of their last ten, the coldest stretch of any club playing Saturday. They score 3.97 runs a game with a .233 average and a .683 OPS, and 119 home runs is the second-lowest total on the board. The pitching remains legitimate at a 3.86 staff ERA with 1,139 strikeouts, which is why Cleveland keeps losing games by two rather than by six.

Luzardo's 176 Strikeouts Against A Rookie Left-Hander

Phillies at Twins

Saturday, 7:10 PM ET | Target Field, Minneapolis, MN

Jesus Luzardo has been the quiet ace of a Philadelphia staff that has struck out 1,208 hitters, more than any team in baseball. He is 10-5 with a 3.32 ERA, a 1.14 WHIP and 176 strikeouts in 143.2 innings, a rate of 11.03 per nine, with a .225 opponent average across 24 starts.

Minnesota counters with Connor Prielipp, 3-5 with a 4.79 ERA, a 1.32 WHIP and 88 strikeouts in 82.2 innings over sixteen starts. The Twins are 60-63 and half a game out of the final wild card spot, carrying a 4.62 staff ERA that has been the drag on the season all summer despite an offense scoring 4.65 a game with 144 home runs.

Philadelphia is 65-58 and 33-28 on the road, the better of its two splits, with 152 home runs and a 4.18 staff ERA. The Phillies are eight and a half games behind Atlanta in the East and holding the top wild card position, so the difference between a 4.41-run night and a quiet one at Target Field carries weight in a race with four clubs inside two games.

The AL West Leader Against The Quietest Bats On The Board

Mariners at Astros

Saturday, 7:10 PM ET | Daikin Park, Houston, TX

Seattle scores 3.87 runs a game with a .229 team average, the second-quietest offense of any club playing Saturday, and the Mariners have lost eight of their last ten to fall to 57-66 and 24-37 on the road. Emerson Hancock has been better than the record suggests at 6-7 with a 3.35 ERA, a 1.12 WHIP and only 30 walks in 121 innings.

Houston leads the AL West at 63-60 with two straight wins, scoring 4.63 a game behind 164 home runs. The staff is the problem and the number is stark: a 4.55 team ERA with 508 walks issued, the most of any club in action, which is a difficult way to hold leads in a division race decided by three games.

Hayden Wesneski starts for the Astros in what amounts to a rebuilt season. He is 2-0 with a 3.86 ERA across three starts and 18.2 innings, a small sample that tells you almost nothing except that Houston is comfortable handing him a division game. Against a lineup hitting .229 that may be all the margin the Astros need, and Seattle's 1,051 strikeouts suggest the same.

Fourteen Wins Against A Home ERA Near Six

Red Sox at Pirates

Saturday, 7:15 PM ET | PNC Park, Pittsburgh, PA

Sonny Gray is 14-3 with a 2.79 ERA and a 1.15 WHIP, 110 strikeouts and 33 walks in 125.2 innings across 22 starts, and the fourteen wins lead every pitcher on the board. His last month has been uneven in an interesting way: five earned in five innings against Toronto on July 25, then four in seven against the Athletics, then six shutout innings against the White Sox on August 5, then two earned in six against Toronto again on August 10.

Jared Jones has the opposite problem. His 5.03 ERA is bad enough, but the split is worse: a 5.82 ERA at PNC Park against 3.96 on the road, and a last start on August 9 that ended after three innings and eight earned runs against the Mets. Ten home runs in 59 innings is a lot for a pitcher with a 1.20 WHIP, which is the signature of an arm that gets hit hard rather than often.

Pittsburgh scores 5.44 runs a game at PNC Park with a .272 average and a .794 OPS at home, the loudest home offense of any National League club playing Saturday, and the Pirates just beat Boston 8-4 on Friday. Boston is the reverse, 36-26 on the road against 29-31 at Fenway, with 4.58 runs a game away from home and a 3.51 staff ERA. FOX has this one nationally at 7:15.

Two Sub-Three ERAs, One Division Leader

Diamondbacks at Braves

Saturday, 7:15 PM ET | Truist Park, Atlanta, GA

Eduardo Rodriguez is having the best season of his Arizona tenure at 11-4 with a 2.70 ERA and a 1.23 WHIP across 143.1 innings, with 101 strikeouts and 47 walks in 24 starts. The Diamondbacks are 65-58 and hold a wild card position, scoring 4.50 a game with a .713 team OPS and the fewest strikeouts recorded by any staff on the board at 839.

Atlanta is 73-49, the best record in the National League East by eight and a half games, and 41-22 at Truist Park. The Braves score 4.84 a game with 164 home runs and hold a 3.59 staff ERA, a balance no other club in this game can claim. Grant Holmes takes the ball at 7-4 with a 3.47 ERA, though his 48 walks and eighteen home runs allowed in 114 innings are the loose threads.

The 9.0 total is the joint-highest of the evening outside Sacramento, which is a strange price for a game featuring a 2.70 ERA and a 3.47 ERA. The explanation sits in the walks. Rodriguez and Holmes have issued 95 free passes between them, and two lineups with a combined 283 home runs punish traffic in a way that box scores rarely forgive. FOX carries this one alongside the other two 7:15 games.

Marquee Matchup - First Place In The National League
FOX / FOX ONE (national)

Brewers at Dodgers

Saturday, 7:15 PM ET | Dodger Stadium, Los Angeles, CA

The two best records in the National League close their weekend series with one game between them: Milwaukee at 75-48, Los Angeles at 74-49. This is Saturday's BetLegend featured game, with the full breakdown here. FOX and FOX ONE carry it nationally at 7:15 ET, and the game total of 7.5 is the lowest number on the fifteen-game board.

Jacob Misiorowski is the reason for that total. He is 11-5 with a 1.76 ERA, a 0.74 WHIP and 204 strikeouts in 133 innings, holding hitters to a .147 average across 22 starts. He already beat this lineup once, on August 2, when he threw seven innings and allowed two runs on two hits with ten strikeouts. A week before that he struck out twelve Rockies while giving up one hit in five innings.

Justin Wrobleski carries an 11-4 record and a 3.44 ERA into the matchup, but his last two starts produced ten earned runs in seven and two-thirds innings, against the Cubs on August 3 and Arizona on August 9. Behind him a Dodgers bullpen missing Tyler Glasnow, Blake Treinen, Brusdar Graterol and Ben Casparius has to cover whatever he cannot. Milwaukee won Thursday 5-4, Los Angeles answered 3-1 on Friday, and the Dodgers lead the season series 3-2.

Two Clubs Playing Out The String In Anaheim

Royals at Angels

Saturday, 9:38 PM ET | Angel Stadium, Anaheim, CA

Kansas City is 50-73 and 21-43 on the road, the worst travelling record in the sport, with a 4.85 staff ERA and 457 walks issued behind an offense that scores 4.15 a game. Randy Dobnak has been a small bright spot in a swing role, carrying a 2.00 ERA across 36 innings with five starts, though 19 strikeouts in that span is a rate of 4.75 per nine and the 1.33 WHIP tells the fuller story.

Los Angeles sits 48-75 with a lineup that has struck out 1,163 times, more than any club playing Saturday, while hitting .237 with a .688 OPS. The Angels' 18 saves are the fewest in the majors and their pitchers have walked 518 hitters, a combination that turns close games into losses with a consistency few teams can match.

Reid Detmers is the one genuinely interesting arm in the building. He is 3-8 with a 4.00 ERA, a 1.10 WHIP and 156 strikeouts in 135 innings at 10.40 per nine, holding hitters to a .213 average, and the record is a monument to the offense behind him. A late-night game between clubs 14 and 15 games out of a division is where a strikeout total like that gets its only audience.

The Highest Total On The Board In West Sacramento

Rangers at Athletics

Saturday, 9:40 PM ET | Sutter Health Park, West Sacramento, CA

The 9.5 total closing the board is the highest of the day, and the Athletics' home ballpark is most of the reason. Sutter Health Park has produced a .263 average, a .789 OPS and 5.03 runs a game for the home side against .223 and 3.69 on the road, a split so extreme it looks like a typo. The staff on the other side of it holds a 5.48 ERA with a 1.50 WHIP and 192 home runs allowed.

J.T. Ginn has survived the environment better than most, sitting 8-6 with a 3.41 ERA and 107 strikeouts in 121.1 innings. His home numbers still show the strain, a 1.45 WHIP in 54.1 innings there against 1.01 on the road, and his 53 walks keep the bases occupied even on the nights the runs stay off the board.

Texas arrives having lost four straight, including 8-3 to these Athletics on Friday, with two runs scored across the last two games. The Rangers are 60-63 and half a game out of the last wild card spot, hitting .240 with 4.02 runs a game on the road. MacKenzie Gore gives them the better arm on paper: 149 strikeouts in 134 innings with a .229 opponent average, and seven earned runs allowed across his last four starts covering 23.1 innings.
