The split that matters tonight: the Yankees have a .739 OPS against left handed pitching this year and .712 against right handers. Rodriguez is a lefty.

Eduardo Rodriguez, Honestly

Rodriguez has a 2.62 ERA over 30 starts and 178.1 innings. That's a real number, and it's the reason this total is 3.5 instead of 4.5. Over his last six starts he's allowed 9 earned runs in 35 innings.

But there are two cracks worth pointing at. Left handed hitters are batting .267 against him with a .703 OPS, well above the .223 and .649 that right handers manage. And his last time out, against Texas at home on September 13, he gave up eight hits and six runs, three of them earned, in 5.1 innings.

He's also averaging just under six innings a start, 178.1 over 30. That's the part of this ticket I like most.

The Innings After Him

If Rodriguez goes his usual six, the Yankees get about three innings against the Arizona bullpen, which has a 3.92 ERA and 1.23 WHIP this season. That's not a disaster bullpen, but it's a lot more hittable than the man starting the game. A lineup that has hit 209 home runs doesn't need many swings against it to find one run, and one or two runs off Rodriguez plus one or two late gets this ticket home.

The Argument Against This Play

Rodriguez has been excellent. A 2.62 ERA doesn't happen by accident, and if he gives the Yankees six innings like his late August starts against Cincinnati and Chicago, when he allowed zero earned runs in 12.2 innings, four runs gets very hard to find.

The season rate is a coin flip at this price. 56.6 percent against a 56.5 percent break even means the full year alone doesn't carry the bet. It's the road rate and the recent form that do.

The Yankees just scored 2 twice. On September 12 and 13 against the Mets they managed two runs in each game. Hot stretches in baseball end without warning.
