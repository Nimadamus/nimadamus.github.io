Two Starters Under A 3.40 ERA In The Loudest Scoring Park In The National League

Miami Marlins at Washington Nationals, Nationals Park, Featured Game of the Day
Published August 29, 2026

Sandy Alcantara has thrown 183.2 innings this season, more than any other starter working today anywhere in baseball. Cade Cavalli answers with a 3.21 ERA and 10.23 strikeouts per nine. They meet at 4:05 in a ballpark that has averaged 11.10 combined runs across 68 home dates this year, which is the tension that makes this the game to watch.

Matchup Context

Neither club is playing for anything. Miami is 68-67 and twelve games behind Atlanta in the National League East. Washington is 64-73 and seventeen back. Both have gone 4-6 across their last ten and the Nationals took Friday's game 9-2.

What makes this the featured game anyway is the two men on the mound. Sandy Alcantara and Cade Cavalli both carry ERAs under 3.40 into a ballpark that has produced more runs per game this season than any National League venue on Saturday's board. One of those things is going to give.

Miami's separator is the pitching staff rather than the lineup. The Marlins own a 3.92 team ERA, a 1.25 WHIP and a .230 opponent average, which is the fourth lowest mark in baseball. They have allowed 572 runs in 135 games. Their bats are 20th in the sport at 4.33 runs a game.

Washington is the mirror image. The Nationals rank second in all of baseball in scoring at 5.20 runs a game on a .757 team OPS, and they have allowed 694 runs behind a 4.67 staff ERA. That is a club built to win 9-7 and lose 3-2.

The Splits That Frame This Game

The home and road numbers cut against the standings. Miami is 42-27 at loanDepot park and 26-40 away from it, which is a sixteen game swing between the two halves of its schedule. This is the road version of the Marlins.

Washington is the opposite kind of strange. The Nationals are 30-38 at home and 34-35 on the road, one of the few clubs in the sport with a better away record than a home one.

The recent form matters more than either. Across their last fifteen games the Nationals have scored 3.87 runs a game and allowed 3.67, which is not the second best offense in baseball, it is a completely different team. Miami has been just as quiet at 3.87 over the same stretch while allowing 4.20.

The Building Is The Story

Nationals Park has hosted 68 completed home dates this season and they have averaged 11.10 combined runs. Only 16 of those 68 finished under 8, which is 24 percent. Twenty seven of the 68 finished under 9.5.

Put that next to the other buildings hosting games today and the gap is stark. Progressive Field in Cleveland is at 8.27 combined runs across 66 dates. Citi Field is at 8.58. American Family Field is at 8.68. Wrigley Field is at 9.47. Only Sutter Health Park in Sacramento, at 11.44, has been louder than Washington this year.

Part of it is the home team. The Nationals have hit 183 home runs and allowed 188, and the second figure is the third highest total in baseball. A club that both hits and allows that many home runs produces a specific kind of game, and 68 of them have now been played in this park.

The Pitching Match-Up

Sandy Alcantara is 13-8 with a 3.38 ERA and a 1.18 WHIP across 183.2 innings and 28 starts. That innings figure is the most of any starting pitcher in either league taking the ball on Saturday, and it is the argument for him in a game like this: he does not hand the middle innings to anybody. Opponents hit .240 against him and he has issued 49 walks with 18 home runs allowed.

His profile is contact management rather than dominance. Alcantara strikes out 6.71 per nine, which is below the league norm for a front line starter, so the outs come from the defense behind him and from a heavy sinking fastball that produces ground balls. Against a lineup that has hit 183 home runs, the ground ball rate is the entire proposition.

Cade Cavalli is 12-5 with a 3.21 ERA and a 1.21 WHIP over 148.2 innings and 28 starts, and he arrives at this game as the better strikeout arm by a distance. His 10.23 strikeouts per nine is more than three above Alcantara's rate. He has walked 43 and allowed 17 home runs, and opponents hit .243.

Two starters this good in the same game is genuinely rare on a Saturday between clubs this far out of the race, and it is the only reason a 68-67 team playing a 64-73 team is worth an hour of anybody's afternoon.

Who Is Carrying Each Club

Washington's offense runs through CJ Abrams, who has 29 home runs, 92 runs batted in and an .864 OPS, the best mark on either roster by a wide margin. Daylen Lile is second on the club with 18 home runs and 75 runs batted in. After those two the Nationals thin out quickly: Jacob Young, Brady House and Dylan Crews all sit at 10 home runs with on base plus slugging figures between .658 and .710.

Miami spreads it further and lower. Heriberto Hernandez leads the club with 21 home runs and a .783 OPS, Kyle Stowers has 16 with a .786, and Otto Lopez is hitting .307 with a .786 OPS and 59 runs batted in. That is a lineup with no single bat a pitcher has to work around, which is the flip side of having no obvious hole either.

The strikeout numbers are worth a line. Miami's staff has struck out 1,103 batters and Washington's 1,055, so neither pitching group is built on missing bats as a club. Cavalli is the outlier on his own roster rather than the norm on it.

Keys To The Game

Marlins Keys
Keep the ball on the ground. Washington has hit 183 home runs. Alcantara has allowed 18 in 183.2 innings, which is the lowest rate of any starter in this game, and the way this ticket to a low score exists at all.
Let Alcantara go seven. The Miami bullpen is good, a 3.92 staff ERA overall, but the road version of this club is 26-40 and every extra inning in a hitter's park is a coin flip nobody needs.
Solve Cavalli early. Miami has scored 3.87 runs a game across its last fifteen. Against 10.23 strikeouts per nine, waiting until the sixth for a rally is not a plan.
Nationals Keys
Get Abrams on base. Twenty nine home runs and 92 runs batted in on an .864 OPS. He is the only bat in this game that changes a scoreboard by himself.
Make Alcantara throw strikes in the zone. He walks few and pitches to contact. A lineup with 183 home runs gets its chances by attacking the pitches it can lift rather than by drawing free passes that are not coming.
Protect the middle innings. Washington has allowed 694 runs behind a 4.67 staff ERA. Cavalli has been excellent, and the last two weeks of 3.67 runs allowed a game show what happens when the relievers hold.

Market Context

The moneyline is as close as a moneyline gets. Washington is -106 and Miami -109, which implies 51.5 percent and 52.2 percent respectively, and the fact that both numbers sit above 50 is simply the market's cut. This is a pick em game in the eyes of the people setting the price.

The total is 8.0 with the under at -115 and the over at -105. That is the number to sit with. Nationals Park has averaged 11.10 combined runs across 68 home dates and has finished under 8 only 16 times, so the posted total is more than three runs below the season average in this park. The market is pricing the two starters and nothing else.

The run line tells the same story: Miami -1.5 pays +148, which is what a market offers when it thinks the game is close and low scoring rather than lopsided. First five innings has Washington at -129, a shorter price than the full game, which is the market saying it trusts the Nationals bullpen less than the Nationals starter.

Final Thoughts

This is a game about which piece of evidence you believe. The season long park data says 11.10 runs a night in this building and only 24 percent of games under 8. The pitching matchup says two starters with ERAs of 3.38 and 3.21, one of whom has thrown more innings than anybody else pitching today.

The tiebreaker, if there is one, is the last fifteen games. Both offenses have gone quiet, Washington at 3.87 runs a game and Miami at the same figure, and the Nationals have allowed only 3.67 across that run. If that stretch is a real change rather than a slump, the starters win the argument. If it is noise, the building does.
