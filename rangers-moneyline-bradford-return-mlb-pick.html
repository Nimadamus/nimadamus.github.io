MLB Free Pick of the Day

Three Units On A Left-Hander Who Has Not Pitched In Two Years

August 5, 2026|7 min read|BetLegend

San Francisco has played 62 road games this season. The Giants have won 22 of them. Narrow that to the road games where the other club started a left-hander and the record collapses to 4-12. Narrow it again to games played on artificial turf, home or away, and San Francisco is 2-12.

This afternoon the Giants play on turf, on the road, against a left-hander.

That is the whole shape of the play. Texas is not the better baseball team by any season-long measure that matters, and this is not an argument that it is. It is an argument that one club walks into a set of conditions it has failed in all year while the other walks into the conditions it handles best.

The Splits That Built This Number

Start with the road record, because it is the loudest number San Francisco carries. The Giants are 26-27 at Oracle Park and 22-39 everywhere else, a swing of thirteen games in fifty-three fewer plate appearances worth of difference. They are 48-66 overall and twelve games out of a wild card spot with eight weeks left, which is the tidy way of saying the season is finished and the road half of it did the finishing.

Handedness sharpens it. San Francisco hits .246 with a .685 OPS against left-handed pitching and .254 with a .738 mark against right-handers, and the record splits the same way: 14-17 against lefty starters across the season, 10-5 of that at home, 4-12 on the road. Cody Bradford throws left-handed. Texas, meanwhile, is one of the few clubs in the sport that prefers seeing a lefty, batting .252 with a .738 OPS against left-handed pitching versus .243 and .713 against right-handers, and the Rangers are 7-2 at home against left-handed starters. Carson Whisenhunt is also left-handed.

Then there is the surface. Globe Life Field is a turf ballpark. Texas is 35-30 on turf and 21-28 on grass. San Francisco is 2-12 on turf. Fourteen games is a small sample and it is not a coincidence either, because the Giants are built around a contact profile that plays differently on a fast surface with a closed roof.

The Rangers pitching staff also changes shape at home in a way that supports laying a price. Texas has a 3.86 ERA and a 1.21 WHIP at Globe Life Field against a 4.78 ERA and a 1.37 WHIP on the road, with a .233 opponent average at home and .260 away. Same arms, very different results depending on the address, and today they are at the good one.

Cody Bradford, Back From Two Years Away

Bradford has spent the entire 2026 season on the 60-day injured list rehabbing left elbow surgery. He has not thrown a major league pitch since 2024. That is the honest headline on him and it is the reason this play is uncomfortable rather than obvious.

What he did before the surgery is the reason it is playable anyway. In 2024 he made 13 starts, threw 76.1 innings and went 6-3 with a 3.54 ERA, a 1.01 WHIP and 70 strikeouts against 13 walks. Opponents hit .218. A 1.01 WHIP over a half season is not a fluke of sequencing, it is command, and command is the skill that tends to return first from an elbow procedure.

The rehab work backs that up. Bradford made five starts in the Texas system this summer, three at Double-A Frisco and two at Triple-A Round Rock. The combined line is 12 innings, 16 strikeouts and two walks, with a 1.50 ERA at Frisco and a 3.00 ERA at Round Rock. Two walks in twelve innings from a pitcher whose calling card was never issuing them is the specific signal Texas needed before activating him.

Nobody should expect six innings. A first start back after that long is a four or five inning assignment with a hard pitch limit, and the Rangers bullpen carries a 4.36 ERA, which is the weakest link in the whole argument. What Bradford has to do is keep a .246-versus-lefties lineup off the bases long enough to hand a lead over.

Carson Whisenhunt And 5.68 Walks Per Nine

San Francisco counters with a 25-year-old left-hander four starts into his major league career, and the numbers are rough. Whisenhunt is 2-2 with a 6.63 ERA and a 1.74 WHIP across 19 innings, and opponents are hitting .284 against him. He has issued 12 walks in those 19 innings, a rate of 5.68 per nine, the highest of any starter on Wednesday's board.

His last time out was the worst of it. On July 31 against San Diego he recorded ten outs, allowed seven hits and four walks, and gave up six earned runs. Before that he walked four Rockies in five and two-thirds on July 9 and gave the Angels five innings and four earned on July 26. There is one good start in the file, five innings of two-run ball against Atlanta on June 17, and it came seven weeks ago.

Behind him the picture does not improve. Giants starters have walked 236 batters in 614.2 innings, more than any rotation involved in today's early games, and the bullpen has issued another 185 in 389.1 innings with a 4.28 ERA and a 1.41 WHIP. On the road that staff runs a 4.70 ERA with a 1.44 WHIP and a .262 opponent average. Texas does not need to hit well this afternoon. It needs to be patient.

Who Is Missing On Both Sides

San Francisco is missing most of the middle of its order. Third baseman Matt Chapman is on the 10-day injured list with an abdominal strain. Outfielder Harrison Bader is on the 10-day list with left plantar fasciitis. Casey Schmitt, who has 21 home runs and a .784 OPS, is on the 10-day list with a left knee meniscus tear. Marcelo Mayer, Victor Bericoto and catcher Jesus Rodriguez are all on the 10-day list as well. That leaves Luis Arraez at .324, Rafael Devers with 23 homers and a .793 OPS, and Jung Hoo Lee at .305 to carry an offense against a left-hander they have hit .246 off all year.

Texas has its own losses and they are real. Third baseman Josh Jung, the club leader at .294 with an .802 OPS, is on the 10-day list with a left calf strain, and catcher Kyle Higashioka is on the 10-day list. On the pitching side Jack Leiter is on the 15-day list with right ankle posterior impingement, Jakob Junis is on the 15-day list with a left adductor strain, and Jordan Montgomery and Robert Garcia are on the 60-day list. The Rangers are running out a patched roster, which is exactly why Bradford's return matters to them.

What is left in Arlington still functions. Ezequiel Duran is hitting .278 with 13 home runs and 63 RBI, Brandon Nimmo is at .271 with a .774 OPS, and Jake Burger has 19 homers and 66 RBI. Texas has also won 20 of its 32 one-run games this year, the best mark of any club on this afternoon's schedule, which is what a team with a bullpen and a ballpark tends to produce.

What Can Beat This

Bradford beats it. Twelve rehab innings is a thin foundation under a three-unit stake, and the range of outcomes for a pitcher two years removed from major league hitters runs from six shutout innings to four runs in the third. If the velocity is down or the release point wanders, Texas is handing five innings to a bullpen with a 4.36 ERA against a lineup that just scored five runs off the Rangers on Monday.

The Rangers form is the second problem. Texas has lost seven of its last ten, including six in a row before Tuesday's 5-4 win, and got swept in Houston by a combined 23-9 from July 31 to August 2. The offense scores 4.13 runs per game, and the club carries a run differential of minus 39 while sitting one game out of a wild card spot. Laying -156 with a team that has been outscored by 39 runs is not a comfortable position and it should not be dressed up as one.

San Francisco is also not without a pulse. The Giants beat Texas 5-1 in the series opener on Monday, they rank sixth in baseball in runs scored at 479, and they actually hit better on the road than at home, .254 with a .730 OPS away versus .249 and .715 at Oracle Park. Luis Arraez has struck out 21 times in 420 at-bats, which is the profile least likely to be overwhelmed by a rusty arm.

The Bottom Line

Three units on Texas at -156 because the conditions stack in one direction and the price does not reflect it. San Francisco is 22-39 on the road, 4-12 away from home against left-handed starters, 2-12 on turf, and missing Chapman, Bader and Schmitt. Texas is 7-2 at home against lefty starters, owns a 3.86 home staff ERA against 4.78 on the road, and gets a pitcher back whose 2024 season featured a 1.01 WHIP and 13 walks in 76.1 innings.

The risk is named and it is Bradford's rust. The reason to accept it is that everything else on the board points the same way.
