A 1.88 ERA On A Getaway Afternoon

Rays at Tigers

Wednesday, 1:10 PM ET | Comerica Park, Detroit, MI

Detroit is 62-70 and has the second best pitching on this board. The Tigers carry a 3.61 team ERA, have allowed 522 runs and have given up only 135 home runs, the second lowest figure of any club playing today. The offense scores 4.45 a game, which is why the record does not match the run prevention.

Tampa Bay is 78-54 and scores 4.47 a game with a .732 OPS. The Rays staff is at 3.79 with a 1.15 WHIP, the cleanest baserunner rate in this game, but they have allowed 168 home runs, the most of either club here by a wide margin.

Troy Melton is the reason to watch this one. Sixteen starts, 96 innings, a 1.88 ERA and a 1.02 WHIP, with a .203 opponent average, the best line of any starter on today's board. Freddy Peralta answers at 5.14 with a 1.41 WHIP and 22 home runs allowed in 140 innings. Detroit is -117 and the total is 7.5.

Thirteen Wins And A 2.61 In The Desert

Cubs at Diamondbacks

Wednesday, 3:40 PM ET | Chase Field, Phoenix, AZ

Chicago brings the best offense on the entire board. The Cubs score 5.23 runs a game with a .762 OPS and 177 home runs, all three the top marks of any club playing today, and they are 76-57.

Arizona is 70-63 and scores 4.53 with a .715 OPS. The Diamondbacks staff is at 4.21 with a 1.29 WHIP and has allowed 600 runs, so the case for the home side is not the pitching depth. It is the man starting.

Eduardo Rodriguez is 13-4 with a 2.61 ERA across 26 starts and 155 innings, a 1.21 WHIP and a .237 opponent average. Matthew Boyd is 8-2 at 4.15 with 14 home runs allowed in 91 innings. This is the only true pick em on the board at -107 both ways, with the total at 8.5.

Webb At 1.11 Against A 1.52

Reds at Giants

Wednesday, 3:45 PM ET | Oracle Park, San Francisco, CA

These are the two quietest offenses on the board. Cincinnati scores 4.05 a game with a .694 OPS and San Francisco scores 4.02 with a .711, and the Giants have allowed the fewest home runs of any club playing today at 120.

The Reds staff is the problem in this matchup. A 4.52 ERA and a 1.41 WHIP, 639 runs allowed, and the walks that come with it. San Francisco is at 4.30 and 1.35, which is not good either, but Oracle Park has been doing part of that work all season.

Logan Webb has a 1.11 WHIP in 141.2 innings with 112 strikeouts against 35 walks and a .229 opponent average. Nick Lodolo is at a 5.04 ERA with a 1.52 WHIP and a .288 opponent average across 75 innings. San Francisco is -117 and the number is 7.5, the joint lowest total on the board.

A 6.41 ERA Against The Best Strikeout Arm Here

Guardians at Angels

Wednesday, 4:07 PM ET | Angel Stadium, Anaheim, CA

Cleveland is 67-66 with a 3.75 staff ERA and scores 4.05 a game, the same tight profile the Guardians have had all year. The Angels are 52-81 with a 4.28 staff ERA and 619 runs allowed, and score 4.10.

Neither lineup is why the total is 8.5. Both offenses sit under .690 OPS, which is the bottom of this board, and the number is being set by two starters who put men on base.

Joey Cantillo has 144 strikeouts in 130.1 innings, the second highest total on this board, but a 1.45 WHIP and 63 walks with it. Grayson Rodriguez carries a 6.41 ERA and a 1.53 WHIP through 14 starts and 66 innings. Cleveland is -138 and the total is 8.5.

Sixty Six Walks In 125 Innings

Pirates at Padres

Wednesday, 4:10 PM ET | Petco Park, San Diego, CA

Pittsburgh is the better offense on paper at 4.90 runs a game against San Diego's 4.26, and it is the worse team on the board at 65-69 against the Padres at 71-62. The gap is entirely pitching: a 4.23 Pirates staff ERA against 3.97 for San Diego, and 630 runs allowed against 552.

Bubba Chandler is the interesting arm here and the risky one. A 4.38 ERA and a .226 opponent average say the contact against him has been poor, and 66 walks in 125.1 innings says he keeps giving the runs back anyway. That is 4.74 walks per nine, the highest rate of any starter on this board.

Randy Vasquez is at 4.19 with a 1.31 WHIP and has allowed 15 home runs in 118 innings. San Diego is -132 in a park that has suppressed runs all year, and the total is 8.5.
