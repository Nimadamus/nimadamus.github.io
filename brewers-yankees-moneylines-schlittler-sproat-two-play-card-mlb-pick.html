Brewers And Yankees Moneylines On A Two-Play Monday

August 3, 2026|7 min read|BetLegend

Cam Schlittler has a 1.10 ERA in fourteen road starts this season and a 3.83 ERA in nine at Yankee Stadium. Same arm, same summer, same 97.9 mph four-seam fastball. Tonight he pitches at home and this card lays two units on him anyway.

Brandon Sproat has the mirror version of the same problem. His ERA inside American Family Field is 5.98 across eight starts, and it drops to 4.33 in ten starts everywhere else. He pitches at home tonight too, and this card lays a unit and a half behind him.

So here is the honest framing before a single number gets defended. Neither of these bets asks you to trust the building. Both ask you to trust everything standing behind the starter, and in Milwaukee and the Bronx tonight, that happens to be the two most complete rosters in this window.

Brewers -139: The Bullpen Nobody Used

Milwaukee owns the best record in baseball at 69-42 and the second-best home record in the sport at 36-20, behind only Tampa Bay. Pittsburgh has beaten Milwaukee five times in six completed meetings this season. Both of those things are true, and the second one is why this price sits where it sits instead of somewhere north of -170.

Take the head-to-head seriously, because it is not one fluke night. The Pirates won 6-0 and 6-3 in Milwaukee in late April, then swept a July 11 doubleheader in Pittsburgh by 7-6 and 3-2 after the July 10 opener was washed out, then hung a 14-5 beating on the Brewers on July 12. Milwaukee's lone win in the set was a 5-0 shutout on April 26. That is a real pattern and it deserves a real discount.

The discount is already in the number. What is not in the number is what happened in Anaheim over the weekend.

Milwaukee's relievers threw four and two-thirds innings across three full games against the Angels. Shane Drohan went six on Friday, Robert Gasser went five on Saturday, and Jacob Misiorowski went seven on eighty-three pitches Sunday with ten strikeouts. Trevor Megill, who is 18 for 20 in save chances with a 2.83 ERA and a 0.94 WHIP, worked Friday and Saturday and had Sunday off. Abner Uribe and his 2.23 ERA did the same. Every high-leverage arm in that pen is rested, and the team ERA behind them is 3.48.

Now put Bubba Chandler on the other mound. The 23-year-old right-hander throws a 98.5 mph four-seam on 47.5 percent of his pitches and backs it with a 90.4 mph slider, which is genuinely exciting stuff and also the reason he is 3-8 with a 4.56 ERA. He has walked 57 batters in 104.2 innings, a rate of 4.90 per nine. Milwaukee draws walks better than it hits for power, 460 free passes against 101 home runs, and a lineup that grinds counts is the exact wrong opponent for a starter whose command comes and goes. Chandler proved the range in eight days: six and a third shutout innings against the Yankees on July 22, then four innings and seven hits against Arizona on July 28.

Sproat is not the reason to bet this. He is 3-6 with a 5.05 ERA and he has surrendered 17 home runs in 92.2 innings, nine of them at home. He is a sinker-and-cutter pitcher, 25.2 percent sinkers at 94.3 and 22.4 percent cutters at 90.7, which means contact is the plan and the plan is loud in a 344-foot-to-left, 345-foot-to-right ballpark under a retractable roof. He also gave Milwaukee six innings of three-run ball against San Francisco on July 27, which is all this bet needs.

A unit and a half, not more. You are paying -139 for a 36-20 home team with a fully loaded bullpen against a club that just got beaten 10-2 in Cincinnati and has lost seven of ten. The starter is the weakest link in the argument, and the argument still holds.

Yankees -192: One Man Against The Weakest Lineup Here

Schlittler is the best pitcher on this card by a distance that is almost uncomfortable to write down. He is 10-6 with a 2.04 ERA, a 0.91 WHIP and a .196 opponent average across 136.2 innings, with 165 strikeouts against 26 walks. That is a 6.35 strikeout-to-walk ratio, and the walks are what separate him from every other arm on this card. Bubba Chandler has issued more than twice as many free passes in thirty fewer innings.

His arsenal explains the whiffs. Four-seam 44.5 percent at 97.9 mph, cutter 26.5 percent at 94.6, sinker 19.4 percent at 97.7. Roughly nine out of every ten pitches he throws arrive at 94 or better, with a rare 85.7 mph curve mixed in to steal a called strike. On July 24 he took that mix into a start against Philadelphia and produced 7.1 innings, zero earned runs, twelve strikeouts and no walks on three hits. On July 6 he gave Tampa Bay eight innings and one run with eight strikeouts and no walks. His last time out, July 29 against the White Sox, was six and a third with one run and eight punchouts.

St. Louis brings the softest offense of the four teams here. The Cardinals are hitting .239 with a .689 OPS and have scored 483 runs in 112 games, a full ninety-five behind Pittsburgh. Their record is 55-57 and their run differential is negative sixteen.

Michael McGreevy has been better than his 4-9 record suggests, with a 3.57 ERA, a 1.20 WHIP and only 29 walks in 116 innings. The trouble is how he gets outs. His four-seam averages 91.7 mph and accounts for a quarter of his pitches; the rest is an 86.8 mph changeup, a 90.6 sinker, a 78.2 curve and a pair of breaking balls in the low eighties. He is a soft-contact pitcher walking into a stadium with a 314-foot right field line, against a club that has hit 157 home runs, the most of any team on this card. He has already allowed 15 homers this year, and his last outing was a wreck: five outs, eight hits, seven earned runs against the Cubs on July 28.

New York's offense is thin and honest about it. The Yankees hit .232 as a team and have scored 512 runs, because Aaron Judge has been on the injured list since June 5 with a stress fracture in his first right rib and no timetable to return, with Cody Bellinger and Giancarlo Stanton also sidelined. They are 63-49 anyway, and the reason is a 3.28 team ERA that ranks better than anything Milwaukee, Pittsburgh or St. Louis has managed. This is a pitching team dressed as a slugging team, and tonight it starts its best pitcher against the weakest lineup on the board.

Two units. This is the bigger stake for a reason.

What Can Beat It

Start with Milwaukee, because the case against it is loud. Pittsburgh is 5-1 against the Brewers in 2026 and leads all four of these teams with 139 home runs, which is a dangerous profile against a home-run-prone starter in a park that plays fair to both alleys. Sproat's 5.98 home ERA is not a small sample quirk anymore at eight starts. Milwaukee also flew back from a Sunday afternoon in Anaheim, got shut out 3-0 in that finale, and now opens a home series on short travel. If Chandler's fastball command shows up the way it did against the Yankees on July 22, the Brewers can be flat and beaten again.

Now the Yankees, and the counterpoint is sharper than the price suggests. New York is 26-23 at Yankee Stadium and 37-26 on the road, which is the opposite of the usual shape, and Schlittler's own home and road splits point the same direction. Laying -192 with an offense missing Judge, Bellinger and Stanton means one swing from the Cardinals can flip an entire evening. The Yankee bullpen also worked all three days at Wrigley, with David Bednar pitching Friday and Sunday, Paul Blackburn pitching Friday and Sunday, and Brent Headrick pitching Saturday and Sunday. If Schlittler exits in the fifth, that relief corps is running on fumes. And St. Louis just took the finale in Toronto 5-1, travels better than it plays at home at 27-25 on the road, and has never seen Schlittler: these clubs have not met once in 2026.

Two moneylines means two ways to lose. The bet is that a rested pen and a 69-42 record cover for one shaky starter in Milwaukee, and that one great starter covers for a banged-up lineup in the Bronx.

The Bottom Line

Yankees -192 takes two units because the gap between Schlittler and McGreevy is the widest talent gap on either side of this card, and because St. Louis scores less than anyone here. Brewers -139 takes a unit and a half, sized down deliberately, since the head-to-head history and Sproat's home numbers are real problems that the price has partially but not fully accounted for. Three and a half units, two moneylines, no derivatives and no parlays.
