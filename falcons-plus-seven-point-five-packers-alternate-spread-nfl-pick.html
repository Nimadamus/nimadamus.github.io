Atlanta goes to Green Bay with an 0-2 record and a new quarterback in the starting lineup. The pick is the Falcons +7.5 at -186. This alternate spread gives Atlanta room to lose by seven and still cash; a Green Bay win by eight or more is the losing result. The extra cushion matters, but the price asks the ticket to win often enough to pay for it. No stake size came with the pick, so this article gives the side and price without assigning units.

Why the number makes sense for this matchup

The Falcons have opened the year with two losses: 20-13 at Pittsburgh, then 34-3 at home against Carolina. Those results are the strongest reason to hesitate. Atlanta scored only 16 points across the first two games, and the 31-point loss to Carolina exposed how little margin the offense had with its top quarterback options unavailable.

That context changes tonight. Michael Penix Jr. is set to make his 2026 debut after missing the opening games while recovering from the knee injury that ended his 2025 season. He practiced fully in the run-up to this game and was named the starter. That is a meaningful upgrade in potential passing ability, but it is not evidence that the offense will immediately click: Penix is returning to live action, and the Falcons have had almost no time to prepare on a short week.

Green Bay is 1-1. The Packers came back to beat the Jets 20-17 in overtime after an uneven offensive showing. Jordan Love finished 16 of 29 for 145 yards with two touchdowns, while the Packers converted only one of nine third downs. That performance gives Atlanta a path to keep the game close if its defense can disrupt timing and force long-yardage situations. It does not erase Green Bay's home-field advantage or the Packers' ability to finish late drives.

What Atlanta needs to do

First, the Falcons need to avoid putting Penix in repeated obvious passing situations. Their ground game was one of the few functional pieces in Pittsburgh, where Atlanta gained 120 rushing yards; the Week 2 blowout was a much less useful test of the offense. Sustaining drives with Bijan Robinson and the run game would shorten the contest, keep the defense off the field, and reduce the pressure on a quarterback making his first appearance of the season.

Second, the defense has to make Green Bay earn third downs. The Packers' one-for-nine third-down result against the Jets is a concrete weakness to test, though a two-game sample is small and an overtime game can distort the raw line. Atlanta's chances improve if it can create third-and-long, keep Love from extending drives, and avoid giving away short fields.

Finally, the Falcons must keep the game in a manageable script. The +7.5 cushion can absorb a one-score loss, but it offers no protection against another offensive collapse like the Carolina game. Turnovers and special-teams mistakes would make a road cover much harder.
