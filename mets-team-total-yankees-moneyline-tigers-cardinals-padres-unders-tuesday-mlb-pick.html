Mets Team Total Under: Sale, Not Pérez

This is the flagship at three units. New York just hung 14 runs on Atlanta in Monday's series opener, Francisco Lindor driving in six by himself, so an under on the Mets' own total needs an explanation, and it starts with who was on the mound. Monday's outburst came against Martín Pérez, a serviceable back-end arm at 6-6 with a 3.38 ERA. Tuesday, New York faces Chris Sale, who is 11-6 with a 2.19 ERA and 134 strikeouts in his 20th start, a completely different class of pitcher and the best arm the Mets will see all series.

Sale's last two outings show exactly why this matters: seven shutout innings on 89 pitches against Texas on July 17, and a season-high-tying 11 strikeouts across six innings against San Diego on July 23. New York is also still without Juan Soto (calf strain, 10-day IL) and Mark Vientos (hand, 10-day IL), the same shorthanded lineup that beat up Pérez Monday but now has to do it against a pitcher averaging better than a strikeout an inning. One good night against a fourth starter does not change what Sale has done to lineups all year. Three units on New York staying under 3.5 at -155.

Yankees Moneyline: Momentum Into Rate Field

New York has won six of its last eight and sits just 2.5 games back of a idle Tampa Bay in the AL East, and it opened this series the way a team in that position should, a 9-5 win Monday behind Ali Sánchez's first career home run, a three-run shot. Gerrit Cole gets the ball Tuesday at 3-5 with a 3.92 ERA, modest numbers for him but still enough against a Chicago club that is 55-50 and leads the AL Central more on the weakness of its division than its own play, dropping its third loss in four games Monday.

Anthony Kay (7-4, 4.21 ERA) starts for the White Sox behind a bullpen fronted by Seranthony Domínguez (12 saves) and Bryan Hudson (2.86 ERA), a relief corps capable of shortening the game if Kay gives Chicago a lead. New York is dealing with real attrition of its own, Aaron Judge on the 60-day IL with a rib stress fracture, Carlos Rodón and Clarke Schmidt both out, Giancarlo Stanton and Cody Bellinger each on 10-day leg-injury stints, and this line reflects that: the board itself has been priced closer to -135 on this side, making this number a fair entry point on a team that has been the better club over the last two weeks regardless of who is missing from the lineup. Two units on New York.

Tigers Team Total Under: The Melton Effect

Detroit is 44-62 and has nothing to play for in the standings, but Troy Melton has quietly turned into the best reason to watch this team. The rookie right-hander opened the year on the injured list with elbow inflammation, debuted May 24, and has been dominant since: 5-1 with a 1.95 ERA and 55 strikeouts, with Detroit going 26-20 in games he has started or the team has played since his debut, third best in the American League over that stretch. Baltimore, at 52-55, has not been a consistent offensive team this season, and the moneyline reflects the gap between the arms, Detroit -144 at home.

This is a small stake for a reason. Team totals on a home underdog carry more variance than the moneyline suggests, and Baltimore's lineup has had spurts of production against pitching worse than Melton's. But betting against a 1.95 ERA in a 4.5-run team total is still sound process, even in a game between two non-contenders. Half a unit at -102.

Cardinals Team Total Under: An Offense Stuck At 3.5

The Cubs' Colin Rea is not a great pitcher on paper, 7-7 with a 4.85 ERA, and coming off a start last Thursday against Detroit in which he allowed four earned runs in five innings. Normally that argues for the other side of a team total. What makes St. Louis the play anyway is the Cardinals' own bat: over their last 10 games, St. Louis is averaging just 3.5 runs, and when the Cardinals score fewer than four runs in a game this season, they are 14-37. That is not a park-factor or a matchup quirk, that is a lineup that has gone cold at the exact moment it needs to be pushing for a playoff spot at 53-53, fourth in the NL Central.

Michael McGreevy is on the mound for St. Louis at 3.07 ERA in his 21st start, so this is not about the Cardinals needing to hold Chicago down, it is purely a bet on St. Louis's own bats staying cold against an average-at-best Cubs staff. A half unit at -145 is a short price, but the recent run of offense supports it.

Padres Team Total Under: A .175 Team Average

San Diego is favored at -190 on the moneyline against a last-place Rockies club and Michael Lorenzen, who is 3-9 with a 6.53 ERA, numbers that would normally point toward the Padres' own total going over, not under. The case here is entirely about what San Diego's lineup has done lately regardless of opponent: the Padres are hitting .175 as a team and averaging 2.5 runs per game over their last 10, a stretch that includes three straight losses while scoring three runs in six of their last eight games. San Diego's own pitching staff has actually been excellent in that same window, a 2.85 ERA over the last 10 games, and the record is still just 4-6 because the lineup cannot produce.

Michael King starts for the Padres at 3.24 ERA, coming off a start against Atlanta where he allowed one run in the first inning and shut the Braves down over his final six, so San Diego does not need a big number to win this game. The team total under is a bet that a cold lineup does not suddenly find four runs against even a well below-average arm, and the last two weeks of games say that is the smart side. One and a half units at -110.

What Can Beat It

The Mets team total is the riskiest of the group precisely because New York just proved it can score in bunches; Lindor is swinging a hot bat, and a short bench day for Sale or one mistake pitch can turn 14 runs on Monday into a big first inning Tuesday. The Yankees moneyline is exposed to how thin the lineup has gotten, Judge, Stanton and Bellinger all out is a lot of power to lose, and Cole's 3.92 ERA is not commanding enough to guarantee a low-scoring path to a win if the offense goes quiet. The Tigers team total is the one small mistake from breaking, since Baltimore's lineup has had scoring bursts against tougher arms than Melton this year. The Cardinals and Padres team totals both carry the same risk: cold streaks end, and if either lineup breaks out of its slump on the wrong night, the number goes over quickly, especially against pitchers (Rea at 4.85 ERA, Lorenzen at 6.53) who are genuinely hittable if the bats wake up.

The Bottom Line

The Mets team total under 3.5 at -155 is three units because Sale's 2.19 ERA is a different problem than the pitcher New York just torched. The Yankees moneyline at -120 backs a club that has won six of eight and is pushing for the AL East lead despite a beat-up lineup. The three smaller plays, Tigers team total under 4.5 (-102), Cardinals team total under 4.5 (-145) and Padres team total under 4.5 (-110), all lean on the same idea: a dominant or improving arm on one side, or a lineup that has gone cold regardless of opponent on the other. Seven and a half units across five games on Tuesday.
