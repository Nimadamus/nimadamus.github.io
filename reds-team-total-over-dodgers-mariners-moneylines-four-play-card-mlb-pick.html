Four Plays Built On Who Gives Away Bases

August 4, 2026|8 min read|BetLegend

J.T. Ginn has not thrown a major league pitch since July 18. That night he gave up one hit across six and a third innings in Washington, the sharpest outing of his season, and then he disappeared for seventeen days. Tonight he reappears at Great American Ball Park carrying 49 walks in 109.1 innings, in a building where the Cincinnati Reds have hit 79 of their 145 home runs.

That is where this whole card starts.

Four bets, three ballparks, and one question underneath every one of them: which starter gives away bases and which one refuses to. Ginn hands out 4.03 free passes per nine innings. Tarik Skubal has walked fourteen men all year. Emerson Hancock has walked twenty seven in 110.1 innings and gets to do it inside the best pitcher's park on the board. Nothing here is about who has the prettier record.

Reds Over 4.5: The Bandbox And The Walk Rate

Get the argument against this on the table first, because it is a good one. Cincinnati scores 4.16 runs per game, which ranks 24th in baseball. At home the number is 4.05 across 57 games. This bet asks a bottom-third offense to clear four and a half runs against a starter with a 3.46 ERA who is holding hitters to a .211 average. On paper it looks like a reach.

The paper is measuring the wrong thing. Ginn's ERA is built on missing bats and limiting hits, and neither of those has ever been the way Great American Ball Park beats a pitcher. This park beats pitchers by turning a walk into a three-run inning, and Ginn walks people. Forty nine of them in 109.1 innings. He issued three in six and a third against Washington in his last start and three more in four and a third against the White Sox on July 12, an outing that cost him eight earned runs. The stuff is real and the command comes and goes, and it is coming and going tonight in a yard where the Reds have hit 79 homers in 57 dates and only 66 in 54 road games.

Then there is the seventeen-day gap. Ginn's last appearance was July 18. Whatever the reason, a starter reappearing after that long is not usually the version who finishes seven innings on eighty five pitches, which means Oakland's bullpen is in this game early. That bullpen belongs to a staff that has surrendered 641 runs, the most in baseball. On August 2, buried 11-0 by Detroit, the Athletics ran five relievers out there and finished the night with outfielder Carlos Cortes pitching a full inning. A team that has to do that in early August is not holding anyone to four runs in Cincinnati.

The Reds lineup is also better than its rank. Elly De La Cruz is hitting .276 with 18 home runs and an .855 OPS. Sal Stewart has 24 homers and 85 RBI. JJ Bleday is batting only .227 and still carries an .805 OPS because he walks and slugs. Cincinnati hung ten runs on Pittsburgh on August 2, took Monday off, and comes back rested against a club that has lost five in a row and is 2-8 in its last ten.

Two units. Not three, because the season-long scoring rank is a genuine drag and the number is 4.5 rather than 3.5.

Reds -129: Oakland Is A Different Team On The Road

Cincinnati is 53-58 and 26-31 inside its own ballpark. Laying anything on a team with that home record deserves a raised eyebrow, and this play gets a unit and a half instead of three for exactly that reason.

What makes it work is the split on the other side. The Athletics at home are a genuinely dangerous offense: 5.00 runs per game, a .264 average, a .790 OPS and 83 home runs in 57 games. On the road they collapse into something unrecognizable. 3.67 runs per game, a .225 average, a .650 OPS and 51 home runs in 55 games. That is a 140-point OPS swing depending on which stadium they walk into, and tonight they walk into somebody else's.

Some of that gap is roster attrition. Oakland is currently without designated hitter Brent Rooker, first baseman Nick Kurtz, catcher Shea Langeliers and third baseman Zack Gelof, four of the bats that made this a top-half offense in the spring. The club is also 24-31 away from home, has lost five straight, and just finished getting outscored 19-6 across two games against Detroit. This is the first regular-season meeting between these clubs in 2026, so there is no head-to-head history for the market to lean on, only two profiles heading in opposite directions.

Brady Singer is the weak link and it would be dishonest to dress him up. He is 5-10 with a 4.68 ERA and a 1.43 WHIP, and he has allowed 21 home runs in 107.2 innings, more than any other starter on this card by a distance. His last start was four and a third innings, seven hits, four walks and four earned runs against Cleveland on July 29. He also threw seven innings of two-run ball at Colorado on July 17 and six and two-thirds against Seattle on July 22, so the range is wide.

Cincinnati's bullpen is the quiet reason to accept him. Reds relievers threw four innings on August 1 and three on August 2, then had all of Monday off. Zach McCambley worked both weekend days and is rested. If Singer gives five, the pen is fully stocked to cover the rest against a road lineup slugging .650.

Dodgers -184: Fourteen Walks In Ninety-Six Innings

Skubal has walked fourteen batters this season. Fourteen. In 96.2 innings, against 116 strikeouts, with a 2.79 ERA, a 0.91 WHIP and a .210 opponent average. That is 1.30 walks per nine, and it is the single cleanest number on the entire board tonight.

His last three starts read like a metronome: seven shutout innings with nine strikeouts and zero walks against the Angels on July 18, seven and a third with twelve strikeouts and two walks against Kansas City on July 24, and six and two-thirds with six strikeouts and one walk against Baltimore on July 29. Three starts, three walks total, and every one of them at least six and two-thirds innings deep.

Depth is the entire point here, because the Dodgers need it. Los Angeles relievers have thrown roughly eleven innings across the last three days. Alex Vesia covered the ninth on August 1. Four arms combined for six and a third on August 2 after Emmet Sheehan got eight outs, with Kyle Hurt and Wyatt Mills going two apiece. Then Justin Wrobleski lasted four and a third on Monday at Wrigley and three more relievers finished, Jonathan Hernandez working two of those innings, in a 10-5 loss. Skubal is the arm that stops the bleeding, and he is built to.

Javier Assad is the opposite kind of starter. His 3.75 ERA is respectable, and his usage tells the real story: one inning on July 20, three innings on July 25, four and a third on July 30. He has not finished five innings in three tries. He also strikes out only 6.20 per nine and has given up twelve home runs in 69.2 innings, which is a difficult profile against a Dodgers club hitting .273 with an .800 OPS and scoring 5.77 runs per game on the road. Los Angeles is 36-21 away from home, the best road record in baseball.

The Cubs bullpen is not fresh either, and it is not whole. Chicago has Daniel Palencia, Phil Maton, Hoby Milner, Ethan Roberts and Gavin Hollowell all on the injured list at the same time, which is most of a functioning relief corps. What is left used three arms Saturday, two Sunday and two more Monday, with Trent Thornton pitching twice in three days. If Assad exits in the fifth again, the middle innings belong to a group that has already worked eight innings this week.

Three units, the largest stake on the card, because the pitching gap is the largest gap on the card.

Mariners -118: The Quietest Bet Here

Seattle is 55-58 and gets laid at -118. That is the market saying this is close to a coin flip with a small home tax, and the market is being reasonable. This play takes one unit and no more.

The reason to take it at all is the most extreme home and road split of any club on this card. The Mariners at T-Mobile Park own a 3.21 staff ERA with a 1.07 WHIP and a 32-24 record. On the road those numbers become a 4.32 ERA, a 1.31 WHIP and a 23-34 record. Same pitchers, same season, completely different results depending on the marine layer. Tonight they are home.

Hancock fits the park like it was built for him. He carries a 3.26 ERA, a 1.04 WHIP and 27 walks in 110.1 innings, and opponents are hitting .217 against him. He gave Cincinnati six innings of two-run ball on July 22 and held the Dodgers to three earned over five on July 29, walking one man in each of those starts.

Now the counterpoint, and it is a serious one. Detroit sends Troy Melton, who has a 1.75 ERA, a 0.93 WHIP and a .174 opponent average across 11 starts. The Tigers have won three straight and their staff ERA of 3.59 is better than Seattle's. Betting against that requires a reason.

Here is the reason. Melton is eleven starts and 67 innings into his first extended run, and the walks have crept in: four in five and two-thirds against the Angels on July 17, four more in five against Kansas City on July 23, and two in seven scoreless against Baltimore on July 28. He has never gone past seven innings. That puts a Tigers bullpen that already covered five innings across the weekend into a late game on the West Coast, and it puts Seattle's own relievers, who threw four innings all weekend with Monday off, in a better spot. Andres Munoz pitched Saturday and Sunday and has had two days since.

The honest version of this bet: Seattle ranks 28th in baseball at 4.02 runs per game and scores 4.04 at home. Nobody should expect a comfortable evening. One unit is the correct expression of a small edge.

What Can Beat This Card

Start in Cincinnati, because that is where the exposure is heaviest. Three and a half units sit on one game across two correlated markets, and if Ginn is the July 18 version who allowed one hit in six and a third, both bets die together. Singer's 21 home runs allowed are a live problem in his own ballpark, the Reds are 26-31 at home, and Oakland's offense is a legitimate 5.00 runs per game somewhere in the league even if that somewhere is not here.

At Wrigley the risk is priced but not eliminated. Chicago scores 5.13 runs per game, second most in baseball, and 5.11 of them at home. Pete Crow-Armstrong is hitting .282 with 24 homers and a .919 OPS, Seiya Suzuki carries an .846 mark, and the Cubs are 32-24 in that ballpark. The Dodgers have lost four in a row and got beaten 10-5 in this very series on Monday. Laying -184 on a team on a four-game skid, with a bullpen that has worked eleven innings in three days, means Skubal has to be excellent rather than merely good.

Seattle is the smallest stake and still the coldest read. Melton's 1.75 ERA is not a mirage across 67 innings, the Mariners are 4-6 in their last ten, and an offense ranked 28th can lose a 2-1 game to a good young arm without anything going wrong tactically. That is why it is one unit.

The Bottom Line

Dodgers -184 takes three units because Skubal's fourteen walks against Assad's three straight short outings is the widest gap on the board, and because Los Angeles hits better on the road than at home. Reds over 4.5 takes two units on the park and the free passes rather than on Cincinnati's season line. Reds -129 takes a unit and a half on Oakland's 3.67 runs per game away from the Coliseum. Mariners -118 takes one unit on a 3.21 home staff ERA and a rookie starter who has not been past seven innings. Seven and a half units, four bets, no parlays and no derivatives beyond the one team total.
